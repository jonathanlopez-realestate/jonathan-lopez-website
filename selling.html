Selling Your Home in Woodland Hills

Jonathan Lopez, Woodland Hills Realtor | Jim Sandoval Group | CA DRE #02282844

Selling a home in Woodland Hills comes down to one thing more than any other: whether the price on day one actually matches what buyers are proving comparable homes are worth. Here's what's really happening in the market right now, and how Jonathan approaches getting a home sold at the right price without the guesswork.

What's Actually Happening in the Market

  • 78 of 426 active Woodland Hills listings (about 18.3%) currently carry a price reduction. That's roughly 1 in 5 sellers who started at one number and had to walk it back.
  • Homes are selling in about 51 days on average, essentially flat compared to 52 days a year ago.
  • The area's sale-to-list ratio is 98.76%, meaning correctly priced homes are selling close to full asking price, not at a discount.
  • Months of supply sits at 1.21, consistent with a balanced market, not one tilted heavily toward buyers or sellers.

The lesson in those numbers: the price cuts aren't a sign of a soft market. They're almost always a sign of a listing priced without a real comparative market analysis behind the number.

Why Your Zestimate Isn't the Answer

Zillow's own published data puts the median error at about 7.06-7.49% for homes that are off market, which is the situation for almost every homeowner just checking their value. On a Woodland Hills home at the median price, that works out to roughly $90,000-$97,000 in either direction. At the higher end of the market, where Woodland Hills homes run up toward $7M, that same style of error can mean $300,000-$600,000 off, because a modest percentage swing becomes a large dollar figure at that price point.

A real comparative market analysis, built from actual recent, comparable sales rather than public records and an algorithm, typically lands within 2-4% of the eventual sale price.
