Woodland Hills First-Time Buyer Guide

Jonathan Lopez, Woodland Hills Realtor | Jim Sandoval Group | CA DRE #02282844

Buying your first home in Woodland Hills involves more steps than most people expect, and most of the stress comes from not knowing what's supposed to happen next. Here's the real process Jonathan walks first-time buyers through, from the first conversation to the day you get your keys.

Step 1: A Real Conversation Before You Ever Tour a Home

Before scheduling a single showing, Jonathan sits down with you to actually understand your situation: why you're buying now, your realistic timeline, what your budget can support, and what you genuinely need versus what would just be nice to have. This isn't a formality. It's the difference between spending weekends touring homes that were never going to work and walking into a search that's already narrowed to what actually fits your life and your finances. Buyers who skip this step usually end up touring the most, and buying the slowest.

Step 2: Your Written Buyer Plan of Action

Before you commit to working together, Jonathan gives you an actual written Plan of Action, not just a verbal promise. It lays out how your search will really be run: how new and off-market opportunities that fit your criteria get surfaced, how you're connected with vetted lenders, inspectors, and other people you'll need along the way, how you're kept updated as the market moves, and how negotiation and the path to closing get handled once you're under contract. It's the same idea as a seller's Plan of Action: proof of process, not a pitch, so you know what's actually going to happen before you ever sign anything.

Step 3: Getting Pre-Approved, Not Just Pre-Qualified

Pre-qualified means you told a lender some numbers and they gave you a rough idea. Pre-approved means a lender has actually verified your income, credit, and assets and issued a real conditional commitment. Sellers and their agents can tell the difference immediately, and in a competitive situation, a pre-approved offer gets taken seriously in a way a pre-qualified one doesn't. Jonathan connects you with lenders before the search starts, not after you've already fallen for a house.

Step 4: Signing a Buyer Representation Agreement

Since the 2024 National Association of Realtors settlement, buyers are required to sign a written agreement before an agent can start showing them homes. It's not paperwork for paperwork's sake. It spells out exactly how Jonathan represents you throughout the transaction and how he's compensated, so there's no ambiguity for either side before you ever step into a house together.

Step 5: Touring Homes With a Real Strategy

Every home you tour gets measured against your actual must-haves and against what comparable homes nearby have actually sold for, not just how the home shows on the day you walk through it. Staging and fresh paint can make a home feel like the right one when the numbers say otherwise. Jonathan's job at this stage is making sure you're falling for the right house for the right reasons.

Step 6: Writing a Competitive Offer

When you find the right home, the offer gets built around real comps and strategy, not a number picked out of excitement or fear of losing it. That includes the price, the earnest money deposit, and which contingencies to include or waive based on your actual risk tolerance. In a competitive situation, how an offer is structured often matters as much as the price itself.

Step 7: Inspection and Appraisal

Once an offer is accepted, a professional inspection and the lender's appraisal happen before anything is final. If the inspection turns up real issues or the appraisal comes in under the agreed price, there's room to renegotiate, request repairs, or adjust terms before you're locked in.

Step 8: Closing Costs and the Final Walkthrough

A few days before closing, you'll walk through the home one more time to confirm it's in the condition you agreed to. Alongside that, you'll get a clear breakdown of closing costs, the fees and prepaid items beyond your down payment, so nothing at the closing table is a surprise.

Step 9: Closing Day

Final paperwork gets signed, ownership transfers, and you get the keys. Jonathan stays involved through this last step to make sure nothing slips between the final walkthrough and the moment the home is actually yours.

First-Time Buyer Assistance Programs in California

California offers first-time buyer assistance programs, including down payment and closing cost help through CalHFA, that can meaningfully change what's realistic for you. Program funding, eligibility rules, and availability change often enough that a specific dollar figure here would likely be outdated by the time you read it, so the right move is confirming current program status directly before assuming what's available. Jonathan can point you toward what's currently active when you're ready to look into it.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much down payment do I actually need to buy a home in Woodland Hills?

It depends on your loan program. Conventional loans can go as low as 3 to 5% down for qualified first-time buyers, FHA loans as low as 3.5%. A lender needs your specific financial picture to give you the real number.

Do I have to sign an agreement to work with a buyer's agent now?

Yes. Since the 2024 NAR settlement, buyers sign a written buyer representation agreement before an agent can show them homes. It spells out how you're represented and how the agent is paid.

What's the difference between pre-qualified and pre-approved?

Pre-qualified is a quick, informal estimate. Pre-approved means a lender has verified your income, credit, and assets and issued a conditional commitment. Sellers take pre-approved offers far more seriously.

What is earnest money?

A deposit, typically 1 to 3% of the purchase price, submitted with an offer to show you're serious. It's held in escrow and applied toward your down payment and closing costs, not an extra cost on top.

Does Jonathan give buyers a written plan before working together?

Yes. Before you commit to anything, Jonathan provides a written Buyer Plan of Action outlining how your search will be run, how you'll be kept updated, and how negotiation and closing are handled, so you know what to expect up front.
