Can I Use a Woodland Hills ADU's Future Rent to Help Qualify for My First Mortgage?

Jonathan Lopez, Woodland Hills Realtor · Jim Sandoval Group · CA DRE #02282844 · Updated November 2026

Woodland Hills has a lot of lots big enough for an accessory dwelling unit, and plenty of homes already come with one built. First-time buyers usually treat that ADU as a nice-to-have, extra space for a home office or a bit of rental income down the road. What many don't realize is that the rent from an existing, legal ADU can actually help them qualify for the purchase loan itself, not just pay them back after closing.

What changed. Fannie Mae updated its Selling Guide to let lenders count rental income from an ADU toward a borrower's qualifying income on a one-unit principal residence, for a purchase or a limited cash-out refinance. The policy went live for manual underwriting on October 8, 2025, and became available through Fannie Mae's automated Desktop Underwriter system on March 21, 2026. Freddie Mac runs a parallel program, refined through 2025, with its own rules for one-unit properties. Both programs exist specifically to help buyers who are purchasing a home they'll actually live in, not investors buying a rental property outright.

The income cap. Both agencies cap ADU rental income at 30% of a borrower's total qualifying income. If your other income (salary, bonus, whatever else a lender counts) adds up to $8,000 a month, ADU rent can add at most $2,400 to that figure for qualifying purposes, even if the unit actually rents for more. On top of that cap, Freddie Mac applies a 75% factor to the lease amount or appraised market rent before counting it, a standard vacancy-and-expense haircut also used for other rental income types.

Documentation requirements differ by agency. Fannie Mae wants either a signed lease or Form 1007, the Single-Family Comparable Rent Schedule, showing fair market rent, plus a full appraisal that specifically identifies the ADU and confirms it's legally permitted. Freddie Mac asks for more on the appraisal side: a full appraisal (no automated waiver allowed) that includes at least one comparable sale with an ADU and three comparable rentals, at least one of which also has an ADU, so the appraiser can show the ADU actually commands rent in that specific market.

The landlord-experience split. This is where the two programs genuinely diverge. Freddie Mac requires the borrower to complete a landlord-education course before closing, unless they can document 12 or more months of prior landlord experience. Fannie Mae doesn't require that education at all, but it adds a different guardrail for first-time landlords instead: if you have less than 12 months of documented landlord experience, the ADU rent counted toward your income can't exceed the subject property's own PITIA payment (principal, interest, taxes, insurance, and any association dues), even if the appraised market rent is higher.

What neither program allows. Rent from an illegal or unpermitted ADU doesn't count under either agency's rules. The unit has to be legally permitted, and the appraiser has to be able to identify and value it as a legitimate accessory dwelling, not an informally converted garage or an undocumented addition. Given how much attention permitting gets in older Woodland Hills neighborhoods, this is worth confirming early, before assuming a listing's "ADU" will actually support the income a seller or agent is advertising.

Why this matters for a first-time Woodland Hills buyer specifically. At a $1,300,000 citywide median, a lot of first-time buyers feel priced out of anything with real extra space. A legally permitted ADU that can contribute real, countable income toward qualifying changes that math, potentially opening up homes that would otherwise sit just outside a buyer's approved range. It's not free money toward the loan, the 30% cap and the haircut mean it's a meaningful boost, not a way to qualify for a home far outside what your base income would otherwise support.

The honest limit here. These are the agencies' own minimum guidelines, not a guarantee every lender applies them the same way. Individual lenders can layer additional requirements (a higher reserve requirement, a stricter cap, extra documentation) on top of what Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac require. A buyer seriously considering a home with an ADU for this reason should confirm the exact numbers with their own loan officer before assuming the rent will count in full.

On today's broader Woodland Hills market (Movoto, citywide: $1,300,000 median sale price, 368 active listings, 54-day average days on market), knowing which homes actually have a legally permitted, income-eligible ADU is its own kind of due diligence, separate from the usual inspection and appraisal process.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use an ADU's rent to help qualify for my first mortgage in Woodland Hills?

Yes, if the ADU is legally permitted and the loan is a conventional purchase or limited cash-out refinance on a one-unit principal residence. Both Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac allow it, each capped at 30% of your total qualifying income.

Does the full rent amount count?

No. Both programs apply limits. Freddie Mac uses 75% of the lease or appraised market rent. Fannie Mae caps a first-time landlord's counted ADU rent at the property's own monthly PITIA payment. Either way, you won't get credit for 100% of the advertised rent.

Do I need landlord experience or training first?

It depends on the agency. Freddie Mac requires a landlord-education course before closing unless you already have 12-plus months of documented landlord experience. Fannie Mae doesn't require the course, but limits how much rent a first-time landlord can count instead.

What if the ADU isn't permitted?

Then the rent doesn't count under either program. The ADU has to be legally permitted and appraised as a legitimate accessory dwelling unit, not an informal conversion.

Is this the same as the ADU post you've written about before?

No. That earlier post covered whether building an ADU raises a home's resale value. This post covers a completely different question: using an existing ADU's rent to help qualify for the purchase loan itself.

How do I find out if a specific Woodland Hills listing's ADU actually qualifies?

Your lender's appraiser has to identify and confirm the ADU is legally permitted as part of the loan file. Ask your agent for the permit history on any listing before counting on the ADU rent in your financing plan.
