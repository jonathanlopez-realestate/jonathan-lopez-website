Can I Assume the Seller's Low-Rate Mortgage When I Buy in Woodland Hills?

Jonathan Lopez, Woodland Hills Realtor · Jim Sandoval Group · CA DRE #02282844 · Updated November 2026

Mortgage rates sitting in the 6.5% to 7.5% range have made a specific question more common among Woodland Hills buyers: can I just take over the seller's existing loan instead of getting a brand new one at today's rate? The short answer is sometimes, but only under specific conditions, and it's worth understanding both the real upside and the real friction before chasing a listing specifically because it might have an assumable loan.

Assumability depends entirely on the type of loan the seller has, not on the property itself. Conventional loans, the kind most sellers have, almost always include a due-on-sale clause that requires the full balance to be paid off at sale, which makes them non-assumable in practice. Government-backed loans work differently. All FHA loans are assumable with the lender's approval; the buyer has to qualify under FHA's current guidelines, including a minimum credit score, before the lender signs off. VA loans are assumable too, and notably the buyer does not have to be a veteran to assume one. The catch for a seller is that unless the buyer is a veteran who substitutes their own VA entitlement for the seller's, the seller's entitlement stays tied up in that loan even after they've sold and moved on, which limits their ability to use a full VA entitlement again right away on a future purchase. USDA loans are also assumable with lender and USDA approval, though they come with their own income and property eligibility rules that rarely apply inside Woodland Hills' price range.

Assuming a loan doesn't mean skipping underwriting. The buyer still has to go through a creditworthiness review with the existing loan's servicer, covering credit score, income, and debt-to-income ratio, essentially the same documentation a new loan application would require. What the buyer keeps is the seller's interest rate and remaining loan term, not a fresh set of qualifying standards.

The real obstacle for most Woodland Hills buyers isn't qualifying, it's the equity gap. If a seller bought or refinanced in 2020 to 2022 when rates sat near 2.5% to 3.5%, their current loan balance is likely well below today's sale price, especially after a few years of appreciation. That difference has to be covered somehow: in cash at closing, through a second mortgage at today's market rate to bridge the gap, effectively blending a low first-position rate with a smaller, higher-rate second, or occasionally through seller financing on part of the gap. None of those options are automatic, and a buyer needs to run the real math, comparing a blended first-plus-second payment against simply financing the whole purchase at today's rate, before assuming the assumption is actually the better deal.

Timing is the other real friction point. Processing an assumption means working through the existing loan servicer, not a standard mortgage lender shopping for your business, and that process commonly takes 45 to 120 days from application to close, well beyond the 30 to 45 days a typical financed purchase takes. VA assumptions specifically are governed by VA Circular 26-23-27, which requires servicers with automatic authority to decide on a complete assumption package within 45 days, though in practice many assumptions still run longer. That timeline has to be built into any purchase contract and disclosed clearly to a seller who's expecting a normal closing date, since showing up with an assumption request on a tight timeline can create real friction in the deal itself.

Locally, 91367's current numbers (Movoto, search-snapshot accessed November 12, 2026, direct page fetch returned a 403 error this session) show a $1,199,000 median list price, 198 active listings, and homes averaging 41 days on market, up from 33 a year ago. In a market with rising inventory and more time on market, a seller sitting on a well-below-market-rate assumable loan may have more room to negotiate on price than one without, since the loan itself becomes part of what makes the listing attractive.

None of this shows up on a standard MLS search by default, since listings don't reliably flag an assumable loan as a searchable field. Finding one usually means asking directly about a seller's loan type before writing an offer, not filtering for it online.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which loans can actually be assumed?

FHA and VA loans are assumable with lender approval, and USDA loans are assumable with lender and USDA approval. Conventional loans almost never qualify because of due-on-sale clauses requiring full payoff at sale.

Do I have to be a veteran to assume a VA loan?

No. Any qualified buyer can assume a VA loan, but unless the buyer is a veteran who substitutes their own entitlement for the seller's, the seller's VA entitlement stays tied to the loan even after the sale closes.

Do I still have to qualify for the loan I'm assuming?

Yes. The existing loan servicer reviews the buyer's credit, income, and debt-to-income ratio, similar to a new loan application, before approving the assumption.

What happens if the seller's loan balance is much lower than the sale price?

That equity gap has to be covered separately, usually in cash, through a second mortgage at current rates, or occasionally seller financing for part of the difference.

How long does an assumption take to close?

Commonly 45 to 120 days, since the existing loan servicer handles the approval rather than a lender competing for new business, so it generally takes longer than a standard 30-to-45-day financed purchase.

How do I find out if a Woodland Hills listing has an assumable loan?

Listings don't typically flag this as a searchable field, so it usually means asking the listing agent directly about the seller's loan type before writing an offer.
