Does the Blocked Woodland Hills Country Club Project Affect My Home Value?

Jonathan Lopez, Woodland Hills Realtor | Jim Sandoval Group | CA DRE #02282844 · Updated September 2026

If you live near the Woodland Hills Country Club, or you're watching that corner of west Woodland Hills as a buyer, there's real news worth knowing about. On August 26, 2026, the Los Angeles City Council voted unanimously to block a large housing development planned for the site, and the reasoning behind that vote has real implications for anyone with property nearby.

What Actually Happened

The development, proposed by Arrium Capital, a Newport Beach based real estate private equity firm, would have added 175 single family homes plus 126 apartments (97 of them affordable units, including 32 for seniors and 65 for families) at 21150 Dumetz Road, on the edge of west Woodland Hills where the neighborhood meets the Santa Monica Mountains. The plan included roughly 835 parking spaces, internal streets, and surface parking areas, with single family homes planned up to three stories and apartment buildings up to four.

Arrium was attempting to fast track the project under AB 2011, a state law allowing faster, ministerial approval for multifamily housing on certain commercial corridors. Councilman Bob Blumenfield's office asked for a zoning amendment specifically to close what his office called "an unintended loophole," the city's rule allowing parking lots on A-1 (agricultural and residential) zoned parcels through a conditional use permit, which Arrium was using to get a foothold for the larger project.

The zoning amendment is a bridge to something bigger: an interim control ordinance from the LA Department of City Planning, expected to come before the council separately, that would block permits for demolition, building, grading, and parking-lot use on A-1 zoned parcels near sensitive areas, specifically very high fire severity zones and hillsides. If approved, that ordinance would run for 45 days, with the option to extend it a full year.

Why the Council Blocked It

Two stated reasons: the site sits inside a Very High Fire Hazard Severity Zone, and it's documented habitat for mountain lions along the Santa Monica Mountains corridor. Blumenfield's office cited "a plethora of documentation" from neighbors along with biological and ecological research from the Santa Monica Mountains Conservancy supporting the wildlife habitat determination.

Not everyone agrees this was the right call. Housing advocacy groups, including Abundant Housing LA and the Housing Action Coalition, argued LA can't afford to block new housing amid a real shortage, and a representative for Arrium told the Los Angeles Times the company will likely sue, arguing the city will eventually be forced to approve the project. So this isn't necessarily the final word, it's a real, current blocking action, not a permanent resolution.

What This Actually Means If You Own or Want to Buy Nearby

Two concrete, practical takeaways for anyone with property near that corner of west Woodland Hills, not speculation about how the litigation turns out:

No major new competing supply is landing there in the near term. A project of this size, 301 total units, would have been a meaningful addition to inventory in that specific pocket of the neighborhood. With it blocked, at least for now, and likely for the better part of a year if the interim control ordinance goes through as expected, homeowners in that immediate area aren't facing new large scale competition anytime soon. This is the same kind of supply-side factor worth weighing alongside how an ADU affects a Woodland Hills home's appraised value, real, local supply dynamics matter more to a specific property's value than broad citywide averages.

The fire hazard zone designation is the same factor already affecting insurance costs. Woodland Hills has real, documented insurance pressure tied to its Very High Fire Hazard Severity Zone areas. Major carriers have been pulling back statewide, State Farm alone non-renewed roughly 30,000 California policies in 2024, pushing more homeowners onto the California FAIR Plan, which has grown 152% since 2022 and just received CDI approval for a 29.1% rate increase effective October 15, 2026. If you're in or near a hillside pocket of 91367, this zoning fight is a reminder that fire zone status isn't an abstract label, it has real, current cost implications. The Honest Caveat

This is a live, contested situation. The developer has signaled it will likely sue. Housing advocates are actively lobbying against the block. The interim control ordinance hasn't been finalized yet. None of that changes the two takeaways above, no near-term new supply, real insurance implications tied to fire zone status, but it does mean this is worth revisiting if the litigation or the ordinance vote produces a different outcome later this year.

Frequently Asked Questions

What exactly did the LA City Council block at the Woodland Hills Country Club?

A proposed development of 175 single family homes and 126 apartments (97 affordable units) at 21150 Dumetz Road. The council approved a zoning amendment on August 26, 2026 that blocks the parking-lot loophole the developer was using to advance the project, and a separate interim control ordinance is expected to formally pause similar hillside development near high fire risk zones.

Why did the council block this specific project?

Two stated reasons: the site sits in a Very High Fire Hazard Severity Zone, and it's documented mountain lion habitat along the Santa Monica Mountains corridor, per research from the Santa Monica Mountains Conservancy.

Is this a permanent block, or could the project still happen?

Not necessarily permanent. The interim control ordinance, if approved, would run 45 days with a possible one-year extension, not indefinitely. The developer has publicly said it will likely sue, arguing the city's own housing laws (AB 2011) should have allowed the project to proceed.

Does this affect home values near the Woodland Hills Country Club?

It removes a large source of near-term new competing inventory, 301 total units, from that specific pocket of west Woodland Hills, at least for the duration of the block. It does not by itself guarantee higher prices. It's one real factor in that area's supply picture, not a prediction.

Does this affect my homeowners insurance if I live near there?
