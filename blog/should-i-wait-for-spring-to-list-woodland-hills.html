Should I Wait for Spring to List My Woodland Hills Home?

Jonathan Lopez, Woodland Hills Realtor | Jim Sandoval Group | CA DRE #02282844 · Updated August 2026

It's one of the most common questions Woodland Hills homeowners ask heading into late summer: should I list now, or hold off for "the spring market" everyone talks about? There's a real, data-backed answer instead of a guess, though it comes with an honest caveat about where the data comes from.

What the Citywide Data Actually Shows

Realtor.com's Los Angeles data (via Clever Real Estate, article updated August 1, 2026) puts the annual average days on market at 41. February, March, April, and June all run faster than that, averaging 36 days, about 5 days quicker than the yearly average. January and December are the slowest months, at 55 and 54 days respectively. Price follows a similar pattern: June brings the highest average sale price citywide at $874,333, roughly $36,000 above the annual average, while January brings the lowest at $815,392, about $23,000 below average.

Why This Isn't a Woodland Hills Specific Number, and What to Do About That

Public data sources don't break out days on market or sale price by month at the zip code or neighborhood level, only citywide. So these figures describe Los Angeles broadly, not Woodland Hills specifically. What can be said with real, cited data about Woodland Hills itself: the area's current average days on market is about 51 days, per Movoto's local market data (updated July 15, 2026), slower than the LA-wide average, which tracks with Woodland Hills' higher price point and narrower buyer pool at that price range. The seasonal pattern is a well-documented citywide and national trend, and there's no market-specific reason to think Woodland Hills is exempt from it, but the exact magnitude locally isn't something public data can currently confirm.

The Rate Question

Mortgage rates were at 6.66% for a 30-year fixed as of July 30, 2026, per Freddie Mac's Primary Mortgage Market Survey. Fannie Mae's most recent 2026 forecast projects rates landing in the 5.9-6.2% range, not a guaranteed near-term drop. Waiting for rates to fall meaningfully before listing is a bet, not a plan, and it comes with a real cost: sitting through the historically slowest months of the year while waiting.

The Actual Tradeoff

Listing now still falls within a reasonably strong window before the citywide slowdown that typically begins in the fall. Waiting until next spring means listing after October through January, the months with the longest average days on market and lowest average prices citywide. That doesn't mean spring is a bad time to sell, it consistently performs well, it means the cost of waiting is sitting through the slow season first to get there. Frequently Asked Questions

What's the best month to sell a house in Los Angeles?

For speed, February through June all average 36 days on market, versus a 41-day annual average. For price, June is the strongest month citywide, averaging $874,333, about $36,000 above the yearly average.

Is August a bad time to list in Woodland Hills?

Not based on the citywide data. August isn't the peak month, but it isn't the slow season either, that's typically October through January. Woodland Hills' own current pace is about 51 days on market, per Movoto, regardless of season, given the price point here.

Should I wait for mortgage rates to drop before listing?

Rates sat at 6.66% as of July 30, 2026 (Freddie Mac), and Fannie Mae's 2026 forecast projects a 5.9-6.2% range, no guaranteed drop. Waiting on rates means also waiting through the slower fall and winter months, a real tradeoff, not a free option.

Does the Los Angeles seasonal pattern actually apply to Woodland Hills?

Public data doesn't report days on market or price by month at the zip or neighborhood level, so this can't be confirmed with hyperlocal precision. The seasonal direction is a well-documented broader trend with no specific reason to expect Woodland Hills is an exception, but the exact local magnitude isn't publicly available data.
