Where Are People Relocating to Woodland Hills From, and What Does That Mean for My Search?

Jonathan Lopez, Woodland Hills Realtor | Jim Sandoval Group | CA DRE #02282844 · Updated August 2026

Woodland Hills gets its share of relocation clients: people whose company moved them, whose family situation changed, or who simply decided a change made sense. A common question from that group, especially anyone who has read a "Californians are fleeing" headline in the last year, is some version of: does anyone actually move here, or am I an outlier? The real migration data answers that clearly, and it points to a more specific and more useful answer than the headlines suggest.

Most Los Angeles Home Searchers Aren't Going Anywhere

Redfin's own Los Angeles metro migration data, covering search activity from January through March 2026, shows that 86% of LA-area homebuyers searched to stay within the LA metro area. Only 14% searched to move somewhere else. The outbound headlines that dominate coverage of California's housing market are describing a real trend, but they're describing the minority of local search activity, not the majority.

Where the Inbound Buyers Are Actually Coming From

Nationally, 7% of homebuyers searching to move into the LA metro came from outside metros entirely, according to the same Redfin data. Ranked by net inflow for the same period, the top five origin metros sending buyers into LA were San Francisco (1,929 net searchers), Seattle (904), Chicago (742), Washington DC (661), and New York (476). Every one of those cities carries a higher cost of living and, in most cases, a higher median home price than Los Angeles itself. That's the practical detail relocation clients tend to miss: the people moving into this market are disproportionately coming from places where their housing dollar goes even less far than it does here.

For balance, it's worth naming the other side of this data too. Las Vegas, San Diego, Bakersfield, Phoenix, and Dallas were the top destinations for people searching to leave LA in the same period, led by Las Vegas at a net outflow of 3,778 searchers. That outbound trend, and what it does and doesn't mean for a Woodland Hills seller, was already covered in an earlier post on whether everyone is really leaving California. This post is specifically about the inbound side, since that's the more relevant number for anyone relocating in rather than out.

No Public Data Breaks This Out Below the Metro Level

It's worth being direct about a limitation here: no migration data source, Redfin included, publishes figures at the zip code or neighborhood level. There's no dataset that says specifically how many people relocated into 91367 versus, say, Sherman Oaks or Calabasas. Los Angeles metro data is the smallest real, publicly available geography for this question, and Woodland Hills sits inside that metro, so that's the data used here, stated plainly rather than implied to be more local than it actually is.

What Woodland Hills' Own Market Looks Like Right Now

Movoto's August 2026 update on Woodland Hills puts the median list price at $1.3M, down 7% from a year earlier, with 277 active listings and 27 new listings on the market. Median time on market sits at 47 days, down 2% year over year, and Movoto's own market summary describes current conditions as slowing. For a buyer relocating from San Francisco or Seattle, where median prices and competition both tend to run higher, that combination, a lower price point relative to their origin market plus a market that isn't in a frenzy, is a meaningfully different experience than what a lot of relocating buyers expect walking in.

What This Means If You're Actually Relocating Here

The migration data is directional, not a guarantee about any one buyer's experience, and it doesn't replace a real conversation about your specific budget, timeline, and priorities. What it does do is correct a common assumption: Woodland Hills isn't short on incoming buyers, and the ones arriving are frequently coming from markets that make the west San Fernando Valley look like relative value, not a compromise.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where are most people relocating to Los Angeles actually coming from?

Per Redfin's Los Angeles metro migration data (Jan-Mar 2026), the top origin metros by net inflow were San Francisco (1,929 net searchers), Seattle (904), Chicago (742), Washington DC (661), and New York (476).

Is Woodland Hills specifically gaining or losing residents right now?

There's no public data at the zip code or neighborhood level to answer that precisely. Los Angeles metro data shows 86% of local searchers plan to stay within the metro and 14% search to leave, and Woodland Hills sits inside that broader LA metro figure.

Does this contradict the reporting on people leaving California?

No, both are true at once. Las Vegas, San Diego, Bakersfield, Phoenix, and Dallas were the top destinations for LA leavers in the same period (Las Vegas led at a net outflow of 3,778 searchers). Outbound and inbound migration are separate, real trends happening simultaneously, and this post focuses on the inbound side specifically for relocating buyers.

Is now a calmer time to buy in Woodland Hills compared to a typical hot market?

Movoto's August 2026 data shows Woodland Hills' median list price down 7% year over year with 277 active listings and a 47-day median time on market, and Movoto's own summary describes conditions as slowing. That's different from the multiple-offer environment common in the metros sending the most buyers here, like San Francisco and Seattle.

What should I do first if my company is relocating me to the Valley?

Start with a real conversation about your budget, timeline, and must-haves rather than relying on national migration data or headlines. A relocation move usually comes with a firm start date, so getting pre-approved and building a search plan early matters more than it would in a less time-sensitive purchase.
