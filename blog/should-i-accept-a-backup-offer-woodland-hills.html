Should I Accept a Backup Offer on My Woodland Hills Listing in Case My Deal Falls Through?

Jonathan Lopez, Woodland Hills Realtor · Jim Sandoval Group · CA DRE #02282844 · Updated November 2026

If you've listed a home in Woodland Hills, accepted an offer, and then had a second buyer come in with a strong offer of their own, you're not stuck choosing between ignoring it or breaking your current contract. California's Back-Up Offer Addendum, C.A.R. Form BUO, exists for exactly this situation, and it's worth understanding before you assume a second offer is either worthless or a reason to walk away from the first one.

A backup offer is a complete, fully signed purchase contract that a seller accepts and holds in second position while the first buyer is still moving through escrow. It isn't a bid to replace the current buyer, and it doesn't let a seller jump to the second buyer just because they'd prefer them; the first contract stays controlling unless and until it's actually cancelled in writing. What the backup offer does is put a second, ready buyer in line, so if the first deal falls apart, the seller doesn't have to relist, remarket, and start the negotiation over from zero.

One detail sellers and backup buyers both find reassuring: the backup buyer's earnest money deposit typically isn't collected until they actually move into first position. While they're sitting in the backup slot, nothing of theirs is financially tied up. If the first buyer closes normally, the backup offer simply expires with no money ever changing hands on the backup buyer's side.

If the first contract does cancel, the transition isn't instant or automatic in the sense of skipping a buyer's ability to reconsider. The backup buyer typically gets a short window, commonly 3 days, to confirm they still want to move forward, essentially re-affirming their offer now that it's actually live. Once that confirmation happens, escrow opens on the backup offer the same way it would for any brand new accepted offer, not as a continuation of the first buyer's now-dead timeline. There's no renegotiation forced on either side and no bidding war; the price and terms are whatever the backup offer already specified when it was signed.

Is this actually worth bothering with for a typical Woodland Hills sale? The numbers suggest it's a reasonable precaution rather than a rare edge case. Redfin's national cancellation data, published July 31, 2026, found that 13.6% of pending U.S. home sales fell out of contract before closing, a little under 1 in 7. Based on a survey of 443 agents handling cancellations, the leading cause by far was inspection or repair disputes, cited in 70.4% of cancellations, followed by the buyer's financing falling through (27.8%), the buyer's own home not selling in time (21.0%), and a change in the buyer's financial circumstances (14.9%); these causes overlap, since a single cancelled deal can involve more than one. None of this data is Woodland Hills-specific, since no local cancellation-rate study exists, but it's a reasonable basis for taking a credible second offer seriously instead of dismissing it.

Locally, 91367's current numbers (Movoto, accessed 2026-11-10, search-snapshot pull; direct page fetch returned a 403 error this session) show a $1,199,000 median list price, 198 active listings, and homes averaging 41 days on market, up from 33 a year ago, a market where a serious second buyer isn't necessarily easy to replace on short notice if the first deal collapses.

Frequently Asked Questions

What exactly is a backup offer in California?

It's a fully signed purchase contract that a seller accepts in second position, using C.A.R.'s Back-Up Offer Addendum (Form BUO), while the first buyer is still actively in escrow on the property.

Can a seller use a backup offer to push out the current buyer early?

No. The first contract remains controlling unless it's actually cancelled in writing; a backup offer doesn't give the seller a way to jump to a preferred second buyer while the first deal is still alive.

Does the backup buyer have to put down a deposit right away?

No. The backup buyer's earnest money deposit typically isn't collected until they actually move into first position, so nothing is tied up while they're waiting behind the active contract.

What happens if the first deal falls through?

The backup buyer typically gets a short window, commonly 3 days, to confirm they still want to proceed. Once confirmed, escrow opens on the backup offer as if it were a brand new accepted offer, not a continuation of the first buyer's timeline.

How often does a deal actually fall through, so is a backup worth bothering with?

Nationally, Redfin found 13.6% of pending home sales cancelled before closing as of mid-2026, with inspection or repair disputes the leading cause (70.4% of cancellations) and financing failure second (27.8%). There's no Woodland Hills-specific rate, but it's common enough to be worth a real conversation rather than ignoring a solid second offer.

Can the backup buyer back out once they're confirmed into first position?

This post's sources don't address that specific scenario in detail; once confirmed, the backup offer functions like a new accepted offer, so the backup buyer would be subject to whatever contingencies and terms that offer itself contains, the same as any other buyer. Your agent and transaction coordinator should walk through the exact form language with you before you rely on this.
