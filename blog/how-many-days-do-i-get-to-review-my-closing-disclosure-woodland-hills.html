How Many Days Do I Get to Review My Closing Disclosure Before Closing on a Woodland Hills Home?

Jonathan Lopez, Jim Sandoval Group | Woodland Hills Realtor | CA DRE #02282844 · Updated October 2026

Woodland Hills first-time buyers often ask how much time they actually get to read the closing paperwork before signing. Per the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB), by law you must receive a copy of your Closing Disclosure three business days prior to closing. The CFPB says to contact your lender or closing agent at least a week before closing to find out who will send it and how: email, postal mail, or a website download.

If something important changes about your loan, you receive a new Closing Disclosure, and the CFPB says that in limited circumstances the law requires a full three business days to review the new one before closing. The CFPB page does not list which changes qualify, so ask your lender which changes would restart the clock on your loan.

What the CFPB suggests checking: the loan amount, loan type, loan term, interest rate, monthly payment, whether there is a prepayment penalty, and whether you are paying points or receiving credits. It also suggests comparing the APR on the Closing Disclosure to the APR on your Loan Estimate as an easy way to see whether costs have increased. By law, some fees cannot increase at all unless you asked for a change or your financial information changed, others are limited to a 10 percent increase, and a third group is not limited, per the CFPB.

Ask your lender or closing agent to send the Promissory Note, the Deed of Trust, and the Deed at the same time as the Closing Disclosure. If you are buying with another person, the CFPB notes that the deed should be structured to give you the type of ownership you want, and that you may want a real estate attorney to review.

Scams: the CFPB warns that scammers can pose as your real estate or settlement agent and suggest last-minute changes to wiring instructions to steal closing funds. Verify any change by phone using a number you already trust.

These are citywide figures. The CFPB guidance is national and applies to most mortgages, not only Woodland Hills. This is general information, not legal or lending advice.

Frequently Asked Questions

When must I receive my Closing Disclosure?

Per the CFPB, three business days before closing.

What if my loan changes late?

You receive a new Closing Disclosure, and in limited circumstances the law requires another full three business days to review it. Ask your lender which changes qualify.

What should I compare it against?

Your most recent Loan Estimate. The CFPB suggests comparing the APRs as an easy way to spot higher costs.

Can closing costs change after my Loan Estimate?

Yes. Per the CFPB, some fees cannot rise at all, some are capped at a 10 percent increase, and others are not limited.

Can I walk away at closing?

The CFPB says you are not committed until you sign, but you might lose your deposit or lender fees. Know your contract first.

How do I avoid wire fraud?

Confirm any wiring change by phone with a number you already know, since scammers can pose as your agent or settlement agent.
