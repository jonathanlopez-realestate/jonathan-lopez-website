Can My Woodland Hills Listing Stay Off Zillow While It's Getting Ready? Here's What Actually Changed in 2026

Jonathan Lopez, Woodland Hills Realtor | Jim Sandoval Group | CA DRE #02282844 · Updated August 2026

Woodland Hills sellers ask a version of this question constantly, usually before a renovation is finished, before a sensitive personal situation is ready to go public, or just because they want buyers to see a fully staged home rather than a work in progress: can my listing stay quiet, off Zillow and Realtor.com, while I get everything ready, and only go fully public once it's show-ready? For most of 2025 the answer was genuinely evolving. As of today, it has a clear answer, and it isn't the one a lot of sellers expect.

What NAR Changed in 2025

After more than a year of industry pressure over its Clear Cooperation Policy, which requires a listing broker to file a property with the MLS within one business day of publicly marketing it, the National Association of REALTORS didn't repeal that policy. Instead, effective March 25, 2025, NAR adopted a companion policy called Multiple Listing Options for Sellers. It created a new listing category, the Delayed Marketing Exempt Listing: a seller could stay filed in the MLS for cooperation with other agents (so buyer's agents could still see and show the home), while the listing broker delayed sending that listing out through IDX and syndication (the feeds that push listings to Zillow, Realtor.com, and similar sites) for a period each local MLS decided on its own. It required a signed seller disclosure acknowledging the tradeoff.

CRMLS Said No

CRMLS, the MLS that covers Woodland Hills and most of the San Fernando Valley, publicly rejected the Delayed Marketing option in an April 18, 2025 statement. Its board's reasoning: the category didn't actually add a real seller choice beyond what its existing Active status already allowed (brokers can already opt a listing out of IDX and syndication at the office level if a seller wants that), and adding a new status risked confusing agents and clients about where a listing would and wouldn't appear online. CRMLS also pointed out a practical gap in NAR's own policy: major portals already receive CRMLS's VOW (Virtual Office Website) data feed regardless of IDX/syndication settings, so a "delayed marketing" listing would still surface on some large sites while just being harder for a consumer to find elsewhere, not actually private.

What CRMLS Offers Instead

Two real options exist for a Woodland Hills seller who isn't ready to go fully public yet. The first is Registered status, sometimes called Office Exclusive: the listing is filed with CRMLS internally (so the listing brokerage has a record and other agents within that same office can work it), but it is not disseminated to other MLS participants, not shown on public portals, and not marketed anywhere at all, no sign, no website mention, no open house. The second is Coming Soon status: a listing can sit in the MLS for up to 21 days while a home is prepped for showings (staging, photography, repairs), visible to other CRMLS agents for cooperation, but with no showings permitted during that window.

The Change That Matters Most Right Now

Through early 2026, Coming Soon status was the closest thing Woodland Hills sellers had to a "get ready quietly" option, since it kept a listing off IDX feeds and public portals while it was being prepped. That changed on March 31, 2026. CRMLS announced it had begun syndicating Coming Soon listings through IDX to public portals, including Zillow, Realtor.com, and Homes.com, for any listing entered into the MLS on or after March 10, 2026. In practice, that means Coming Soon listings in Woodland Hills today show up on the same major sites as an active listing. It still blocks showings during the up-to-21-day window, but it no longer keeps a listing out of public view.

So What's Left for Genuine Privacy

Registered (Office Exclusive) status is the only option that still keeps a listing off public sites entirely, and it comes with real limits. Under Clear Cooperation, the moment any public marketing happens, a sign in the yard, a mention on a brokerage website, a flyer, certain multi-brokerage communication about the property, the listing broker has one business day to move that listing into Coming Soon or Active status. Registered status also means the listing isn't being shown to buyer's agents outside the listing office, which can mean fewer eyes on the home during that period, a real tradeoff against the privacy benefit.

How This Connects to Woodland Hills' Current Market

With 401 active listings and about a quarter of them already price-reduced (Movoto, updated August 19, 2026), buyer's agents are actively working this market and checking new inventory closely. A seller weighing privacy against exposure should understand that choosing Registered status over Coming Soon or Active isn't a neutral choice right now, it trades away a meaningful slice of the buyer pool that's actively watching new listings hit the market in a competitive window. That's the same exposure math worth running before deciding whether to wait for a different season to list at all, timing and visibility decisions both come back to how many real buyers actually see the home.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I keep my Woodland Hills home off Zillow while I get it ready to show?

Only with Registered (Office Exclusive) status, and only until any public marketing happens, at which point CRMLS rules require moving it to Coming Soon or Active within one business day. Coming Soon status, as of March 31, 2026, no longer keeps a listing off Zillow, Realtor.com, or Homes.com.

What's the difference between Registered and Coming Soon status at CRMLS?

Registered means no marketing anywhere, not even to other agents outside your listing office. Coming Soon means the listing is visible to other CRMLS agents for cooperation and, since March 31, 2026, also visible on public portals, but showings aren't allowed for up to 21 days.

Did NAR actually kill the Clear Cooperation Policy in 2025?

No. NAR retained Clear Cooperation and layered a new companion option (Delayed Marketing Exempt Listings) on top of it for MLSs that chose to adopt it. CRMLS is not one of them.

Why did CRMLS reject NAR's new Delayed Marketing option?

CRMLS's board called it confusing, redundant with tools CRMLS already offered through its Active status, and inconsistent with CRMLS's transparency-first approach. CRMLS also noted that major portals already receive its full listing feed through a separate data channel, so the new option wouldn't have made a listing truly private anyway.

Does choosing Registered status hurt my chances of a fast sale?

It can. Registered listings aren't shared with buyer's agents outside your own brokerage, which narrows exposure during that period. In a market like Woodland Hills' current one, where about 1 in 4 active listings has already had a price reduction, less exposure during the early window is a real tradeoff worth weighing against whatever privacy concern is driving the decision.

How long can a listing actually stay in Coming Soon status?

Up to 21 days under CRMLS rules (longer for new construction). No showings are allowed during that window regardless of how the listing is being marketed.
