Should I Take the First Offer on My Woodland Hills Home?

Jonathan Lopez, Jim Sandoval Group | Woodland Hills Realtor | CA DRE #02282844 · Updated September 2026

Woodland Hills sellers often ask whether to take the first offer that comes in or wait for a better one. The honest answer from the local numbers is that the speed of an offer tells you less than what the offer actually says.

What the Woodland Hills Numbers Say

Redfin's July 2026 data for Woodland Hills (a 3-month rolling average) shows homes selling at 99.5% of list price on average, up 1.4 points from a year earlier. About 34.6% of homes sold above list price, up 8.2 points, while 29.6% of homes had a price drop, down 5.4 points. The median time on market was 44 days, six days faster than a year earlier, and 229 homes sold. Movoto's data, updated September 30, 2026, shows 421 active listings, 71 of them price-reduced, and an average of 48 days on market compared with 45 a year ago, with 367 homes sold in August versus 331 a year earlier. The two sources measure things differently, so the days-on-market figures do not match exactly, but both describe an active market with real buyers and real price cuts.

When a First Offer Is a Real Signal

If your home is priced in line with current comparable sales and a strong offer arrives quickly, that is often the market confirming your price. With roughly a third of Woodland Hills homes selling above list, a fast, clean offer at or near your asking price is not something to dismiss just because it came first.

When It May Be the Only Serious Offer

Nearly 3 in 10 homes here needed a price drop. If your home is priced above where comparable sales support, an early offer may be the best signal you will get before a price reduction becomes necessary. Waiting for a better offer on an overpriced listing can cost more in time and eventual price cuts than a fair early offer would have. See will I have to cut my price if I list right now for how pricing drives those cuts.

What to Compare Besides the Price

Price is only one line. Compare the buyer's financing strength, the size of the deposit, the length and number of contingencies, the closing timeline, and whether the offer depends on the buyer selling another home. A slightly lower offer with fewer contingencies can be worth more than a higher one that is more likely to fall apart. For how a low appraisal can affect a deal, read what happens if the appraisal comes in low.

Bottom Line

Do not take an offer or reject it based on how early it showed up. Decide based on a current comparable sales analysis and a clear look at the terms, not the calendar.
