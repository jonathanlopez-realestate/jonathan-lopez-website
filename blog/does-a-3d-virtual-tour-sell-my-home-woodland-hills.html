Does a 3D Virtual Tour Actually Help Sell a Woodland Hills Home Above the Median?

Jonathan Lopez, Woodland Hills Realtor | Jim Sandoval Group | CA DRE #02282844 · Updated August 2026

Every listing pitch above a certain price point now includes a 3D virtual tour. Photographers offer it as an add-on, agents list it as a selling point in their marketing packages, and sellers are told it makes a listing "stand out." What almost nobody breaks down for a seller is what the actual data says a virtual tour does, and doesn't do, once it's live. That question matters more the higher a home is priced, and Woodland Hills' current median is already $1,345,000, per Movoto's market trends page, updated August 25, 2026.

What the Engagement Data Actually Shows

Zillow published the clearest numbers on this in a 2022 press release announcing that its 3D Home tours would automatically sync to Redfin listings as well. In that release, Zillow stated plainly: "Homes on Zillow that included 3D Home tours saw improved performance compared to those without, earning 81% more views and being saved by buyers 53% more often." That's a real, measurable jump in engagement, and it's not a small one. It also means a single 3D tour, once built, reaches both of the two most visited listing portals without any extra work from the agent or seller, since Zillow pushes it to Redfin automatically.

More views and more saves are genuinely useful. A save means a buyer flagged the home to revisit, come back to, or bring up with their agent. A view means the listing got noticed in a crowded feed instead of scrolled past. For a seller trying to stand out among Woodland Hills' 389 currently active listings, that's not nothing.

What Buyers Themselves Say Actually Helps Them

Here's where the picture gets more honest. The National Association of Realtors' Real Estate in a Digital Age report, based on its 2018 NAR Home Buyer and Seller Generational Trends Report, asked buyers directly which website features they found "very useful" during their home search. Photos topped the list at 87%. Floor plans came in at 52%. Virtual tours came in at 46%, behind both. In other words, buyers click on virtual tours more than average listing content, which is exactly what Zillow's engagement numbers show, but when asked what actually helped them make sense of a home and decide whether it was worth pursuing, they ranked plain photos and a floor plan higher than the tour itself.

That's not a contradiction. It's two different questions with two different honest answers. Does a 3D tour get more eyes on a listing? Yes, measurably. Is it the single feature buyers say mattered most to their decision? No, photos and floor plans still rank higher.

Who Actually Uses This Feature the Most

NAR's report also broke its findings down by generation, and the split matters for a Woodland Hills seller thinking about who's likely to be looking at their listing. The report found that both Younger and Older Boomers used online video content more frequently than other age groups when searching for a home, and that among the Silent Generation specifically, real estate agent contact information, virtual tours, and photos ranked as the most important website features, more so than for younger buyers. That runs counter to the assumption that virtual tours are mainly a tool for tech-savvy younger buyers. If a Woodland Hills seller's likely buyer pool skews toward an established, higher-net-worth demographic, which is common at this price point, a 3D tour isn't a gimmick aimed at a younger audience. It's a feature an older, more deliberate buyer is statistically more likely to actually use.

What It Actually Costs

Pricing varies by vendor and square footage, but multiple Los Angeles-area real estate photography pricing guides converge on a typical range of roughly $200 to $400 per listing for a standard-sized home, with larger or more elaborate properties running higher depending on square footage and add-ons like guided tour narration or drone footage bundled in. That's a real cost range from current vendor pricing, not a single controlled study, so it should be treated as a starting point for getting an actual quote rather than a fixed number.

What This Means for a Woodland Hills Listing Above the Median

No single marketing feature, a 3D tour included, is the thing that sells a home on its own. What the data supports is a layered approach: professional photography and a clear floor plan still do the heaviest lifting in a buyer's actual decision-making, and a 3D tour adds a real, verified engagement boost on top of that rather than replacing it. A seller being pitched "just get the tour, skip the rest" is being sold an incomplete package. A seller being pitched "photos, floor plan, and a tour together" is getting the version the data actually supports.

There's also a practical use case the numbers don't fully capture: a 3D tour lets an out-of-area or time-constrained buyer, the kind of buyer a higher-priced Woodland Hills listing is more likely to attract, narrow down which homes are worth flying in or driving across town to see in person. That's a real function even if it isn't captured cleanly in a "percent very useful" survey question.

How This Connects to Woodland Hills' Current Market

With 389 active listings and roughly a quarter of them already carrying a price reduction (Movoto, updated August 25, 2026), a Woodland Hills seller isn't just marketing to interested buyers, they're competing for attention against a real, sizeable pool of other active listings at the same time. A 49-day average time on market means most homes aren't selling in the first week. Every extra day a listing sits is another day it's competing in that same feed against everything else active. In that environment, the engagement boost a 3D tour delivers, more views, more saves, more of a listing's window of attention before a buyer moves on, is a genuine asset. It's just not a substitute for the photos and floor plan buyers say actually help them decide. That's the same lesson that showed up in the earlier look at photography and staging above $1 million: presentation works together to widen the buyer pool and shorten time on market, no single tactic is a guaranteed price bump on its own.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a 3D virtual tour actually help sell a Woodland Hills home faster?

It helps a listing get more attention. Zillow's own data shows listings with 3D Home tours earn 81% more views and get saved 53% more often. Whether that translates into a faster sale depends on everything else about the listing too, price, photos, and floor plan, not the tour alone.

Do buyers actually prefer virtual tours over regular photos?

Not according to NAR's Real Estate in a Digital Age report. Buyers found photos "very useful" 87% of the time, versus 46% for virtual tours. Buyers click on tours more, but when asked what actually helped their decision, they ranked photos and floor plans higher.

Is a 3D tour worth the extra cost for a luxury listing?

The data supports it as an add-on to strong photography and a floor plan, not a replacement for either. It adds a real, measurable engagement boost, more eyes on the listing, especially useful for out-of-area buyers narrowing down which homes to see in person, but it isn't the feature buyers say matters most.

Does a 3D tour posted on Zillow also show up on Redfin?

Yes. Since 2022, Zillow automatically syncs its 3D Home tours to matching Redfin listings, so a single tour reaches both major portals without any extra work from the agent.

What actually matters most to a buyer researching a Woodland Hills home online?

Based on NAR's data, professional photos first (87% "very useful"), a clear floor plan second (52%), and a virtual tour third (46%). All three working together outperform any one of them alone.

How competitive is the Woodland Hills market right now for buyer attention?

There are 389 active listings as of Movoto's August 25, 2026 update, and about 1 in 4 already carry a price reduction. The average home is on the market 49 days. Standing out in that pool is a real factor, which is why layered marketing, not any single tactic, matters.
