How Long Does My Woodland Hills Buyer Representation Agreement Actually Last, and Can I Get Out of It Early?

Jonathan Lopez, Woodland Hills Realtor · Jim Sandoval Group · CA DRE #02282844 · Updated November 2026

Most of the attention on buyer representation agreements over the past couple of years has gone to one question: do I have to sign one at all before touring a home? That part is settled now; since the industry-wide practice changes that took effect August 17, 2024, a buyer generally does need a signed, written agreement with their agent before touring a home, and that agreement has to state a specific, reasonable end date rather than running open-ended. What gets almost no attention is the next, more practical question for a buyer already mid-search: how long does that agreement actually run, and what happens if you want out of it before it expires?

There's no fixed state-mandated length. California doesn't set a statutory minimum or maximum term for a buyer representation agreement the way some other states do. What the law requires is that the agreement state a specific, identifiable end date, not that the date be any particular number of days out. In practice, 30, 60, and 90 days are the most common ranges Woodland Hills buyers see, but a longer term, especially for a buyer doing a slower, more selective search, isn't unusual either. The length is a negotiated term like any other part of the agreement, which means the time to raise it is before you sign, not after you're a month in and wish you'd asked for something shorter.

Getting out early is usually more straightforward than people assume. If a buyer isn't happy with their agent, whether it's responsiveness, fit, or just a change of plans, most standard agreements allow for mutual written cancellation: both the buyer and the agent sign a release ending the agreement before its stated term expires. This isn't automatic and isn't one-sided, since the agent generally has to agree to the release too, but in practice a buyer who clearly isn't being served well, and who raises it directly, usually isn't stuck for the full term against their will. What a buyer can't generally do is simply stop responding to their agent and consider the agreement void; if there's a dispute, the actual written terms of the signed agreement control, not an assumption that it quietly lapses.

The part that catches people off guard: the protection period after the agreement ends. Widely used agreement forms in California, including the C.A.R. Residential Buyer/Broker Agreement, commonly include a post-termination or post-expiration protection clause, sometimes called a holdover or safety clause, often running around 90 days (the exact number is set by the specific agreement signed, not by statute). Under this kind of clause, if a buyer ends up purchasing a specific property that their agent actually introduced, showed, or submitted an offer on during the agreement's active term, the original agent can still be entitled to compensation even if the sale closes after the agreement ended or was cancelled. This protects the agent's actual work on a specific property, not a general claim over every home a buyer might look at afterward with someone else.

Why this matters before you sign, and before you try to cancel. A buyer who skims past the term-length and protection-period language, then later switches agents mid-search, can end up in a dispute over compensation on a home the first agent genuinely introduced, something that's avoidable just by reading those two sections closely up front. On the other side, a buyer who understands the real term and the real cancellation mechanics going in has a much easier time raising concerns early, instead of feeling stuck in an agreement that, in most cases, has a real, negotiated off-ramp.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a Woodland Hills buyer representation agreement last?

There's no state-mandated length. The agreement must state a specific, reasonable end date, and 30, 60, or 90 days are common negotiated ranges, though a longer term is sometimes used for a slower search. The exact length is set by what you and your agent agree to before signing.

Can I cancel my buyer representation agreement early if I'm not happy?

Usually, yes, through mutual written cancellation, meaning both you and your agent sign a release ending the agreement before its term expires. The agent generally has to agree to the release; simply stopping communication doesn't automatically void the agreement.

If I cancel and switch agents, can my old agent still get paid if I buy a home they showed me?

Possibly, under a common post-termination protection or holdover clause, often around 90 days, that can apply if you buy a specific property that agent actually introduced, showed, or wrote an offer on during the agreement's active term. It generally doesn't extend to every home you might look at afterward with someone else.

Is the protection period the same length in every agreement?

No. The length is set by the specific agreement you signed, not by state law. Read that section before you sign, since it's negotiable at that point, not after.

Does this agreement also set how much I pay my agent?

Compensation terms are negotiated directly between you and your agent and spelled out in the same written agreement, separate from the term-length and protection-period questions covered here. See this site's existing post on signing a buyer agreement for how that negotiation works post-2024.

What should I actually check before I sign a buyer representation agreement in Woodland Hills?

Confirm the stated end date, ask what happens if you want to cancel early, and read the post-termination protection clause so you know how long it runs and what it covers. A good agent will walk through all three with you before you sign, not just hand you the signature line.
