Does a Pool Actually Add Value to My Woodland Hills Home? Here's the Real Data

Jonathan Lopez, Woodland Hills Realtor | Jim Sandoval Group | CA DRE #02282844 · Updated September 2026

If you own a home in Woodland Hills with a pool, or you're weighing whether to build one, you've probably heard both sides of the argument. Some people will tell you a pool is a great investment that pays for itself at resale. Others will tell you it's a money pit that scares off half your buyer pool. Both takes exist because both can be true, depending entirely on where you live. In a hot, dry climate like the San Fernando Valley, the math tends to favor pool owners more than almost anywhere else in the country.

The Headline Number, and Its Real Age

The most cited figure for pool value in Los Angeles comes from a Redfin analysis published in March 2019 and last updated in October 2020. Redfin's data journalism team calculated the value of a private pool as a premium on a home's per-square-foot sale price across 19 major U.S. metro areas that met their inclusion criteria. Los Angeles came out on top of every metro analyzed: homes with pools sold for an estimated $95,393 more than comparable homes without one, a $56.45 per-square-foot premium. For comparison, the next closest metros, Orange County and Austin, added roughly $50,000 to $55,000. Phoenix, despite also being a hot, dry climate, added only about $11,591, a reminder that heat alone doesn't drive the number, local buyer expectations do.

It's worth being direct about the age of that figure. Redfin has not republished this specific study since 2020, so $95,393 reflects 2018 sales data, not a live 2026 number. Home prices, construction costs, and buyer preferences have all shifted since then. What hasn't changed, based on everything else in the data below, is the underlying pattern: pools carry a real premium in Los Angeles, and that premium is larger here than almost anywhere else measured. One detail from that original report is worth repeating directly: Redfin agent Lindsay Katz, who works in the LA market, said pools are "almost a given" in parts of the San Fernando Valley specifically, and that a home without one can be genuinely harder to sell there.

How Common Are Pools in Woodland Hills, Specifically?

This is where the local data gets useful. Crosstown LA, a data journalism outlet that analyzed 20 years of Los Angeles Department of Building and Safety permit records, found that Woodland Hills received 716 pool permits between 2020 and July 31, 2025. That's the 5th highest total of any neighborhood in the city of Los Angeles, behind only Sherman Oaks (940), Chatsworth (882), Encino (845), and Brentwood (742). In other words, this isn't a neighborhood where a pool is a rare, standout feature. It's one of the most pool-dense parts of the city, which supports the idea that buyers here are used to seeing them, expecting them, and pricing accordingly.

What It Actually Costs to Build One Today

Before weighing whether to add a pool, the cost side matters as much as the value side. According to PoolGuard USA's 2026 Los Angeles cost guide, the average in-ground pool installation runs $82,140, with most homeowners spending between $58,880 and $105,400. Material choice moves that range significantly: vinyl liner pools run $40,000 to $75,000, fiberglass $55,000 to $100,000, and concrete or gunite, the most durable and customizable option, $65,000 to $120,000. On top of installation, expect ongoing costs: national data puts annual pool maintenance at roughly $3,000 to $5,000 in a typical year, more if major repairs come up.

The Honest ROI Picture

Nationally, a pool adds an estimated 5% to 8% to a home's value on average, according to Zillow data cited by Opendoor's 2026 pool-value guide. That range climbs to 10% to 15% or more in Sun Belt states, California included, where the climate supports year-round use. But even at the high end, the typical return on installation cost lands at 40% to 60%. That means an $82,000 pool, roughly the LA average, might add something in the neighborhood of $33,000 to $49,000 in resale value, not the full cost back. A pool is, realistically, part investment and part lifestyle purchase. Anyone building one purely to make money at resale is usually disappointed; anyone building one to actually use it for years, with a partial value recovery as a bonus, tends to be more satisfied with the decision either way. This is a similar tradeoff to the one covered in whether an ADU actually raises your home's value, where the real return also lands well short of full cost recovery.

Where Woodland Hills' Market Stands Right Now

As of today, there are 391 active listings in Woodland Hills, with 34 newly listed and 93 already price-reduced. The median sale price in August 2026 was $1,348,500, with homes taking an average of 48 days to sell, up slightly from 45 days a year ago. 367 homes sold here in August 2026, up from 331 the year before (Movoto). In a market this active, with this much pool inventory already built into local expectations, whether your home has a pool, needs one, or is competing against pool homes nearby is a real factor in how it should be priced and marketed, not a footnote.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a pool actually add value to a home in Woodland Hills?

The best available data, Redfin's 2019 metro analysis, found Los Angeles homes with pools sold for an estimated $95,393 more than comparable homes without one, the highest premium of any major U.S. metro measured. That figure is dated (2018 sales data, not updated since 2020), but Woodland Hills' own pool density (716 permits since 2020, 5th most in the city) supports the idea that pools remain a real expectation here, not just a historical trend.

How much does it cost to build a pool in Woodland Hills right now?

Local 2026 cost guides put the average Los Angeles in-ground pool installation at $82,140, typically ranging from $58,880 to $105,400 depending on size and material. Concrete and gunite pools run higher, $65,000 to $120,000, while vinyl liner pools are the most affordable at $40,000 to $75,000.

Will I get my full pool cost back when I sell?

Usually not. National data shows pools typically recover 40% to 60% of installation cost at resale, even in markets like Los Angeles where the value premium is strong. A pool tends to be a better decision when it's built primarily to be used and enjoyed, with resale value as a partial bonus rather than the main goal.

Is not having a pool a disadvantage when selling in Woodland Hills?

It can be. Woodland Hills is one of the more pool-dense neighborhoods in Los Angeles, with 716 pool permits issued between 2020 and mid-2025, the 5th most citywide. Redfin's own LA agents have noted that in parts of the San Fernando Valley, a pool is close to an expected feature, and its absence can make a home a harder sell relative to comparable homes that have one.

Is the $95,393 figure specific to Woodland Hills, or is it a broader number?

It's a Los Angeles metro-wide figure from Redfin, not a Woodland Hills specific one, and no public source breaks this particular study out by neighborhood or zip code. Woodland Hills' own pool permit volume is used here as the best available local signal of how normalized pools are in this specific area.

How do I know what my specific pool, or lack of one, means for my home's value?

Pool age, condition, permitting status, and how common pools are on your specific street all affect the real number for your property far more than any national or metro average can. A real comparative market analysis that accounts for your pool situation, not a generic online estimate, is the only reliable way to answer this for a specific address.
