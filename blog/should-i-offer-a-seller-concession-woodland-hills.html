Should I Offer a Seller Concession Instead of Cutting My Price in Woodland Hills?

Jonathan Lopez, Realtor serving Woodland Hills with the Jim Sandoval Group · CA DRE #02282844 · Updated October 2026

Sellers in Woodland Hills facing a slower-than-expected market often jump straight to one decision: cut the price. A recent Redfin report on seller concessions suggests there's a second lever worth comparing first, and it's already the more common move nationally.

Redfin's analysis of August 2026 closings, published September 18, 2026, found that 44.7% of U.S. home sales included a seller concession, defined as seller-paid closing costs, repairs, or a mortgage-rate buydown. That's up from 42.6% a year earlier, a 2.1 percentage point increase, and the highest August share Redfin has recorded since at least 2020. Concessions aren't a fringe tactic anymore; in close to half of all sales nationally, the seller gave the buyer something beyond the price itself to get the deal done.

The Los Angeles metro, which covers Woodland Hills, ran meaningfully above that national number: 56.2% of August sales here included a concession, essentially flat year over year (up 0.2 percentage points from 56.0%). For comparison, Redfin's data showed wide variation by metro, from San Jose at just 4.2% of sales up to Riverside at 58.0%. Los Angeles sits firmly in the higher-concession tier, closer to markets like San Diego (57.1%) and Sacramento (53.3%) than to the Bay Area metros.

Here's the detail that matters most for a seller trying to choose between a concession and a price cut: only 15.8% of August sales nationally combined a concession with a price reduction, barely changed from 15.6% a year earlier. In other words, offering a concession and cutting your price are mostly not the same sellers doing both. For most sellers in this data, the concession stood in for the price cut rather than stacking on top of it.

That distinction matters because the two levers work differently. A price cut lowers your number publicly, it's visible in the listing history, and it can signal to buyers that more room exists to negotiate further. A concession, structured correctly, keeps your list price intact while giving the buyer something concrete, typically help with closing costs or a temporary rate buydown that lowers their monthly payment in the early years of the loan. For a buyer facing today's rate environment, a rate buydown or closing cost credit can matter more to their monthly budget than a few points off the purchase price would.

None of this means a concession is automatically the better move, or that it's free. It comes out of your net proceeds just like a price cut does; the question is which one gets the deal done faster and costs you less once you run the actual numbers for your situation. Redfin's report gives prevalence, how often sellers are doing this, not an average dollar size, so there's no shortcut number to plug in here. The right comparison has to be specific to your price point, your buyer pool, and current rates.

Locally, Movoto's Woodland Hills page, updated October 27, 2026, shows 71 of the city's 379 active listings already price-reduced, a median sale price of $1,299,000 for September, and an average 50 days on market, up from 44 a year ago. Those figures are citywide, not broken out by whether a concession was offered; no public source tracks concession prevalence at the Woodland Hills or 91367 level the way Redfin tracks it by metro. If close to a quarter of active Woodland Hills listings are already sitting with a price reduction, it's a reasonable moment to ask whether a concession might have reached the same result without the public price cut.

Frequently Asked Questions

What counts as a seller concession?

Per Redfin's definition, seller-paid closing costs, repairs, or a mortgage-rate buydown, anything of value the seller gives the buyer beyond the sale price itself.

How common are seller concessions right now?

Redfin found 44.7% of U.S. home sales in August 2026 included one, up from 42.6% a year earlier, the highest August share since at least 2020.

Is Los Angeles above or below the national rate?

Above. Redfin's data shows 56.2% of August 2026 Los Angeles metro sales included a concession, versus 44.7% nationally.

Do most sellers offer a concession and cut the price?

No. Only 15.8% of August sales nationally combined a concession with a price reduction, suggesting most sellers used one lever or the other, not both.

Does Redfin say how much a typical concession costs the seller?

No. The report measures how often sellers offered a concession, not the average dollar amount, so there's no national average to apply directly to a specific sale.

Is a concession always better than a price cut?

Not automatically. Both come out of net proceeds. Which one works better depends on your price point, your likely buyer pool, and current rates, which is a real-numbers conversation, not a rule of thumb.
