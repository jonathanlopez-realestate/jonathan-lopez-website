Is My Zestimate Actually Accurate? What Woodland Hills Homeowners Should Know Before They Trust It

Jonathan Lopez, Woodland Hills Realtor | Jim Sandoval Group | CA DRE #02282844 · Updated July 2026

If you've pulled up your Woodland Hills home on Zillow and stared at the number wondering how close it really is, you're not alone, and there's a real, published answer, not just an agent's opinion.

What Zillow Itself Says About Its Own Accuracy

Zillow publishes its own Zestimate accuracy figures. For homes currently listed for sale, the median error rate runs about 2.4% (sources cite a range of roughly 1.74%-2.4%). But most homeowners aren't checking their value while actively listed. For homes that are off market, which is the situation for almost every homeowner just curious what their house is worth, the median error jumps to roughly 7.06%-7.49%.

What That Actually Means in Dollars for a Woodland Hills Home

At Woodland Hills' 91367 current median sale price of about $1.29M, a 7.06%-7.49% error range works out to roughly $90,000-$97,000 in either direction. That's not a small margin, and it can be the difference between pricing a home competitively and leaving money on the table, or overpricing and sitting on the market.

It gets more dramatic at the higher end. Woodland Hills' price range runs up toward $7M for larger and luxury properties, and independent research on automated valuation models shows error rates in the 10%-18% range specifically for luxury homes. On a $3M home, a 10% error is $300,000. On a $5M home, a 12% error is $600,000. The percentage looks modest. The dollar figure isn't. Even Zillow's own former CEO experienced this directly: in 2016 he sold his personal home for nearly 40% less than its Zestimate at the time.

Why the Gap Exists

An automated valuation model works from public records and comparable sales data, but it can't walk through your house. It doesn't know if your kitchen was renovated last year, whether your lot backs up to a busy street or a quiet cul-de-sac, or what's actually happening with the three homes that just went into contract on your specific street. A comparative market analysis (CMA), built by an agent using verified, recent, truly comparable sales, accounts for all of that.

A Zestimate is a fine starting point for idle curiosity. It is not something to make a pricing, listing, or selling decision around, especially in a market like Woodland Hills where the price range, and therefore the dollar-value error, is wide. Frequently Asked Questions

How accurate is a Zestimate really?

Zillow's own published data puts the median error at about 2.4% for homes currently listed and roughly 7.06%-7.49% for homes that are off market. Off-market is the number that applies to most homeowners just checking their value.

Why is the dollar error so much bigger on expensive Woodland Hills homes?

Percentage errors scale with price. A 7% error on a $1.29M home is around $90,000. That same style of error at the luxury end of Woodland Hills can mean $300,000-$600,000, because the underlying percentage doesn't shrink, the price it's applied to just gets bigger.

What's the actual difference between a Zestimate and a CMA?

A Zestimate is generated by an algorithm using public records and general comparable sales data, with no knowledge of your home's actual condition or specific street. A CMA is built by an agent using verified, recent, truly comparable sales and adjusts for condition, upgrades, and location.
