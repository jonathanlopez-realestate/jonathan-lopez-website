Does Waiting Cost Me Money? What the First Four Weeks on the Market Mean for Your Woodland Hills Listing

Jonathan Lopez, Jim Sandoval Group | Woodland Hills Realtor | CA DRE #02282844 · Updated October 2026

If you are about to list your Woodland Hills home, one of the first questions is simple: does it matter how fast it sells? The best recent data says time on market and final price move together.

What the national report found

Realtor.com's June 2026 sale-to-list report, covered by Inman on June 11, 2026, found that homes closing at or before the four-week mark sold for 1.8% more than homes that sold at the average days on market, which the article puts at 52. Homes on the market 18 weeks or longer sold for 1.3% less. Inman describes this as roughly a 3-point gap between the best and worst performers. The same coverage says price cuts are most common in the South and West, which Realtor.com calls buyer-friendly territory.

What that means in dollars

On a $1,300,000 list price, 1.8% is about $23,400 and 1.3% is about $16,900. Together that is a spread near $40,300. That is my arithmetic applied to a national average, not a forecast for your home. It also shows an association: homes that sell quickly are often the ones priced and prepared well from day one, so speed is partly a result, not only a cause.

Where Woodland Hills sits

Redfin's Woodland Hills page (data for August 2026) shows a median of 46 days on market, 231 homes sold, a 99.3% sale-to-list ratio, and 32.4% of homes with a price drop, roughly unchanged from a year earlier. Redfin's boundary may differ from the 91367 zip code.

How to use this when you list

First, start from recent sold comparables near you, since the opening price sets the pace of the first four weeks. Second, have photos, preparation and disclosures ready before the listing goes live, because the first weeks are when the most buyers are paying attention. Third, decide in advance what activity you expect in the first two to three weeks and what you will change if it does not show up. Fourth, remember that a price that works in week one is usually worth more than a bigger cut in week ten.
