Will I Have to Cut My Price If I List My Woodland Hills Home Right Now?

Jonathan Lopez, Woodland Hills Realtor | Jim Sandoval Group | CA DRE #02282844 · Updated July 2026

It's one of the first questions Woodland Hills sellers ask before they even sign a listing agreement: what if I price it wrong and end up cutting the price in front of everyone? It's a fair worry, and there's a real, current answer instead of a guess.

What's Actually Happening in the Market Right Now

As of Movoto's Woodland Hills data, last updated July 15, 2026, there are 426 active listings in the area, and 78 of them, about 1 in 5, already carry a price reduction. That's not a small number. It means roughly one out of every five sellers on the market today started at one number and had to walk it back.

Homes are currently selling in about 51 days on average, essentially flat compared to 52 days a year ago. So the pace of the market hasn't meaningfully sped up or slowed down. What hasn't changed either is buyer behavior: when a home is priced above where the actual comparable sales support it, buyers don't make offers, they wait. The home accumulates days on market, which buyers and their agents can see, and eventually the price comes down anyway, except now the listing is carrying a visible history of sitting unsold.

Why Correct Pricing Still Works

Houzeo's 2026 Woodland Hills market forecast puts the area's sale-to-list ratio at 98.76%, meaning homes are, on average, selling for close to their full asking price. That's not a market where sellers need to lowball themselves to attract interest. It's a market where a price grounded in real, recent comparable sales tends to hold. The same forecast puts months of supply at 1.21, which lines up with Woodland Hills sitting in a balanced market rather than one tilted heavily toward buyers or sellers.

The Actual Lesson in the Numbers

The 18% of listings sitting with a price reduction right now aren't necessarily in a bad market. They're very likely priced without a real comparative market analysis behind the number, or priced against hope instead of evidence. In a balanced market with a 98.76% sale-to-list ratio, the sellers getting close to full ask are the ones whose day-one price matched what buyers were already proving they'd pay for comparable homes nearby.

The risk of a price cut isn't really about the market being soft. It's about whether the starting number is backed by actual recent sales or by a guess. That's the difference a real CMA makes before a home ever goes live.
