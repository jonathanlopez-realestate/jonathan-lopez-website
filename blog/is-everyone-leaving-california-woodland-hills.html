Should I Worry About Selling in Woodland Hills If Everyone's Leaving California?

Jonathan Lopez, Woodland Hills Realtor | Jim Sandoval Group | CA DRE #02282844 · Updated August 2026

Clients relocating out of Los Angeles for a new job ask a version of this question almost every time: "I keep seeing that everyone is leaving California, does that mean my Woodland Hills home is going to sit on the market or sell for less?" It's a fair question. The migration numbers behind the headlines are real. What they actually measure is where the confusion starts.

What the Data Actually Shows

Redfin's Q1 2026 migration report, published June 29, 2026 and covering search activity through March 2026, ranks Los Angeles third among the top 100 U.S. metros for net outflow of house hunters, meaning 23,971 more Redfin.com users searched to leave the LA metro than searched to move in during the quarter. California ranked first among all states for outflow, at 54,767, roughly double New York's total, the next closest state. Among people leaving LA, San Diego is the top in-state destination and Las Vegas is the top out-of-state destination.

Nationally, 19.1% of house hunters looked to relocate to a different metro area in Q1 2026, the highest share since Redfin started tracking this in 2021, up slightly from 18.9% a year earlier.

What the Data Does Not Show

Redfin's own methodology defines a "migrant" as a user who views at least 20 homes in a destination metro within a one-month period. It's a measure of search behavior by people looking to leave, not a measure of buyer demand for the homes they leave behind. A rising outflow number tells you where current residents are looking next. It doesn't tell you how many buyers are actively shopping for a home in Woodland Hills right now, what they're willing to pay, or how fast homes here are actually moving.

For that, the more useful number is Woodland Hills' own current median sale price of $1,372,000 (Movoto, updated July 15, 2026), real, on-the-ground local data rather than a national search trend. No public migration data breaks out figures at the zip code level for west Woodland Hills (91367) specifically, this is Los Angeles metro and California statewide data, the smallest real geography publicly available for this question.

What This Means If You're Actually Relocating

A national or statewide outflow trend is not, by itself, evidence that your specific home will sell slower or for less. It's context, not a forecast. The number that actually predicts your outcome is a real, current comparative market analysis on your street, paired with a realistic timeline built around your actual moving date. Frequently Asked Questions

Does high outbound migration from California mean home values in Woodland Hills are dropping?

Not according to this data. The migration report measures search behavior of people leaving, not sale prices or buyer demand for homes here. Woodland Hills' own current median sale price is $1,372,000 (Movoto, updated July 15, 2026).

Where are most people leaving Los Angeles actually moving to?

Per Redfin's Q1 2026 report, San Diego is the top destination for LA leavers who stay in California, and Las Vegas is the top out-of-state destination.

Is there migration data specific to west Woodland Hills or the 91367 zip code?

No. Public migration data, including Redfin's report, is tracked at the metro and state level, not by zip code. Los Angeles metro and California statewide figures are the smallest real geography available for this question.
