Can First-Time Buyers in Woodland Hills Use Fannie Mae's HomeReady or Freddie Mac's Home Possible 3% Down Loan?

Jonathan Lopez, Woodland Hills Realtor · Jim Sandoval Group · CA DRE #02282844 · Updated November 2026

If you're a first-time buyer house-hunting in Woodland Hills, you've probably come across Fannie Mae's HomeReady and Freddie Mac's Home Possible loan programs in your research. Both let you put down as little as 3%, both offer reduced private mortgage insurance compared to a standard conventional loan, and both get marketed heavily toward first-time buyers. The part that matters most for a market like Woodland Hills, and the part that trips people up, is the income limit that comes attached to both programs.

Neither program is actually restricted to first-time buyers. Repeat buyers can use HomeReady or Home Possible too, as long as they meet the income requirement, and as long as they don't already hold financing on more than one other property. The real gatekeeper for both programs is that a borrower's qualifying income cannot exceed 80% of the area median income, commonly written as AMI, for the specific location of the home being purchased. That's a meaningfully different rule than a flat income cap that applies the same way everywhere. The limit is set by location, and it also varies with household size, since a one-person household and a four-person household have different AMI thresholds even for homes a block apart.

It's worth being direct about why this matters specifically in Woodland Hills. At today's median sale price of $1,300,000 (Movoto, data as of November 5, 2026), the buyers who can actually afford to compete for homes in this market often have household incomes well above the kind of 80%-of-AMI threshold these programs are built around. That doesn't make HomeReady or Home Possible useless here, lower-priced condos and townhomes in the area can still fit, and household size affects the limit meaningfully, but it does mean a lot of buyers who hear "3% down" and get excited need to check the actual income number for their specific household size and the specific property before assuming they qualify.

And that number genuinely has to be checked property by property. Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac each publish their own free online lookup tool where you enter a property address and household size and get back the exact income limit that applies. This isn't a shortcut around real research, it's the only accurate way to get the number, because the limit is tied to the property's census tract rather than the city or county as a whole. It's also worth knowing that Fannie Mae used to waive the income limit entirely for properties located in designated low-income census tracts, but removed that exception back in 2019. So today, the 80%-of-AMI cap applies no matter which part of Woodland Hills the home sits in; there's no neighborhood-based workaround to the income test anymore.

A couple of other mechanics are worth knowing if you do land inside the income limit. If every borrower who will actually occupy the home is a first-time buyer, at least one of them has to complete a homeownership education course, typically a few hours done online, before closing. And HomeReady specifically includes an extra benefit for buyers at the lower end of the income scale: a $2,500 credit toward the purchase when the qualifying income is at or below 50% of AMI and at least one borrower on the loan is a first-time buyer. On the mortgage insurance side, HomeReady's PMI is reduced compared to a standard conventional loan and becomes removable once you build meaningful equity, though published sources describe the exact cancellation trigger slightly differently (one cites automatic cancellation at 78% loan-to-value, another describes requesting removal at 20% equity with automatic cancellation at 22%), so confirm the specific terms with your lender rather than assuming either figure applies exactly to your loan.

None of this means HomeReady or Home Possible is off the table for a Woodland Hills purchase, especially for a smaller household, a lower-priced property type, or a buyer whose income genuinely sits in that 80%-of-AMI range. But given how far the city's median price sits from what these programs were designed around, the single most useful thing a first-time buyer can do before getting attached to the idea is run their actual household size and a specific address through Fannie Mae's or Freddie Mac's lookup tool, rather than assuming "3% down" automatically applies to them.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are HomeReady and Home Possible only for first-time buyers?

No. Both are open to first-time and repeat buyers. The real requirement is that qualifying household income stays at or below 80% of the area median income (AMI) for the home's specific location, and that the borrower doesn't already finance more than one other property.

Why does the income limit matter so much in Woodland Hills specifically?

Because the city's current median sale price is $1,300,000 (Movoto), the household incomes typical of buyers competing in this market often run well above the 80%-of-AMI threshold these two programs are built around, especially for smaller households.

Is the income limit the same everywhere in Los Angeles County?

No. It's set by the specific property's census tract and by household size together, not by one flat county or city number. Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac each publish a free lookup tool that returns the exact limit for a given address and household size.

Can I qualify in a lower-income neighborhood even if my income is higher?

Not anymore. Fannie Mae removed the exception that once waived the income limit entirely for properties in designated low-income census tracts; that change took effect in 2019, so the 80%-of-AMI cap now applies regardless of the property's location within Woodland Hills.

Do I have to take a class to use either program?

Only if every borrower who will occupy the home is a first-time buyer. In that case, at least one borrower has to complete a short homeownership education course before closing.

Is there extra help if my income is very low?

Yes, under HomeReady specifically. Buyers with qualifying income at or below 50% of AMI, with at least one first-time buyer on the loan, can receive a $2,500 credit toward the purchase.
