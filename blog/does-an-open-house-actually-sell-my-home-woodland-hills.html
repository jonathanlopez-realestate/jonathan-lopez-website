Does an Open House Actually Sell My Woodland Hills Home?

Jonathan Lopez, Woodland Hills Realtor | Jim Sandoval Group | CA DRE #02282844 · Updated September 2026

Almost every seller asks the same question before listing: should we hold an open house, or is it just a Sunday afternoon of strangers walking through with nothing to show for it? The real answer, backed by the National Association of REALTORS' own research, is more nuanced than either "definitely yes" or "definitely no," and understanding the nuance actually changes how you should think about your marketing plan.

The Discovery Numbers Are Genuinely Weak

According to NAR's Quick Real Estate Statistics, only about 4% of buyers found the home they purchased through a yard sign or open house sign. NAR's 2024 Home Buyers and Sellers Generational Trends Report adds another data point in the same direction: just 3% of buyers named an open house as the very first step in their home search. If the question is "will an open house be how a buyer discovers my listing," the honest data-backed answer is almost never.

But That's the Wrong Question

The same NAR research found that 50% of buyers attend at least one open house during their search, a completely different number telling a completely different story. Buyers overwhelmingly find listings online or through their agent first, and then use an open house as a low-pressure way to actually walk through and evaluate a property they already know about. An open house isn't a discovery tool. It's an evaluation tool that half of all buyers use somewhere in their process.

What This Means for Your Listing

The heavy lifting of getting a buyer to your door happens before any open house sign goes up: professional photography, an accurate and complete online listing, and getting your home in front of agents who are actively working with buyers who fit your home's price point and features. An open house doesn't replace that work, it comes after it, giving a buyer who already found you online or through their agent a convenient, no-appointment way to see the home in person. This is the same reason your list price needs to match how buyers actually search online, since the discovery step happens well before anyone walks through your front door.

The Other Real Value: Agent Exposure and Market Feedback

Open houses, especially a broker's open (a preview specifically for other agents rather than the public), give agents who represent active buyers a fast way to see your home without scheduling an individual showing. That matters because agents, not walk-in strangers, are the ones most likely to bring you an actual offer. A well-attended open house, public or broker's, also produces honest, in-person feedback on price and condition that a showing report or online analytics simply can't capture, useful information if your home isn't generating offers as quickly as expected.

Current Woodland Hills Context

As of this writing, there are 388 active listings and 20 scheduled open houses across Woodland Hills, with 79 of those active listings, roughly one in five, already price-reduced. In a market with this much active competition, giving serious buyers every reasonable way to see your home, including an open house, still has real value, even though the data is clear that it's rarely the first touchpoint that brought them to your address. Timing matters here too, similar to how seasonal listing timing affects buyer traffic, an open house tends to perform best when overall buyer activity is already elevated.

When an Open House Makes the Most Sense

Open houses tend to add the most value during the first weekend a home is listed, when buyer interest and online views are at their peak and agents are actively previewing new inventory. They can also be worth running again if a home has been on the market for a while without offers, both to re-generate buzz and to gather fresh, honest feedback on price or presentation. For unique or higher-end properties, an open house (or a broker's open specifically) can help a wider pool of agents become familiar with a home's layout and features, which matters when the eventual buyer is more likely to come through an agent referral than a public walk-in.

Frequently Asked Questions

What percentage of buyers actually find the home they purchase through an open house?

Only about 4%, according to NAR's own research, and just 3% named an open house as the first step in their search. Open houses are not typically how buyers discover a listing.

If open houses don't sell homes, why do agents still hold them?

Because "finding" a home and "evaluating" a home are different steps. NAR's research found that 50% of buyers attend at least one open house during their search, using it to walk through a property they already know about, plus open houses give other agents, who represent real buyers, a fast way to preview your listing.

Is there a difference between a public open house and a broker's open house?

Yes. A public open house is for prospective buyers. A broker's open is a preview specifically for other real estate agents, often held on a weekday, and can be more valuable for generating serious interest since it's agents, not walk-in visitors, who are statistically most likely to bring the eventual buyer.

What actually matters more than an open house for selling my home?

Professional photography, an accurate and complete online listing, and exposure to agents actively working with qualified buyers. These are what bring a buyer to your home in the first place; the open house is what happens after that.

When does an open house make the most sense for a Woodland Hills listing?

The first weekend after listing, when buyer interest is highest, and again later if a home has been on the market for a while without offers, both to regenerate interest and to gather honest in-person feedback on price and condition.

Should I skip the open house entirely if the data shows it rarely finds the buyer?

Not necessarily. With 388 active listings and 79 price-reduced homes currently competing for buyer attention in Woodland Hills, giving serious buyers every reasonable opportunity to see your home in person still has value, it's just not a substitute for strong online marketing and agent exposure.
