I'm Relocating to Woodland Hills With Kids. Can I Enroll Them in School Right Away, or Do I Have to Wait?

Jonathan Lopez, Woodland Hills Realtor · Jim Sandoval Group · CA DRE #02282844 · Updated November 2026

Families moving to Woodland Hills for a new job, a shorter commute, or just a change of scenery often ask a version of the same question once the house search gets serious: once we close or sign a lease, can our kids actually start school right away, or is there some enrollment window we need to plan our move around?

It's a reasonable worry. Plenty of districts around the country do run enrollment on a fixed calendar, so a family moving mid-year can end up stuck waiting weeks for a seat. LAUSD, the district covering Woodland Hills, works differently for most families, though there's a real wrinkle worth knowing before you assume anything.

Your own resident school: enroll any time

Any family living within LAUSD's boundaries can enroll a child at their own assigned neighborhood, or "resident," school at any point during the year. There's no need to wait for a semester break, a specific application period, or a particular calendar date just to start at the school your address is zoned for. If you close on a home or sign a lease in Woodland Hills in the middle of a school year, your kids can generally start at their boundary school right away, not months later.

What you'll actually need to show up with

The practical side of enrollment comes down to documentation, not timing. Expect to need proof of address, which LAUSD and area schools generally accept in the form of a recent gas or electric bill, a signed mortgage statement, or a lease agreement. That last part matters for relocating families specifically: you don't necessarily have to wait for a utility bill to arrive in your name before you can enroll, a closing document or signed lease can serve the same purpose. Beyond address proof, you'll typically need the child's birth certificate, current immunization records, and a parent's photo ID. If your child is coming from another school, a current report card or any applicable IEP paperwork helps the transition too.

Where the calendar actually does matter

The year-round rule above is specifically about your own resident, boundary-assigned school. If what you actually want is a different LAUSD school, through the district's separate K-12 Open Enrollment choice program (a magnet, a dual-language program, or simply a school outside your home's boundary), that runs on a real, fixed calendar: on-time applications open in mid-April and close in mid-May, with a later application window available after that for families who miss the first one. If choice-school access matters to your relocation plans, that calendar is worth building into your home search and move timeline, since it doesn't bend the way resident enrollment does.

One thing worth checking before you write an offer

Attendance boundaries in LAUSD are address-specific, not neighborhood-wide. Two homes a few blocks apart, both reasonably described as "Woodland Hills," can be zoned for different elementary, middle, or high schools. If a specific school is part of why you're moving to this area, confirm the exact assigned school for a home's actual address before making an offer, rather than assuming based on the broader neighborhood's reputation or a listing description.

The local market context

Movoto's Woodland Hills page, updated November 5, 2026, shows a $1,300,000 median sale price (down 3.6% year over year), 368 active listings, 37 new listings in the past week, and homes averaging 54 days on market. With that much inventory moving at any given time, there's real room to be selective about matching a home to the right school boundary rather than rushing a decision.

The bottom line for relocating families

If you're moving to Woodland Hills with school-age kids, the standard worry (having to wait weeks to enroll) usually isn't the real obstacle, enrollment at your own resident school can typically start right away with the right paperwork. The thing actually worth planning around is confirming which school a specific home is zoned for, and, if a choice program matters to your family, the separate April-May application window that governs it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I have to wait for a specific date to enroll my kids after moving to Woodland Hills?

Not for your own assigned neighborhood school. LAUSD allows any family living within district boundaries to enroll at their resident school at any time of year, not just at semester breaks or a fixed application date.

What documents do I need to enroll a child after relocating?

Typically proof of address (a recent gas or electric bill, a signed mortgage statement, or a lease agreement), the child's birth certificate, current immunization records, and a parent's photo ID. A previous school's report card or IEP paperwork helps if applicable.

Can I enroll before my utility bills switch into my name?

Based on the accepted proof-of-address list, a signed mortgage statement or lease agreement can serve the same purpose as a utility bill, so a closing document alone may be enough to start the process. Confirm the accepted documents directly with your specific assigned school, since individual schools can vary slightly.

Is there ever a fixed deadline I need to worry about?

Yes, but only if you want a school other than your own resident boundary school. LAUSD's Open Enrollment choice program (magnets, dual-language programs, or out-of-boundary schools) runs on a fixed calendar: on-time applications mid-April through mid-May, with a later window after that.

How do I know which school a home is actually zoned for?

LAUSD attendance boundaries are address-specific, not neighborhood-wide, so two homes in the same general area can be zoned for different schools. Confirm the exact assigned school for a specific address before writing an offer if school assignment is a priority.

Where can I verify the current rules before I move?

Directly with your target home's specific assigned school, or at enroll.lausd.net.
