Should I Accept an Offer From a Buyer Who Has to Sell Their Home First?

Jonathan Lopez, Jim Sandoval Group | Woodland Hills Realtor | CA DRE #02282844 · Updated October 2026

If an offer on your Woodland Hills home comes with a condition that the buyer must first sell their own home, it can look appealing on price and risky on timing. Here is how to think about it.

What the Contingency Means for You

Redfin's contingency guide explains that with a home sale contingency, the seller receives an offer that depends on the buyer's current home selling. The seller bears the risk if that sale falls through, including losing time on the market. Redfin notes these deals typically run 30 to 60 days and that sellers generally prefer non-contingent offers because they close faster with less risk. The guide has no publication date, so treat the specifics as general.

How Often Deals Fall Apart

Redfin's analysis of contingent offers (published July 31, 2026) found that 13.6% of pending U.S. home sales fell out of contract before closing, roughly 1 in 7. Among the reasons it lists, inspection or repair issues came up in 70.4% of cancellations, buyer financing problems in 27.8%, the buyer's existing home not selling in 21.0%, and changes in the buyer's financial situation in 14.9%. The percentages add to more than 100% because a deal can have more than one reason. These are national figures, not Woodland Hills data.

The Kick-Out Clause

Redfin describes a kick-out clause as allowing the seller to keep showing the home and accept another offer while the buyer's contingency is in place. If a better offer arrives, the first buyer typically has 24 to 72 hours to remove the contingency or step aside. Redfin also says pre-listing inspections and accurate pricing help prevent cancellations. See whether a pre-listing inspection makes sense for your home.

What Woodland Hills Timing Looks Like

Movoto's Woodland Hills page (updated October 5, 2026) shows 428 active listings, 76 price-reduced, and 50 days on market compared with 44 a year earlier. A buyer who needs to sell a home in this market could take well over a month to do it, and that wait becomes your wait unless a kick-out clause protects you. This is reasoning from the local numbers, not a prediction for any specific buyer.

How to Decide

Compare the contingent offer against your next best realistic offer on price, closing date, and risk. Ask whether the buyer's home is already listed, priced realistically, and under contract. Ask for a kick-out clause and a firm deadline. Then decide whether the premium on the table covers the added risk. For the broader question, see whether you should take the first offer.

This is general information, not legal advice. Contingency terms are set in the purchase contract. Review any offer with your agent and consult an attorney for legal questions.
