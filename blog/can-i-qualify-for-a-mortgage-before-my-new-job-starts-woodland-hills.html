Can I Qualify for a Mortgage in Woodland Hills Before My New Job Actually Starts?

Jonathan Lopez, Woodland Hills Realtor · Jim Sandoval Group · CA DRE #02282844 · Updated November 2026

If you are relocating to Woodland Hills for a new job that has not started yet, the mortgage question that stops most people cold is simple: how does a lender qualify income from a job you do not currently have? For years the practical answer was "wait for your first paystub," which could mean sitting out the local market for months after a move. That is no longer the only path, at least for conventional financing.

What Fannie Mae's rule actually allows. Fannie Mae's Selling Guide, Section B3-3.3-03, "Employment Offers or Contracts," lets a lender use income from a future job to qualify a conventional borrower, provided the offer is fully executed and non-contingent, meaning both the employer and the borrower have signed it and nothing (a background check, a licensing exam, a department budget approval) is still pending. The offer letter has to state the position, the pay rate, and the start date in plain terms. As of the October 7, 2026 update to this rule, the start date itself has to fall within a defined window: no earlier than 30 days before the loan's note date, and no later than 90 days after it. A borrower starting a new role four months from now does not qualify under this provision; one starting within that 30-day-before to 90-day-after window does.

The lender still checks the offer is real. This is not a case of simply handing over a letter and walking away with a loan. The lender is required to independently verify the offer, either through a verbal verification of employment or written confirmation from the employer that the terms have not changed, especially when the start date falls on or after the note date itself. If the job terms shift (a lower starting salary, a pushed-back start date, a withdrawn offer) between application and closing, the loan is no longer supported by the original documentation.

Two different documentation paths. Fannie Mae gives lenders two ways to handle the gap between "no paycheck yet" and "loan closes now." Under the first option, the lender obtains the borrower's most recent paystub before the loan is delivered, which usually means the new job has already started and produced at least one pay period by the time the loan actually funds or shortly after. Under the second option, no paystub is required before delivery at all, but the loan has to be delivered carrying a specific tracking code, and the borrower has to document real financial cushion instead: either six months of PITIA (principal, interest, taxes, insurance, and any association dues) in verified reserves, or enough documented resources, savings plus any current income still coming in, to cover every monthly obligation including PITIA for each month between the note date and the new job's start date, plus one additional month beyond that for a safety margin.

Why this matters for a Woodland Hills relocation specifically. A lot of relocation moves to the west San Fernando Valley follow a job offer by weeks, not months, and buyers often want to close on a home before the new role's actual first day so they are not paying for temporary housing on top of a mortgage. This rule exists for exactly that situation, letting a documented, real offer stand in for pay history within a defined window, rather than forcing a buyer to either wait out a vacancy or find a way to qualify on savings and old income alone.

What this rule does not cover. This provision lives inside Fannie Mae's conventional-loan guidelines. FHA, VA, and jumbo loans are underwritten under separate guides with their own treatment of future employment income, and none of them are required to mirror Fannie Mae's exact 30-day/90-day window or reserve math. A relocating buyer planning to use an FHA or VA loan, or a jumbo loan above conforming limits, should ask their specific lender how that program treats an unstarted job before assuming this rule applies the same way.

The practical takeaway. If you have a real, signed offer letter in hand and a start date that lands within that 30-day-before to 90-day-after window around your expected closing date, it is worth asking a lender directly which of the two documentation paths fits your situation, rather than assuming you need to wait for a paystub to even start the conversation. Reserve requirements under the no-paystub option are real and have to be independently verified, so this is not a shortcut around proving financial stability, it is a different, equally documented way of proving it.

On today's broader Woodland Hills market (Movoto, citywide: $1,300,000 median sale price, 368 active listings, 54-day average days on market), inventory and timing both matter for how tightly a relocation purchase needs to line up with a new job's start date, which is one more reason this conversation is worth having with a lender and an agent together, not separately.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I get a mortgage in Woodland Hills before my new job actually starts?

Yes, for conventional financing, if you have a fully executed, non-contingent offer letter and your start date falls no earlier than 30 days before your loan's note date or no later than 90 days after it, under Fannie Mae's rule updated October 7, 2026.

Does the lender just take my offer letter at face value?

No. The lender independently verifies the offer is still accurate, either through a verbal verification of employment or written confirmation from the employer, especially if your start date falls on or after the note date.

Do I need a paystub before I close?

Not necessarily. One path requires a paystub before the loan is delivered. The other skips that requirement but requires you to document six months of PITIA reserves, or enough resources to cover your payments from the note date through your start date plus one extra month.

Does this rule apply to FHA or VA loans too?

Not automatically. This specific 30-day/90-day window and reserve structure comes from Fannie Mae's conventional-loan guide. FHA, VA, and jumbo loans are governed separately and may treat a future job offer differently, so confirm with your lender based on the loan type you are using.

What happens if my start date changes or the offer falls through before closing?

The loan is no longer supported by the documentation the lender originally verified. Tell your lender and your agent immediately if anything about the offer changes, timing, pay, or the offer itself, since it directly affects whether the loan can still close as structured.

Is this the same as the relocation lump-sum coverage you have written about before?

No. That earlier post covered whether a relocation package's lump-sum payment covers closing costs. This is a separate question about whether your future paycheck itself can be used to qualify for the loan in the first place.
