Does My Woodland Hills Luxury Listing Need Drone Video, or Will Photos Still Sell It?

Jonathan Lopez, Woodland Hills Realtor · Jim Sandoval Group · CA DRE #02282844 · Updated November 2026

A question that comes up often from Woodland Hills sellers pricing above the area's median: is drone video actually worth the extra cost and coordination, or is a solid set of professional photos still enough to market a higher-end home?

It's a fair question, since not long ago drone footage really was an optional extra reserved for the most expensive listings. That's changed.

How common drone video actually is now

According to NAR's 2026 REALTORS Technology Survey, 48% of real estate agents nationally now use drone photography or video in their business. That ranks it the fifth most-used technology tool among agents, behind MLS access (96%), e-signature platforms (79%), showing-schedule software (68%), and CMA/pricing tools (59%). The year before, in NAR's 2025 survey, adoption stood at 52%, a small dip rather than a rise, which actually reinforces the point: this isn't a tool still climbing toward mainstream use, it has already arrived there and settled in as a standard part of how a meaningful share of agents market listings.

What that looks like specifically at the luxury tier

Coldwell Banker Global Luxury's 2026 marketing guidance, aimed specifically at agents working above-median listings, describes a now-standard package: twilight exterior photography, drone footage, a two-to-three-minute cinematic video tour, and vertical cuts of that same video edited for social media. Notably, that guidance doesn't frame these as optional upgrades a seller can choose to skip to save money. It frames them as the baseline a luxury listing is expected to show up with.

That matters for a practical reason: buyers shopping above the median aren't just comparing your home to itself, they're comparing it to every other listing in the same price band, and most of those competing listings already include this package. A home marketed with ground-level photos alone, however good those photos are, can end up looking under-marketed by comparison, even if the home itself is excellent.

What drone video actually adds that photos can't

Aerial footage does a few things ground-level photography simply can't: it shows lot size and boundaries clearly, situates the home relative to views, hillside positioning, or privacy from neighbors (all relevant in a hillside market like Woodland Hills), and gives buyers a sense of the property's scale and surroundings before they ever schedule a showing. A two-to-three-minute cinematic tour combining aerial and interior footage also gives a listing something to actually post and share on social media, where a static photo gallery doesn't travel nearly as well.

Being honest about what the data doesn't say

A few marketing-industry sources claim drone video makes homes sell a specific percentage faster or for a specific price premium. Those figures couldn't be traced back to any named study, MLS dataset, or primary research source during this review, so they're deliberately left out here. What's verifiable is adoption: nearly half of all agents now use this tool, and the luxury-marketing guidance from a major national brokerage network treats it as standard rather than optional. That's a real signal about buyer expectations, even without a precise sale-speed statistic attached to it.

The local picture

Movoto's Woodland Hills page, updated November 5, 2026, shows a $1,300,000 median sale price (down 3.6% year over year), 368 active listings, 37 new listings in the past week, and homes averaging 54 days on market, down slightly from 56 a year ago. With that much active competition at any given time, a listing's first impression, especially the one buyers see online before ever requesting a showing, carries real weight.

The bottom line for Woodland Hills sellers

Professional ground photography still matters and isn't being replaced. But at the luxury tier, it's increasingly treated as the first layer of a marketing package, not the whole package. If your home is priced meaningfully above the area median, drone video and a short cinematic tour are worth budgeting for as standard, not as an optional extra to consider only if there's room left in the marketing budget.
