I'm Retiring and Relocating to Woodland Hills. Can I Qualify for a Mortgage Without a Paycheck?

Jonathan Lopez, Woodland Hills Realtor · Jim Sandoval Group · CA DRE #02282844 · Updated November 2026

A lot of people planning a retirement move to Woodland Hills assume that without a paycheck, a mortgage is off the table. That assumption is outdated. Both of the agencies that set the rules most lenders follow, Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac, have long allowed borrowers to qualify using "asset depletion," a method that turns liquid savings and investments into a monthly income figure for underwriting purposes, with no employment income required at all.

The two agencies calculate it differently, and the difference matters. Fannie Mae's method divides your net eligible assets, generally retirement accounts, brokerage accounts, and similar liquid holdings, by the loan's full amortization term in months. For a standard 30 year loan, that's 360 months. Fannie Mae also caps the loan to value ratio at 70% when relying on asset depletion, but raises that to 80% if the owner of the qualifying assets is 62 or older at closing, a meaningful break for most retirees.

Freddie Mac takes a more generous approach today, dividing net eligible assets by 240 months instead of 360, which produces a noticeably higher monthly qualifying income from the same pool of assets. And the rules are about to get more generous still: Freddie Mac's Bulletin 2026-10 shortens that divisor again, to 180 months, a change sellers can adopt ahead of a mandatory date of February 3, 2027. That same bulletin also removes Freddie Mac's prior age-based requirement, so the more favorable divisor won't depend on the borrower's age going forward.

The numbers make the difference concrete. Take $1.5 million in net eligible assets as an illustration, not a specific borrower's figures. Under Fannie Mae's 360 month divisor, that produces about $4,167 a month in qualifying income. Under Freddie Mac's current 240 month divisor, the same $1.5 million produces about $6,250 a month. Once Freddie Mac's 180 month divisor takes full effect, that number rises again, to roughly $8,333 a month. Which agency's rules apply depends on which loan program and lender you use, which is exactly why this is a conversation to have with a lender before you start touring homes, not after you've made an offer.

Asset depletion income doesn't have to carry the whole weight of qualifying on its own, either. Most retirees relocating here are also drawing Social Security, and often a pension. Social Security income is typically not subject to federal income tax, and lenders can generally "gross up" non taxable income for qualifying purposes, treating it as somewhat higher than its stated dollar amount to reflect the fact that it isn't taxed the way wage income is. The exact percentage used for that adjustment varies by lender and by how much documentation you provide, so rather than quote a single number here, the honest answer is: ask your lender directly what adjustment they'll apply to your specific Social Security benefit. Combined with asset depletion income, Social Security and any pension income often get a retiree well past what either source could support alone.

None of this changes the basic mechanics of buying in Woodland Hills. In the 91367 zip code right now, the median home price is around $1,199,000, with roughly 212 homes actively listed and an average of about 41 days on market. A retiree using asset depletion is shopping in the same market, against the same comps, as any W-2 buyer, which is exactly why knowing your real qualifying number, not a rough guess, matters before you start looking seriously.

There's a practical sequencing point worth knowing too: not every lender offers asset depletion loans, and not every loan officer at a given lender is equally fluent in structuring one correctly. Getting pre-qualified with a lender who actually does this regularly, rather than one encountering it for the first time on your file, tends to produce a smoother and more accurate number the first time around.

This is general information about underwriting guidelines, not a loan commitment or financial advice. Exact qualifying income depends on your specific assets, documentation, loan program, and lender, and should be confirmed directly with a mortgage professional before you make decisions based on it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I get a mortgage in retirement if I have no job and no W-2 income?

Yes. Both Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac allow "asset depletion" qualifying, which converts liquid retirement assets into a monthly income figure for underwriting, with no employment income required.

How is asset depletion income actually calculated?

Fannie Mae divides your net eligible assets by the loan's amortization term in months, 360 for a 30 year loan. Freddie Mac currently uses a shorter 240 month divisor, which produces a higher monthly figure from the same assets, and is moving to a 180 month divisor under Bulletin 2026-10 ahead of a mandatory February 3, 2027 date.

Does my age affect how much I can qualify for?

Under Fannie Mae's rules, yes: the maximum loan to value ratio using asset depletion rises from 70% to 80% if the asset owner is 62 or older at closing. Freddie Mac's Bulletin 2026-10 removes its prior age-based requirement entirely.

Does Social Security income help me qualify too?

Generally yes. Social Security is typically non taxable, and lenders can often "gross up" that income for qualifying purposes. The exact adjustment varies by lender and documentation, so confirm the specific number with your lender rather than assuming a fixed percentage.

How much mortgage could $1.5 million in assets actually support?

As an illustration only, $1.5 million in net eligible assets produces roughly $4,167 a month in qualifying income under Fannie Mae's method, or roughly $6,250 a month under Freddie Mac's current method. Your actual figure depends on your specific assets, loan program, and lender.

Should I get pre-qualified before I start touring Woodland Hills homes?

Yes, especially with asset depletion financing. Not every lender structures these loans the same way, and starting with a lender who handles them regularly tends to produce a more accurate number before you're competing on an offer.
