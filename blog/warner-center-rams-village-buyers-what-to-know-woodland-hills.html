What Buyers Near Warner Center Should Actually Know About the Rams Village Project

Jonathan Lopez, Woodland Hills Realtor | Jim Sandoval Group | CA DRE #02282844 · Updated September 2026

If you've driven along Topanga Canyon Boulevard or Owensmouth Avenue lately, you've seen it: the old Promenade mall, empty for years, is being torn down. Demolition isn't a rumor or a rendering, it's a physical fact happening right now in west Woodland Hills. What replaces it, and what that means if you're buying nearby, is a genuinely different question, and it deserves a straight answer instead of either hype or worry.

What's Already Built, Not Just Planned

It helps to separate two things that get talked about as one project. The Los Angeles Rams' own headquarters and practice facility sits on part of the 52-acre core site, the old Promenade plus the adjacent former Anthem office tower, and has been open and in active use since August 2024. That part isn't speculative. The team practices there, the building exists, and it's not going anywhere.

The much bigger piece, what's now being called "Rams Village," is different. Rams owner Stan Kroenke first unveiled that broader vision in April 2025: a roughly $10 billion mixed-use plan for the site, described in reporting as including residential units, retail space, office space, a hotel, and two entertainment venues, one seating around 5,000, a smaller one around 2,500. That part is still moving through the city's planning and entitlement process. The specific building heights, exact unit counts, and construction timeline you might read in an article aren't locked in yet, and could still shift before final approval.

What's Actually Happening on the Ground Right Now

According to Valley News Group, a local Woodland Hills paper, demolition permits for the old Promenade structure were issued in December 2025, and soft demolition, removing interior fixtures and utilities before the exterior work begins, started in January 2026. Kroenke officials told the paper they expected full site clearance by spring 2026. The P.F. Chang's restaurant on the property stayed open through the demolition process, a small detail that tells you this is happening in phases, not all at once.

Beyond the demolition itself, broader reporting on the project describes a total footprint of roughly 5.2 million square feet, including upwards of 3,000 housing units and close to 2 million square feet of retail, office, and hotel space, once the full plan is built out. Construction of the permanent, non-team buildings has been reported as targeted to start around 2027, with the full phased buildout extending potentially into the mid-2030s. That's a decade-long horizon, not a next-year change.

What This Means If You're Buying Nearby

Here's where secondhand chatter gets it wrong in both directions. Some people assume a huge development coming means a future home's value will jump. Others assume it means years of noise and disruption that will hurt livability and resale. The truth is nobody, not the developer, not the city, not any outside analyst, has published an actual value study tied to this specific project. Treat both the optimistic and pessimistic version the same way, as an unproven claim, not a fact to price into an offer.

What is fact-checkable, and worth actually checking before writing an offer on a home near Warner Center, comes down to a few concrete questions. How far is this specific property from the active construction perimeter, not the neighborhood in general, but the actual parcel boundary? What phase is the project in right now, since site clearance in progress and vertical construction starting are very different experiences to live next to? And realistically, if construction noise, dust, or truck traffic during the demolition and early build years would bother you day to day, that's worth weighing on its own, separate from any theory about what the finished project might eventually do to prices a decade from now. It's a similar due-diligence habit to checking how a nearby development's actual status, not the rumor about it, changes the local supply picture.

Why This Is a Buyer Representation Question, Not a Guessing Game

This is exactly the kind of thing a buyer's agent should be checking for you rather than leaving you to piece together from headlines and outdated articles. Pulling the property's actual distance to the site, checking the most current permit and construction status, which changes month to month right now, and giving an honest, unhyped read on what living near an active decade-long construction zone is actually like, that's basic due diligence, not a nice-to-have. It's also why having your own representation matters even on a home that isn't directly next to the project: half-true assumptions about a high-profile development like this spread fast, and you want someone checking the actual current facts on your specific address, not the general neighborhood narrative.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the old Promenade mall actually being torn down right now?

Yes. Demolition permits were issued in December 2025, and soft demolition (removing interior fixtures and utilities) began in January 2026, according to Valley News Group. Full site clearance was expected by spring 2026.

Is the Rams Village mixed-use project fully approved by the city?

The Rams' own headquarters and practice facility is already built and in use. The broader mixed-use master plan (housing, retail, hotel, entertainment venues) was still moving through the city planning and entitlement process as of the most recent reporting, so specifics could still change before final approval.

Will this project raise property values nearby?

No independent value study has been published tied to this specific project. Don't treat either "it will raise values" or "it will hurt them" as settled fact, it's speculation either way at this stage.

When would construction actually be finished?

Reported estimates put the start of permanent (non-team) building construction around 2027, with a full phased buildout that could extend roughly a decade, into the mid-2030s. That's a long horizon, and individual phases will look very different from each other.

Is the Rams' own facility still under construction, adding to the disruption?

No. The Rams headquarters and practice facility has been open and in active use since August 2024. It's the surrounding mixed-use redevelopment (old mall site) that's currently in the demolition phase.
