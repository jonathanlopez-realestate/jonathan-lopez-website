What Closing Costs Will I Actually Pay as a Buyer in Woodland Hills?

Jonathan Lopez, Woodland Hills Realtor | Jim Sandoval Group | CA DRE #02282844 · Updated September 2026

Most first-time buyers plan carefully for the down payment and then get a real surprise at the closing table: a second, separate bill that isn't part of the purchase price at all. Closing costs are real money due on top of whatever you're putting down, and in Woodland Hills, where the median sale price sits well above the statewide average, the dollar amounts involved are worth understanding well before you write an offer.

The Overall Range

California buyer closing costs typically total 2% to 5% of the purchase price. Applied to Woodland Hills' current median sale price of $1,348,500, that's roughly $27,000 to $67,000, a wide range because it depends heavily on your specific loan amount, lender, and how the purchase contract allocates costs between you and the seller.

Lender Fees Are Usually the Largest Category

The loan origination fee alone typically runs 0.5% to 1% of your loan amount. On top of that, expect underwriting fees in the $300 to $900 range, a credit report fee of $30 to $50, and document preparation charges. Because lender fees vary meaningfully between institutions, getting Loan Estimates from two or three lenders before choosing one is one of the few parts of this process a buyer can actually shop and reduce.

Title Insurance, But Only the Lender's Half in Most Cases

Buyers with a mortgage always pay for the lender's title insurance policy, typically 0.2% to 0.5% of the loan amount. This is separate from the owner's title insurance policy, which protects the buyer's own ownership interest rather than the lender's collateral.

The Regional Detail That Catches People Off Guard

In Southern California, including all of Los Angeles County, the seller customarily pays for the owner's title insurance policy, not the buyer. In Northern California, that custom flips and the buyer typically pays it instead. This isn't set by law, it's regional custom that the purchase contract can override either way, but out-of-state buyers who've researched California closing costs generally often assume they'll owe the owner's title premium here and are pleasantly surprised when local custom has the seller covering it. This is a similar geography lesson to how appraisal gap coverage works, where knowing the local mechanics ahead of time avoids a surprise mid-transaction.

Escrow Fees, Split Down the Middle

California escrow companies generally charge a base fee (roughly $250 to $450) plus $2 to $3 per $1,000 of the purchase price, split 50/50 between buyer and seller by default. On a Woodland Hills home at the current median, that per-thousand rate alone adds up to nearly $2,700 in escrow charges before the base fee, with each side typically covering about half.

The Other Real Costs: Appraisal, Inspection, and Prepaids

Budget $500 to $800 for the appraisal and $400 to $600 for a home inspection, both standard steps in a financed purchase. Beyond those, prepaid expenses (not fees for a service, but costs paid in advance) round out the total: a full year of homeowner's insurance paid upfront, daily prepaid interest from your closing date to the end of the month, and an initial deposit into your lender's impound account covering 2 to 6 months of future property taxes and insurance. These prepaids often surprise buyers more than any single fee because they're substantial but easy to overlook when budgeting from a simple percentage estimate. This is separate from your earnest money deposit, which is credited toward your purchase at closing rather than being an additional cost.

None of This Is Fixed by California Law

Unlike the documentary transfer tax, which is set by statute, most closing costs are governed by regional custom and whatever the purchase contract actually says. That means in a slower market, a buyer can sometimes negotiate a seller credit toward closing costs as part of the offer, while in a competitive market, that kind of concession becomes much harder to get. Knowing the going market conditions before writing an offer matters as much as knowing the cost categories themselves.

What to Do Before Writing an Offer

Get a Loan Estimate from your lender early. It itemizes every lender-related cost so there are no surprises on that side. Then ask about a preliminary closing cost estimate from a title and escrow company for your specific price range and loan type, since the escrow fee and title premiums scale directly with purchase price. Once you have real numbers instead of a percentage range, we can build them into what you're actually comfortable offering on a Woodland Hills home.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much should I actually budget for closing costs on a Woodland Hills home?

Using the current $1,348,500 median sale price and the statewide 2% to 5% range, budget roughly $27,000 to $67,000, separate from your down payment. Your lender's Loan Estimate will narrow that to an exact number for your specific loan.

Do I have to pay for the owner's title insurance policy as a buyer here?

Generally no. In Los Angeles County and the rest of Southern California, sellers customarily pay for the owner's title insurance policy. You'll still pay for the lender's title insurance policy if you're financing the purchase.

What's the single biggest closing cost category for a buyer?

Lender fees, typically. The loan origination fee alone runs 0.5% to 1% of your loan amount, and that's before underwriting, credit report, and document preparation fees are added.

Can I negotiate who pays what?

Yes. Most closing costs in California are set by regional custom, not law, and the purchase contract controls the actual allocation. Sellers sometimes offer a closing cost credit in slower markets; it's less common in competitive ones.

What are prepaid expenses, and why do they surprise buyers?

Prepaids are costs paid in advance for ongoing homeownership rather than one-time fees: a year of homeowner's insurance, daily interest through the end of your closing month, and an initial property tax and insurance impound deposit. They add up to a real amount that a simple closing cost percentage estimate doesn't always make obvious upfront.

How do I get an exact number instead of a range?

Request a Loan Estimate from your lender within three business days of applying, and ask a title and escrow company for a preliminary estimate based on your target price and county. Together those replace the range with real figures for your specific purchase.
