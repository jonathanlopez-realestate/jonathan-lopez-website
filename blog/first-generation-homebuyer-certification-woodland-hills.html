Does Getting Certified as a "First-Generation Homebuyer" Actually Save Me Money in Woodland Hills?

Jonathan Lopez, Woodland Hills Realtor · Jim Sandoval Group · CA DRE #02282844 · Updated November 2026

A Real Certification Most Buyers Have Never Heard Of

If you're buying your first home in Woodland Hills, you may qualify for a specific status Fannie Mae calls a "first-generation homebuyer." It's a real, documented category in Fannie Mae's Selling Guide (Topic B2-2-07, tracing to Announcement SEL-2024-03, effective May 1, 2024, and still current in the Guide as published November 4, 2026). But before getting excited, it's worth understanding exactly what this certification does and does not do, because the two get confused constantly.

Who Actually Qualifies

Per Fannie Mae's Selling Guide, a loan qualifies as a first-generation homebuyer loan when every borrower on it meets all of the following:

- Is purchasing the property and will live in it as their principal residence - Has had no ownership interest, sole or joint, in any property during the three years before the loan's note date - And, on top of that, at least one of the following also applies: no parent of the borrower has had ownership interest in a property during that same three-year window, the borrower aged out of the foster care system, or the borrower was legally emancipated

In practice, this is aimed at buyers who are genuinely the first in their family, in a real, recent sense, to have a shot at homeownership, not just buyers who happen to be purchasing for the first time themselves.

The Paperwork: Form 1109

If you believe you qualify, each borrower on the loan completes Fannie Mae's First-Generation Homebuyer Certification, Form 1109. The lender is required to keep a signed copy in your loan file. This is the mechanism that actually creates the record, simply believing you qualify isn't enough on its own.

The Part Most Buyers Get Wrong

Here's where expectations and reality often diverge. Per the Selling Guide, identifying a loan as a first-generation homebuyer loan is currently optional for lenders. A lender that chooses to flag it may report a specific delivery code (SFC 886) when selling the loan to Fannie Mae, but doing so requires every borrower's certification to already be on file. Nothing in the Guide ties this certification itself to an automatic interest rate reduction or a cash credit at closing. A direct search of FHFA's own website for "first-generation homebuyer" turned up no published results describing a universal pricing benefit tied to this specific designation as of this writing, so this post is not going to tell you it guarantees savings, because that claim isn't supported by what's actually published right now.

What the certification does do is create documentation that can matter if your specific lender, or a specific program you're applying through, offers something tied to first-generation status. Policies at that level change, and some state and local programs do target first-generation buyers specifically. The move is to ask your loan officer directly: does this lender recognize first-generation homebuyer status, and does it change anything about my rate, my fees, or my eligibility for any down payment assistance program?

Where the Real Money Usually Comes From

If you're a first-time buyer in Woodland Hills, the programs most likely to actually put money in your pocket are the ones that already carry a defined benefit: CalHFA down payment assistance (subject to income limits), a Mortgage Credit Certificate for an annual federal tax credit, or a Fannie Mae HomeReady or Freddie Mac Home Possible loan allowing as little as 3% down with income limits based on your property's census tract. Being certified as a first-generation homebuyer doesn't replace any of these, but it's worth mentioning to your lender when you apply for them, since it may be a factor in how your file gets reviewed internally even where no blanket public benefit is published.

Bottom Line

First-generation homebuyer status is a real, Fannie Mae-defined certification with a specific three-part test and a specific form. It's worth asking about if you think you qualify. Just don't assume the label itself writes you a check. The actual savings, when they exist, come from whatever specific program you stack it with, and the only way to know what's on the table is to ask your lender directly and in writing.

Frequently Asked Questions

What exactly is a "first-generation homebuyer" for mortgage purposes?

Per Fannie Mae's Selling Guide (B2-2-07), it's a borrower who is purchasing a home to live in as their principal residence, has had no ownership interest in any property in the prior three years, and either has a parent with no ownership interest in that same three-year window, aged out of foster care, or was legally emancipated.

How do I actually get certified as a first-generation homebuyer?

Each borrower on the loan completes Fannie Mae's Form 1109, the First-Generation Homebuyer Certification, and the lender keeps the signed form in the loan file.

Does this certification automatically lower my interest rate or give me a credit?

Not by itself, based on what's currently published. Identifying a loan this way is optional for lenders, and no published FHFA or Fannie Mae policy ties the certification alone to a guaranteed rate reduction or cash credit. Any savings typically come from a separate program you qualify for and stack it with.

Which programs should I actually ask about if I think I'm a first-generation buyer?

Ask about CalHFA down payment assistance, a Mortgage Credit Certificate, and Fannie Mae HomeReady or Freddie Mac Home Possible 3%-down loans. These have defined, published benefits; the first-generation certification on its own does not.

Can I still buy in Woodland Hills if I don't meet the first-generation definition?

Yes. This is one narrow certification among many first-time buyer tools. Plenty of buyers who don't meet this specific three-year/parental-ownership test still qualify for other down payment assistance, tax credit, or low-down-payment programs.

Who verifies whether I actually qualify?

Your lender does, through the Form 1109 certification process and their own underwriting review. If you're not sure whether you meet the definition, ask your loan officer to walk through the three-year ownership and parental-ownership questions with you directly.
