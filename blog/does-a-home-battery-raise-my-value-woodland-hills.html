Does Adding a Home Battery Actually Raise My Woodland Hills Home's Value?

Jonathan Lopez, Woodland Hills Realtor · Jim Sandoval Group · CA DRE #02282844 · Updated November 2026

If you already have solar panels on your Woodland Hills home, or you are shopping for a home that does, you have probably seen a home battery like a Tesla Powerwall mentioned as the next upgrade. The question worth asking before spending $13,000 to $18,500 installing one is whether it actually raises what your home is worth, or whether it is purely a lifestyle and backup-power purchase. The honest answer right now is: it is mostly the second one, with a real and growing exception around buyer demand.

What the national data actually shows. Zillow's 2026 home trends report found that mentions of whole-home batteries in listing descriptions rose 40% in 2025, on top of a 62% jump the year before. That is a real signal that agents are increasingly marketing battery backup as a selling point, and that buyers are responding to it, especially in areas prone to outages. Separately, Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory's U.S. Distributed Solar and Storage Data shows that 74% of new residential solar installations in California added battery storage in 2025, up sharply from 58% in 2024. Nationally the attach rate is lower, 37% in 2025 versus 25% in 2024, which tells you California households are adopting batteries faster than the rest of the country.

Why the rest of California is moving so fast, and why that story does not fully apply here. Most of that California surge is being driven by the Net Billing Tariff, commonly called NEM 3.0, which took effect for customers of the state's investor-owned utilities (Southern California Edison, PG&E, San Diego Gas and Electric) in April 2023. NEM 3.0 cut what those utilities pay for exported solar power from close to the full retail rate down to a much lower avoided-cost rate, which is exactly why pairing solar with a battery (to store power and use it yourself later, rather than sell it back cheap) became financially necessary almost overnight for those customers.

Woodland Hills is inside the City of Los Angeles, which means it is served by the Los Angeles Department of Water and Power, a municipal utility, not Southern California Edison. LADWP's own Net Energy Metering Guidelines confirm that exported solar energy is still credited at the applicable retail rate schedule, the same rate you pay to buy electricity, not a reduced avoided-cost rate. In plain terms: the specific financial pressure pushing most of California toward batteries right now does not land the same way on a Woodland Hills electric bill, because the export credit here has not been cut the way it has for NEM 3.0 utility customers.

So does a battery still make sense here? Likely yes, just for a different reason than the bill-savings pitch you will see in most national content. The strongest case for a Woodland Hills homeowner is backup power during an outage and the marketability boost documented in Zillow's listing-mention data, not a locked-in NEM 3.0 financial return that does not actually apply under LADWP's rate structure.

What this means for your home's value specifically. This is the honest limit of what current data supports: there is no independent appraisal study for battery storage the way there is for owned solar panels (the Lawrence Berkeley "Selling Into the Sun" study used in an earlier post on this page found a real, calculable price premium for owned PV systems). No comparable study exists yet quantifying a dollar premium for battery storage specifically. What exists is rising buyer interest and rising listing-mention rates, which is a real market signal worth knowing about, but it is not the same thing as a documented appraisal add. Treat a battery as a genuine value-add to marketability and buyer appeal, not as a guaranteed line item an appraiser will credit the way they might credit square footage or a remodeled kitchen.

If you are selling soon. A documented, working battery backup system is worth highlighting in your listing description and photos, since buyer interest in backup power is real and rising. If you are deciding whether to spend the money before listing versus letting a buyer factor it in themselves, that is exactly the kind of judgment call worth a conversation about your specific home and timeline, not a generic yes-or-no answer.

On Woodland Hills' current market (Movoto: $1,300,000 median sale price, 368 active listings, 54-day average days on market), a battery is a relatively small spend against the total transaction, but it is the kind of detail that can meaningfully affect how a listing reads to a buyer who has lived through a power outage recently.

This post is general information, not financial or appraisal advice. Confirm current LADWP net metering terms, installer pricing and incentives before buying.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does adding a home battery raise my home's appraised value in Woodland Hills?

There is no independent study documenting a specific appraisal premium for battery storage, unlike owned solar panels, which do have a studied resale premium. What is documented is rising buyer demand and listing-mention rates, which affects marketability more than a guaranteed appraisal line item.

Is a home battery as financially necessary here as it is in the rest of California?

Not for the same reason. Most of the state's battery surge is driven by NEM 3.0, which cut export payments for customers of Southern California Edison, PG&E, and San Diego Gas and Electric. Woodland Hills is served by LADWP, a municipal utility that still credits solar exports at the full retail rate, so that specific financial pressure does not apply the same way here.

What utility serves Woodland Hills?

The Los Angeles Department of Water and Power (LADWP), since Woodland Hills is within Los Angeles city limits. Areas outside the city limits in this region are typically served by Southern California Edison instead.

If a battery is not about bill savings here, why would I get one?

Backup power during an outage, and marketability. Zillow's 2026 home trends report found listings mentioning whole-home batteries jumped 40% in 2025, meaning more buyers are actively looking for this feature regardless of the underlying utility economics.

How much does a home battery cost to install?

Current guides put a single unit (roughly 13.5 kWh) at approximately $13,000 to $18,500 installed in California, varying with installation complexity and incentives like the state's Self-Generation Incentive Program. Get a current quote from a licensed installer rather than relying on a general figure.

Should I install a battery before listing my home, or let the buyer decide?

That depends on your timeline and budget more than a universal rule. If you already have solar and are considering it, it is worth a direct conversation about your specific home and market timing rather than a blanket recommendation either way.
