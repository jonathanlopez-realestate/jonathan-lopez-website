A Lawsuit Was Just Filed Over the Blocked Woodland Hills Country Club Housing Project. What It Means for Local Supply

Jonathan Lopez, Woodland Hills Realtor · Jim Sandoval Group · CA DRE #02282844 · Updated November 2026

There is a new development, literally and legally, in a story Woodland Hills has been watching for months: whether the Woodland Hills Country Club golf course gets redeveloped into housing. This week, three housing advocacy groups, YIMBY Law, Californians for Homeownership, and the California Housing Defense Fund, filed suit against the City of Los Angeles in LA County Superior Court.

The lawsuit challenges the city's August 20, 2026 determination that an application for 398 homes at 4868 Canoga Avenue, on 20 of the Country Club's 93 acres, did not qualify for the streamlined, by-right approval process available under AB 2011, the Affordable Housing and High Road Jobs Act. City planners' stated reason was that the golf course functions as habitat for mountain lions, a designation that, if it stands, exempts the site from the fast-track process entirely. Earlier reporting on this property described a related but not identical 301-unit configuration (175 single-family homes and 126 apartments, including 97 affordable units) that the Los Angeles City Council separately voted 11-1 to block on August 26, 2026, through a zoning amendment affecting the A-1 agricultural/residential zone. Both actions concern the same Country Club site; this post is specifically about the newer August 20 AB 2011 rejection and this week's lawsuit over it.

The housing groups are not litigating alone against a neutral referee. The California Department of Housing and Community Development sent the city a formal warning letter on October 28, 2026, stating that the city's habitat rationale "appears to improperly expand the statutory standard" that AB 2011 actually allows for denial. That letter gave the city until November 25, 2026 to correct course. The lawsuit asks the court to set aside the city's August 20 determination and order approval of the application under state law.

What makes this different from a routine zoning dispute is the money now on the table. AB 712, a state law that took effect January 1, 2026, lets a court impose escalated fines on a city found to have violated state housing law after being formally warned and given a chance to fix it. The statute's own minimum is $10,000 per unit for projects over four units, or $50,000 per unit for smaller ones, and that multiplies further if the same city has already been found violating the same law within the same planning period. Applied illustratively to a 398-unit project, the base exposure alone would run into the millions, a real number, not a scare figure, though it is a court that would actually set it, and only if the city loses and still has not corrected the issue after November 25.

None of this means shovels are going in the ground soon. A lawsuit is a request for a court to act, not a building permit. But for anyone tracking the west side of Woodland Hills, this is the clearest signal yet that the Country Club site remains an open, contested question rather than a settled one, with real legal and financial pressure now pushing toward resolution one way or the other. If you are a seller near this corridor wondering how a large pending project affects buyer perception, or a buyer wondering whether nearby supply might change, this is a story worth revisiting as the November 25 deadline and the lawsuit both move forward.

Frequently Asked Questions

What exactly got sued over this week?

Three housing advocacy groups sued the City of Los Angeles over its August 20, 2026 decision that a 398-home application at the Woodland Hills Country Club (4868 Canoga Avenue) did not qualify for AB 2011's streamlined approval process, because the city determined the golf course is mountain lion habitat.

Is this the same project the City Council voted on in August?

It is the same Country Club property, but the two actions are not identical. The City Council's August 26, 2026 zoning amendment addressed a related 301-unit configuration (175 single-family homes, 126 apartments) at 21150 Dumetz Road. This lawsuit challenges a separate August 20, 2026 administrative rejection of a 398-home AB 2011 application at 4868 Canoga Avenue on the same site.

Why did the state get involved?

The California Department of Housing and Community Development warned the city on October 28, 2026 that its mountain lion habitat rationale "appears to improperly expand the statutory standard" under AB 2011, and gave the city until November 25, 2026 to respond before further state action.

Could the city actually be fined over this?

Possibly. AB 712, effective since January 1, 2026, allows courts to fine a city a minimum of $10,000 per unit (for projects over four units) if it is found to have violated state housing law after being warned and failing to correct it. That is a court-set outcome tied to the litigation's result, not a fine that has been issued yet.

Does this change anything for buyers or sellers near the Country Club right now?

Not immediately. No construction has been approved and this is still active litigation. The practical takeaway is that this large site remains an open question for future supply in that part of Woodland Hills, worth watching rather than acting on today.

Where can I follow this as it develops?

The city has a November 25, 2026 deadline to respond to the state's warning letter, and the lawsuit will proceed in LA County Superior Court. I track local land-use stories like this as part of working with buyers and sellers in this specific corridor.
