Should I Require Proof of Funds Before Showing My Woodland Hills Luxury Listing?

Jonathan Lopez, Woodland Hills Realtor · Jim Sandoval Group · CA DRE #02282844 · Updated November 2026

If you are marketing a higher-end Woodland Hills home, you have probably heard that some of Los Angeles's top luxury listings require buyers to prove they can actually afford the home before anyone even schedules a walkthrough. The question worth asking before copying that approach is whether it actually fits a Woodland Hills listing, or whether it is a practice built for a different price tier entirely.

What this practice actually looks like. At the higher end of the Los Angeles luxury market, commonly homes priced at $5 million and above, it has become standard for listing agents to request a proof-of-funds letter, a bank or brokerage statement showing sufficient liquid assets, or a lender's pre-approval letter, before confirming a showing request. The reasoning is straightforward: showings at that level take real coordination (security, staff, sometimes a listing agent's own time on-site), and sellers want assurance the person touring the home could genuinely close on it, not just that they are curious what a $6 million house looks like inside. This is not unique to Los Angeles. Luxury brokerages in other high-end markets have used the same screening step for years; one Sotheby's International Realty affiliate in Greenville, South Carolina has required 24-hour notice plus verification of funds or a pre-approval letter on its luxury listings for roughly 15 years, evidence that this is an established luxury-market practice generally, not a one-off Los Angeles quirk.

Why Woodland Hills is a different question than Bel Air or Beverly Hills. The $5 million-plus threshold commonly cited for this practice describes Los Angeles's ultra-luxury submarkets, not the luxury tier most Woodland Hills sellers are actually marketing in. An earlier post on this page, researching what counts as "luxury" by LA-metro standards, found the regional luxury threshold sits closer to $3.9 million using a top-10-percent definition, and most homes marketed as luxury in Woodland Hills specifically fall in a roughly $1.5 million to $7 million range, well below the ultra-luxury tier where proof-of-funds screening became routine. Requiring a buyer's agent to produce a pre-approval letter before scheduling a tour on a $1.8 million Woodland Hills listing is a meaningfully different ask than doing the same thing on a $12 million Bel Air estate, even though both are technically "luxury" by some definition.

The real tradeoff, either way. Any screening step before a showing filters out two different kinds of people at once: buyers who are not actually qualified to purchase the home, and buyers who are qualified but find the extra step inconvenient enough to skip touring a home they might otherwise have toured. At the ultra-luxury tier, sellers generally accept that tradeoff because the pool of genuinely qualified buyers is small to begin with, and wasted showings carry a real cost. At a lower luxury price point with a larger pool of realistically qualified buyers, the same screening step risks turning away legitimate interest that a less restrictive showing policy would have captured.

What this looks like in practice for a Woodland Hills seller. If your home is priced well above the typical Woodland Hills luxury range, closer to the ultra-luxury end of the Los Angeles market, a proof-of-funds or pre-approval requirement before showings is a reasonable, increasingly normal ask, and buyers shopping at that level generally expect it. If your home sits in the more common $1.5 million to $3 million Woodland Hills luxury range, the better move is usually a lighter version of the same idea: asking a buyer's agent to confirm their client is pre-approved or has verified funds before confirming the appointment, without necessarily demanding a formal letter up front, preserving some friction-reduction while still filtering out clearly unqualified lookers.

Where this fits into your broader marketing strategy. Showing policy is one lever among several covered elsewhere on this page, drone video, international buyer outreach, auction formats, and fire-insurance readiness among them, and none of them work in isolation. The right showing policy for your specific home depends on its actual price point within Woodland Hills' own luxury range, not a rule imported wholesale from Bel Air or Beverly Hills.

On today's broader Woodland Hills market (Movoto, citywide, all price tiers: $1,300,000 median sale price, 368 active listings, 54-day average days on market), luxury inventory moves on its own timeline and competitive dynamics that this citywide figure does not fully capture, which is exactly why a showing-policy decision benefits from a real conversation about your specific listing rather than a general rule.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I require proof of funds before showing my Woodland Hills luxury listing?

It depends on where your home sits in the market. At the $5 million-plus tier common in Los Angeles's ultra-luxury submarkets, requiring a proof-of-funds letter or lender pre-approval before a showing has become standard practice. At the more typical $1.5 million to $3 million Woodland Hills luxury range, a lighter version, simply confirming pre-approval or verified funds before the appointment, usually strikes a better balance.

Why do luxury sellers require this at all?

Showings at the higher end require real coordination and sometimes the listing agent's own time on-site, and sellers want assurance the person touring the home can actually close, not just that they are curious.

Does this practice actually turn away legitimate buyers?

It can. Any screening step before a showing filters out unqualified lookers, but it can also discourage a qualified buyer who finds the extra step inconvenient enough to skip touring the home. That tradeoff is more often worth it at the ultra-luxury tier, where the pool of genuinely qualified buyers is small, than at a lower luxury price point with a larger realistic buyer pool.

Is this a Los Angeles-specific practice?

No. It is well-documented in other high-end markets too; one Sotheby's International Realty affiliate outside Los Angeles has used a similar 24-hour notice plus verification-of-funds requirement on its luxury listings for about 15 years, suggesting it is an established luxury-market norm generally, not something unique to this region.

What counts as "luxury" in Woodland Hills specifically?

Based on prior research on this page, Woodland Hills luxury listings generally run roughly $1.5 million to $7 million, below the $3.9 million-plus LA-metro top-10-percent threshold and well below the $5 million-plus tier where proof-of-funds screening is most common.

What's a reasonable middle ground if I don't want a formal proof-of-funds policy?

Ask your listing agent to have buyer's agents confirm their client is pre-approved or has verified funds before confirming a showing appointment, without necessarily requiring a formal letter up front. This keeps some friction-reduction for serious buyers while still filtering out clearly unqualified lookers.
