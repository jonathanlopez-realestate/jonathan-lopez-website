If I Cancel and Relist My Woodland Hills Home, Does the "Days on Market" Clock Actually Reset?

Jonathan Lopez, Woodland Hills Realtor · Jim Sandoval Group · CA DRE #02282844 · Updated November 2026

A question that comes up a lot with Woodland Hills sellers whose homes have sat on the market longer than they expected: if the listing just isn't getting traction, what happens if I cancel it and put it back up as something new? Does that clear the "days on market" number buyers see, or does it just look like a fresh listing on the surface while the real history is still visible underneath?

The honest answer, as of a rule change that took effect less than a year ago, is mostly no, not anymore.

Two different clocks, not one

To understand why, it helps to know that the regional MLS covering Woodland Hills, CRMLS, actually tracks two separate numbers behind the scenes, not one.

The first is DAM, or Days Active in MLS. This is simple: it's the day count for the current listing specifically, starting the moment it goes live in Active status (or Active Under Contract) and accruing from there. If you cancel a listing and start a brand new one, the new listing's own DAM does start over at zero.

The second is CDAM, or Cumulative Days Active in MLS. This number adds together the DAM from the current listing and every prior listing of that same property, going back through however many times it's been listed. CDAM is the number that actually travels out to the public-facing portals buyers use, Zillow, Redfin, Realtor.com, which is the real reason this matters to a seller. A buyer scrolling listings doesn't usually dig into MLS-internal status history, but the cumulative days-on-market figure those portals surface is exactly the kind of thing that shapes a buyer's gut read on whether a home is fresh or has been sitting, and whether that's worth a lower offer.

What changed, and when

Before November 19, 2025, CRMLS reset CDAM to zero once a property had been completely off the MLS for 90 consecutive days. That's a fairly generous window, long enough that a seller could cancel a stalled listing, wait a season, and relist with a genuinely clean cumulative count.

CRMLS shortened that window to 30 days, effective November 19, 2025, specifically to keep the cumulative figure a more accurate, real-time reflection of how long a property has actually been trying to sell, rather than letting a short off-market gap wipe the slate clean. New listings entered on or after that date fall under the new 30-day standard, and existing listings were brought in line with it in the weeks that followed.

Practically, that means a seller today has to take a listing completely off CRMLS for at least 30 straight days, no showings, no active marketing, before relisting resets the cumulative number that buyers and their agents actually see. Cancel on a Monday and relist two weeks later, and the cumulative clock just keeps running from where it left off, even though the new listing's own DAM reads as brand new.

Two real exceptions worth knowing

There are two situations where CDAM genuinely does reset, no 30-day wait required.

The first is a change of ownership. If the property actually sells and changes hands, the next listing of that home starts a completely fresh CDAM count, since the cumulative history is tied to continuous ownership, not just the address.

The second is Coming Soon status. CRMLS allows a listing to sit in Coming Soon for up to 21 days before it has to go fully Active, and because no showings are permitted while a listing is in that status, it doesn't accrue any DAM at all during that window. That's a genuinely useful tool for building buzz before a listing officially starts its public clock, but it's a pre-market preview period, not a way to erase time a home has already spent actively trying to sell.

Why this is worth knowing before you cancel anything

If a listing in Woodland Hills has stalled, the instinct to "start fresh" is understandable, a new listing date, a clean-looking zero in the days-on-market field, can feel like it buys a second first impression. The 30-day rule means that instinct mostly doesn't work the way it used to unless you're genuinely prepared to pull the home off the market entirely for a full month. Relist inside that window, and the cumulative number buyers actually see keeps climbing right where it was.

That doesn't mean a stalled listing has no real options. Price, photos, marketing, and showing availability are all things that can meaningfully change a listing's trajectory without needing to touch the MLS status at all, and in most cases those are the actual lever worth pulling, not a status reset that no longer accomplishes what it used to.

Locally, Movoto's Woodland Hills page, updated October 30, 2026, shows a median sale price of $1,299,000, 379 active listings, and an average 50 days on market, up from 44 a year ago. In a market where days on market has been drifting upward, a listing that reads as stale to a buyer is a real, measurable risk, and it's worth planning around with an actual strategy rather than a status trick that no longer works the way it once did.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does canceling and relisting my home actually make it look brand new to buyers?

The new listing's own Days Active in MLS (DAM) does start at zero, but the Cumulative Days Active in MLS (CDAM) figure, the one that travels out to Zillow, Redfin, and other portals, only resets if the property has been completely off CRMLS for at least 30 consecutive days first.

What changed about this rule, and when?

CRMLS shortened the CDAM reset window from 90 days to 30 days, effective November 19, 2025, specifically to keep the cumulative days-on-market figure a more accurate, real-time reflection of how long a home has actually been trying to sell.

Are there any ways CDAM resets immediately, without waiting 30 days?

Yes, two: if the property actually changes ownership, or if the relevant time was spent in Coming Soon status, which doesn't accrue Days Active in MLS at all since no showings are permitted during that period.

Does this rule apply everywhere, or just locally?

It's specific to CRMLS, the regional MLS covering Woodland Hills. NAR leaves the cumulative days-on-market reset period up to each local MLS, so other markets may use a different window.

If the 30-day reset doesn't fix a stalled listing's optics, what actually does?

In most cases, price, photography, marketing reach, and showing availability move the needle more than a status reset. A genuine 30-day full withdrawal is one option, but it comes with its own cost: zero visibility and zero showings for a full month in an already competitive market.

Should I still consider taking my home off the market for 30 days if it's been sitting a long time?

It depends on why it's been sitting. If the real issue is price or presentation, fixing those usually works faster and with less downside than a full 30-day pause. I can help sort out which one actually applies to your situation.
