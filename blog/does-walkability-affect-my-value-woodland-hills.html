Does My Woodland Hills Home's Walkability Actually Affect Its Value?

Jonathan Lopez, Woodland Hills Realtor · Jim Sandoval Group · CA DRE #02282844 · Updated November 2026

Most Woodland Hills sellers think about location in big, obvious terms: which side of the Boulevard, which school boundary, whether there's a view. A narrower locational factor gets far less attention, even though it has a real, researched dollar value attached to it: how walkable the specific address actually is, not the neighborhood in general, but that address.

What Walk Score measures. Walk Score is a 0-100 rating, built by analyzing the distance from a specific address to nearby grocery stores, restaurants, parks, schools, and other everyday destinations, along with street connectivity and intersection density. It's not a vague impression, it's a calculated number, and it shows up automatically on most major real estate listing sites next to a property's address. A score in the 90s is a "Walker's Paradise," 70 to 89 is "Very Walkable," 50 to 69 is "Somewhat Walkable," 25 to 49 is "Car-Dependent," and below 25 means almost all errands require a car.

The research behind the dollar figure. In 2016, Redfin published its own analysis of more than one million home sales across 14 major U.S. metro areas, using sale data from January 2014 through April 2016. Using hedonic regression, a statistical method that isolates one variable's effect by controlling for everything else that normally drives price, the study held size, bedroom and bathroom count, age, property type, neighborhood income, and broader market conditions constant, and then measured what Walk Score alone contributed. Across all 14 metros combined, one additional Walk Score point added an average of $3,250, or 0.9%, to a home's sale price. That average masked enormous variation by metro: a single point was worth as little as $114 in Orange County and as much as $4,386 in Washington, D.C.

The Los Angeles-specific number. For the Los Angeles metro specifically, Redfin's study found a $3,948 premium per Walk Score point, a 0.83% increase, on a metro median sale price of $475,000 at the time. That's the fourth-highest dollar premium of the 14 metros studied, behind only Washington, D.C., San Francisco, and Boston, meaning walkability carried more real weight in the LA market than in most of the country, even back in 2016.

What that looks like against today's Woodland Hills numbers. This study is a decade old now, built on 2014-2016 sale prices, and Redfin has not re-run it with current data, so there's no fresh 2026 per-point dollar figure to cite directly. What can be done honestly is applying the study's own percentage rate, 0.83% per point, to today's actual market. Woodland Hills' current citywide median sale price is $1,300,000 (Movoto). At 0.83% per point, that works out to roughly $10,790 per Walk Score point today. This is this post's own illustrative arithmetic built on the study's real rate, not a new survey or a re-run of Redfin's methodology, and it's presented that way rather than as a freshly verified 2026 figure.

Why this matters more in Woodland Hills than it might seem. Walk Score's own published rating for the broader Woodland Hills-Warner Center neighborhood is 48 out of 100, squarely in the car-dependent range, and 82nd among Los Angeles neighborhoods for walkability. That single neighborhood number hides a lot of real variation underneath it. Individual 91367 addresses that Walk Score has actually scored range from the low 20s, fully car-dependent, up near the low 80s, very walkable, depending almost entirely on how close that specific address sits to the Ventura Blvd retail corridor or the Warner Center area. Two homes just a few blocks apart, otherwise similar in size and condition, can carry meaningfully different scores and, per Redfin's own research, meaningfully different amenity-driven value.

What this means practically, for sellers and buyers alike. For a seller, knowing your home's actual Walk Score, not a guess, not the neighborhood average, is a legitimate marketing data point if it's a strong one, and useful context either way when setting an asking price relative to a comparable home closer to or farther from the corridor. For a buyer, it's a factor worth weighing the same way a view or a school boundary gets weighed, since it's measuring something concrete: how much of daily life can happen without a car.

Where this isn't the whole story. Walk Score measures proximity and street layout, not subjective preference. Some buyers specifically want distance from retail traffic and noise, which is the flip side already covered in a separate post on this site about busy streets reducing value. Walkability and quiet don't always point the same direction on the same block, and a Walk Score alone doesn't settle which matters more to a specific buyer.

On today's broader Woodland Hills market (Movoto, citywide: $1,300,000 median sale price, 368 active listings, 54-day average days on market), a locational factor worth roughly five figures per point, by Redfin's own research rate, is worth checking before you set a list price, not guessing at.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Walk Score, exactly?

It's a 0-100 rating, calculated for a specific address, based on the distance to nearby grocery stores, restaurants, parks, schools, and other everyday destinations, plus street connectivity. It's not a subjective neighborhood reputation score, it's calculated the same way for every address and shown on most major listing sites.

Does walkability really add measurable dollar value to a home?

Yes, according to Redfin's own 2016 study of more than 1 million home sales across 14 major metros, which controlled for size, age, bedrooms, and other features and still found a real premium tied specifically to Walk Score. In the Los Angeles metro, that premium was $3,948 per point at the time, a 0.83% increase on the metro's median sale price.

Is Woodland Hills generally walkable?

Not as a whole. Walk Score rates the broader Woodland Hills-Warner Center neighborhood 48 out of 100, car-dependent, and 82nd among Los Angeles neighborhoods. But that single number hides real variation between specific addresses.

How much does Walk Score actually vary within Woodland Hills?

Significantly. Individual 91367 addresses Walk Score has scored range from the low 20s, car-dependent, up to the low 80s, very walkable, depending mostly on proximity to the Ventura Blvd corridor and Warner Center. Two similar homes a few blocks apart can score very differently.

Is the dollar figure in this post a current, 2026 number?

No, and this post says so directly. Redfin's $3,948-per-point figure is from a 2016 study using 2014-2016 sale data, not a fresh 2026 calculation. The roughly $10,790-per-point figure applied to today's Woodland Hills median is this post's own illustrative math using that same study's percentage rate, not a new study.

How do I find out what my own home's Walk Score actually is?

It's a free, instant lookup at walkscore.com for any specific address, not an estimate. I'm happy to pull it for your home and talk through what it, along with the rest of your home's features, means for pricing it.
