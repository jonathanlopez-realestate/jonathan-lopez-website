Should I Market My Woodland Hills Luxury Listing to International Buyers?

Jonathan Lopez, Woodland Hills Realtor · Jim Sandoval Group · CA DRE #02282844 · Updated November 2026

Sellers marketing a luxury home in Woodland Hills usually think first about the local buyer pool: San Fernando Valley upsizers, entertainment-industry buyers, people relocating within Los Angeles. That instinct is not wrong, but it can leave real demand on the table, because a meaningful share of the buyers purchasing property in California right now are not domestic buyers at all.

The National Association of REALTORS publishes an annual International Transactions in U.S. Residential Real Estate report, and the 2026 edition, covering the twelve months from April 2025 through March 2026, puts real numbers behind that demand. Nationally, foreign buyers purchased 67,100 U.S. homes worth a combined $45.3 billion during that period, at a national median purchase price of $465,000, with 48% of those deals closing entirely in cash. The top countries of origin nationally were Canada (16% of foreign-buyer transactions), Mexico (14%), and China (11%).

California's own numbers inside that national report are where this gets directly relevant to Woodland Hills. California accounted for 19% of all foreign-buyer purchases nationwide, the second-largest destination in the country behind only Florida. Within California specifically, the top countries of origin were Mexico, China, and Canada, the median purchase price was $965,900, 69% of California's foreign buyers reside outside the United States at the time of purchase, and 38% bought the home as a primary residence rather than a vacation property or investment.

It's worth being direct about what that $965,900 median does and doesn't tell a Woodland Hills luxury seller. It sits below where most homes in this market's actual luxury tier are priced, so it would be dishonest to read this data as proof that international buyers are specifically shopping Woodland Hills' highest-end inventory. What it does show is that California, broadly, is the second-biggest state in the country for this kind of buyer, that a large majority of them are purchasing from outside the U.S. and therefore are not finding a listing through the same local channels a Valley buyer would use, and that nearly half of all international transactions nationally close in cash, a detail that matters directly to a seller weighing offer quality and certainty of close. None of the NAR data breaks out results at the city or zip-code level, since no public source currently publishes international buyer figures that granular, and that limitation is disclosed here rather than papered over.

What this means practically for a Woodland Hills luxury listing is less about chasing a specific nationality of buyer and more about making sure the marketing plan doesn't quietly exclude an entire category of real, documented demand. A listing that only runs through MLS syndication to U.S. portals, local social media, and English-language print is invisible to a California-bound buyer researching from Mexico City, Shanghai, or Toronto before they ever set foot in the state. Reaching that buyer usually means working with a brokerage or marketing package that actually syndicates to international platforms, offering materials in more than one language where it's a realistic fit for the home and price point, and being deliberate about timing given that a 69%-majority of these buyers are coordinating a purchase from outside the country, which often means a longer, more logistics-heavy process than a local buyer's.

None of this replaces strong local marketing, and it shouldn't. The two aren't competing strategies, they're additive. A Woodland Hills luxury listing that reaches both the local buyer pool and the documented international demand covered in NAR's own research is simply reaching more of the real market than one that reaches only half of it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are international buyers really active in the California luxury market right now?

Yes. NAR's 2026 International Transactions report (April 2025-March 2026) shows California captured 19% of all foreign-buyer purchases nationwide, second only to Florida, with a median purchase price of $965,900 statewide.

Does that mean international buyers are specifically shopping Woodland Hills' luxury tier?

Not provably. NAR's data is statewide, not broken out by city or zip code, and the $965,900 California median sits below where most Woodland Hills luxury homes are priced. The honest takeaway is that documented demand exists in California broadly, not a guarantee it's concentrated in this exact market.

Which countries make up the largest share of California's foreign buyers?

Mexico, China, and Canada are the top three countries of origin for international buyers purchasing in California, per NAR's 2026 report.

Do international buyers typically pay cash?

Nationally, 48% of foreign-buyer transactions closed entirely in cash in NAR's 2026 report. That's a meaningful share, and it matters to a seller comparing offer certainty, though it isn't broken out separately for California.

If most foreign buyers live outside the U.S., how would they even find my listing?

That's the core marketing gap this post is about. Standard MLS syndication to U.S. portals and local marketing generally does not reach a buyer researching from abroad. Reaching them takes a marketing plan that deliberately includes international syndication and outreach, not just domestic channels run harder.

Should I replace my local marketing plan with an international one?

No. The two should run together. International reach adds to a strong local campaign, it doesn't substitute for one, since most of Woodland Hills' real buyer demand is still local.
