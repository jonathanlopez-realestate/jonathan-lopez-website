Why Do Luxury-Tier Woodland Hills Homes Take Longer to Sell, Even When Demand Is Strong?

Jonathan Lopez · Woodland Hills Realtor, Jim Sandoval Group · CA DRE #02282844 · Updated October 2026

If you're marketing a Woodland Hills home at the higher end of the local price range, a recent national report explains a pattern worth knowing before you list: luxury homes are taking longer to sell than the rest of the market, even though demand for them is climbing.

Redfin's Luxury Market Summary, covering the three months ending April 30, 2026 and published May 26, 2026, defines a luxury home as one estimated to be in the top 5% of its metro area's price range. Nationally, the median luxury home took 60 days to sell, up 6 days from a year earlier. Non-luxury homes took 51 days, up 5 days over the same period. The gap itself isn't new, luxury homes have typically taken longer to sell than the broader market, but both tiers are slowing, and luxury is slowing from a smaller base of buyers to begin with.

What makes this worth a second look is what's happening on the demand side at the same time. Pending sales of luxury homes jumped 4.3% year over year, Redfin's largest gain in that measure since January 2025. New listings of luxury homes climbed 2% year over year over the same three months. Homes sold rose 0.8% and active listings rose 1.4%, both modest but positive. None of that reads like a soft segment. Redfin's own explanation is straightforward: luxury buyers are typically less sensitive to mortgage rate swings than non-luxury buyers, since a smaller share of that purchase price is typically financed at all. That insulates luxury demand from rate headlines in a way the rest of the market isn't insulated, even as each individual sale still takes longer to close.

The practical takeaway for a Woodland Hills seller at the top of the local range: a longer time on market in that tier is not automatically a sign your price or your marketing is off. It may just be the tier. The mistake would be reacting to 60, 70, or 90 days on market the same way you'd react to that timeline on a median-priced listing, with a reflexive price cut, when the data suggests the better response is patience paired with marketing built for a smaller, more deliberate buyer pool.

It's worth being direct about what this data can and can't tell a Woodland Hills seller. These are national figures. No public source, not Redfin, not Movoto, not the local MLS data available publicly, breaks out days on market by price tier for Woodland Hills or the 91367 zip code specifically. Movoto's Woodland Hills page, updated October 27, 2026, shows a citywide median sale price of $1,299,000 for September, 379 active listings, 71 of them already price-reduced, and an average 50 days on market versus 44 a year ago. That figure blends every price point in the city, from entry-level condos to the handful of listings that would actually clear Redfin's top-5% luxury bar locally. There is no way to isolate the luxury-tier number from public data alone, which is exactly why a real, current CMA at your specific price point matters more than a citywide average once you're above the median.

If your Woodland Hills home is priced well above the city's $1,299,000 median, the marketing conversation should start with an honest timeline, not a rushed one. That means a plan built for the buyer pool that actually shops at that price point: more deliberate decision-making, less rate sensitivity, and a longer runway by national pattern. A seller who knows that going in is in a much better position than one who panics at day 45.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do luxury homes really take longer to sell than other homes?

Per Redfin's Luxury Market Summary (three months ending April 30, 2026), yes nationally: 60 days median for luxury versus 51 days for non-luxury, both up year over year.

Does that mean luxury demand is weak?

No. Redfin reported pending sales of luxury homes up 4.3% year over year over the same period, the largest gain since January 2025, alongside rising new listings.

Why would luxury homes sell at a healthy pace but still take longer?

Redfin attributes part of this to luxury buyers being typically less sensitive to mortgage rate swings than non-luxury buyers, which keeps demand steadier even as the typical sale timeline runs longer.

How does Redfin define a luxury home?

The top 5% of a given metro area's price range, a different and higher bar than some other luxury thresholds you may see cited elsewhere.

Is there Woodland Hills-specific luxury days-on-market data?

Not publicly. Movoto's Woodland Hills page blends all price tiers into one days-on-market figure, 50 days as of the October 27, 2026 update. No public source isolates the luxury tier locally.

If my home is priced at the top of the local range, should I expect it to sit longer?

Possibly, based on the national pattern, and that's not necessarily a sign of a pricing or marketing problem. A real conversation about your specific price point and comparable sales is the way to know for sure.
