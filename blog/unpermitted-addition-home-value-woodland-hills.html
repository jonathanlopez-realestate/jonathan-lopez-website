Does an Unpermitted Addition Count Toward My Woodland Hills Home's Value?

Jonathan Lopez, Jim Sandoval Group | Woodland Hills Realtor | CA DRE #02282844 · Updated September 2026

Many Woodland Hills homes have a converted garage, a closed-in patio, or an added bedroom that was built without a city permit, sometimes decades ago by a prior owner. Homeowners then ask whether that space counts when they sell or refinance. The honest answer is that it depends, and the rules are less rigid than most people assume.

What Fannie Mae Actually Says

Fannie Mae's Appraisal and Property-Related FAQ (updated November 2023) addresses unpermitted additions directly. When an appraiser identifies one, the appraiser must comment on the quality and appearance of the work and assess its impact, if any, on the market value of the property. The FAQ does not say the space must be excluded. It says the appraiser must analyze it. Individual lenders, loan programs, and appraisers may apply more conservative approaches, so this is a baseline for conventional loans, not a guarantee for every transaction.

Why This Matters in Woodland Hills Right Now

Movoto's data (updated September 24, 2026) shows a $1,348,500 median sale price for August 2026, 408 active listings, 77 already price-reduced, and an average of 48 days on market versus 45 a year earlier. In a market where buyers have options, they and their lenders can afford to be selective. A value built partly on space that cannot be verified through permit records is easier to challenge in an appraisal or a negotiation. Disclosure Is a Separate Question

Under California Civil Code sections 1102 through 1102.14, sellers of most one-to-four unit residential properties complete a statutory disclosure form that covers known material issues, including unpermitted structural changes. So even if an appraiser does not discount the space, a seller who knows about unpermitted work is expected to disclose it, and buyers will often ask about it during negotiation. What a Homeowner Can Do Before Listing

Check the property's permit history with the City of Los Angeles Department of Building and Safety to see what is on record. Talk with a licensed contractor or your agent about whether legalizing the work is realistic in your case. Then get a comparable-sales analysis that shows the home's value with and without the added space, so you know the range before pricing.

This is general information, not legal, tax, or appraisal advice. Permit and code questions should go to the City and a licensed professional.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will an appraiser count my unpermitted room?

Fannie Mae requires the appraiser to comment on the work's quality and assess its market-value impact. It does not automatically exclude or include it, so outcomes vary by appraiser, lender, and loan program.

Do I have to disclose unpermitted work when I sell?

California's statutory Transfer Disclosure Statement covers known unpermitted structural changes, so known unpermitted work should be disclosed. Confirm specifics with a real estate attorney.

Does unpermitted space change my listing price?

It can. A CMA that shows value with and without the space gives you a defensible range before you set a price.

How do I find out what is permitted on my Woodland Hills home?

Start with the City of Los Angeles Department of Building and Safety permit records for your address.

Is this the same rule for every loan type?

No. The cited guidance is Fannie Mae's, which applies to conventional loans sold to Fannie Mae. Other loan programs and individual lenders may be stricter.
