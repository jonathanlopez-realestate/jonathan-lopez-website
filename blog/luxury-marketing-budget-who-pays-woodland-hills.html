How Much Should My Woodland Hills Luxury Listing's Marketing Budget Actually Be, and Who Pays For It?

Jonathan Lopez, Woodland Hills Realtor · Jim Sandoval Group · CA DRE #02282844 · Updated November 2026

Sellers thinking about listing a higher-end Woodland Hills home almost always ask some version of the same question eventually: how much marketing is actually enough, and whose job is it to pay for it? Both parts of that question have real answers, but neither one is a fixed, industry-wide number the way, say, a standard commission split used to be talked about. It genuinely depends on the home, the agent, and what's actually written into the listing agreement.

On the budget side, multiple industry sources covering luxury marketing in 2026 put the typical range at roughly 0.5% to 2% of the home's list price for a full marketing plan, with lower-tier luxury listings trending toward the bottom of that range and true trophy properties trending toward the top. That's a planning range, not a guarantee, and it is not based on a Woodland Hills-specific study. Still, it gives a useful sense of scale: on a $2 million home, 0.5% to 2% works out to $10,000 to $40,000 across an entire marketing plan, not any one vendor's invoice. That total typically covers professional photography, some form of 3D tour or video, digital advertising (commonly social platforms like Facebook and Instagram), MLS syndication, and often printed pieces like brochures or postcards, plus staging consultation.

To make that concrete without pretending it's a Woodland Hills figure, one Las Vegas brokerage's published November 2026 vendor price list itemizes HDR photography at $145 to $225 depending on square footage, a Matterport-style 3D scan at $195 to $325, twilight photography as a $75 add-on, drone stills around $75, and virtual staging at $15 per photo, with a combined photo, twilight, and Matterport package on a 3,500 square foot home landing around $490. That's one market's actual numbers, cited only to show what line items typically make up a marketing budget, not what Woodland Hills vendors specifically charge.

The "who pays" question is where sellers most often get surprised, because there genuinely isn't an industry standard. Some agents fold standard marketing, photography, a 3D tour, basic digital ads, into their service at no separate cost to the seller, treating it as part of what the commission covers. Others itemize everything beyond a bare-bones package, charging the seller directly for upgrades like drone video, premium print pieces, or a dedicated single-property website. Neither approach is wrong, but they produce very different out-of-pocket costs for the seller, and the only way to know which one you're getting is to read the actual listing agreement, not assume based on what a friend's agent did.

That's also why this is worth settling before you sign anything, not after the first invoice shows up. Ask directly: what's included in the base marketing plan at no extra charge, what costs extra and how much, and who approves spending before it happens. A good agent should be able to answer all three without hedging.

Context matters for sizing the plan correctly too. In the 91367 zip code right now, the median home price is around $1,199,000, with roughly 212 active listings and homes averaging about 41 days on market. A marketing plan sized for a $1.2 million listing and one sized for a $4 million hillside estate should not look the same, and a seller is well served asking an agent to explain why the proposed budget fits their specific home rather than accepting a generic package.

This is general planning information, not a quote for any specific listing. Actual costs vary by vendor, by home, and by what's already included in a given brokerage's standard service.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there a standard percentage luxury sellers should budget for marketing?

There's no fixed industry rule, but multiple 2026 sources put a typical range at roughly 0.5% to 2% of the list price for a full marketing plan, with higher-tier and trophy properties trending toward the top of that range.

Does the seller or the agent pay for marketing?

It depends entirely on the listing agreement, not on an industry standard. Some agents include standard marketing (photography, a 3D tour, basic digital ads) at no separate cost; others itemize premium add-ons as optional seller-funded upgrades. Read the agreement to know which applies.

What's typically included in a luxury marketing budget?

Common line items include professional photography, a 3D tour or Matterport-style scan, drone or twilight photography, digital advertising on platforms like Facebook and Instagram, MLS syndication, and sometimes printed brochures or postcards, plus a staging consultation.

Should a $1.2 million listing and a $4 million listing have the same marketing budget?

No. The marketing plan should be sized to the home. A seller should ask their agent to explain why a proposed budget fits their specific property rather than accepting a one-size-fits-all package.

What should I ask before signing a listing agreement about marketing costs?

Ask what's included in the base plan at no extra charge, what costs extra and the actual dollar amount, and who has to approve any spending before it happens. Get the answers in writing.

Does a bigger marketing budget guarantee a faster or higher sale?

Not automatically. A well-matched marketing plan increases exposure to real qualified buyers, but price, condition, and the current market (currently around 41 days on market in 91367) still drive the outcome more than raw marketing spend alone.
