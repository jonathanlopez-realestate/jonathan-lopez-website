Will My Home Inspection Catch a Bad Sewer Lateral When I Buy in Woodland Hills?

Jonathan Lopez, Woodland Hills Realtor · Jim Sandoval Group · CA DRE #02282844 · Updated November 2026

If you're buying a home in Woodland Hills, there's a reasonable assumption that trips up a lot of first-time and even repeat buyers: that a general home inspection covers the sewer line running from the house out to the street. In most cases, it doesn't. Standard home inspection contracts in California, including the ones most Woodland Hills buyers sign, specifically exclude the underground sewer lateral, the private pipe that connects your home's plumbing to the city's sewer main beneath the street. A general inspector will check visible plumbing fixtures, water pressure, and obvious leaks, but they are not equipped, and typically not contracted, to run a camera down a pipe buried several feet underground.

That gap matters more in some California cities than others, because a number of them have closed it with a point-of-sale requirement. At least 30 California municipalities have adopted formal ordinances requiring a certified lateral inspection, or a compliance certificate, before escrow can close. Oakland is a well-known example: the city mandates a video inspection of the lateral prior to any property transfer. Los Angeles is not one of these cities. There is no dedicated point-of-sale sewer lateral ordinance here, which means nothing in the standard LA transaction automatically tells you whether the pipe underground is sound.

It would be reasonable to assume the one report the city does require fills that gap. It doesn't. Every residential seller in the City of Los Angeles has to apply for and deliver a Report of Residential Property Records and Pending Special Assessment Liens, sometimes called the RPR or Form 9, under LA Municipal Code Section 96.300, before the sale can close. It costs $70.85 and it's a real, mandatory document. But its purpose is narrow and specific: it reports the property's zoning classification, permitted use and occupancy records, and any pending special assessment liens recorded against it. It says nothing about the condition of the plumbing, sewer or otherwise. A buyer who sees "the city report" land in their escrow file can easily assume the infrastructure side of the house has been checked. It hasn't. The same pattern shows up in the required Natural Hazard Disclosure report: mandatory, useful for fire/flood/seismic zones, and still silent on sewer laterals.

Ownership of the lateral itself is also worth understanding before you close, not after. In Los Angeles, the property owner is responsible for the full house connection sewer, from the building's foundation all the way to its connection point with the public main under the street, including the segment that runs beneath the public right-of-way in front of the house. The city maintains the main line in the street; you maintain everything on your side of that connection. That responsibility transfers to you the moment you close, with or without a sewer certification in hand.

So what should a Woodland Hills buyer actually do? The practical answer is to treat a sewer lateral scope the same way you'd treat a roof or foundation check: as an optional, specialized add-on to your standard inspection, done during your inspection contingency period while you still have real leverage. A private CCTV sewer scope in the LA market typically runs in the $300 to $500 range for a full pre-purchase inspection, tracing the line from an interior cleanout out to the city main, with a timestamped video report that holds up in an escrow negotiation. A narrower lateral-only scope, covering just the branch line rather than the full run, tends to run closer to $150 to $300. Either way, it's a modest cost relative to what it protects against: a full sewer line replacement in LA County averages around $20,000 according to current market cost data, and that's the kind of repair nobody wants to discover is necessary six months after closing, with no seller left to negotiate with.

There's a real silver lining once you do own the home. LA Sanitation runs a Sewer Lateral Repair Rebate program open to any City of LA ratepayer in good standing on their sewer service charge. It offers up to $300 back for having your lateral professionally televised by a licensed, bonded contractor, and up to $2,500 back toward an actual repair, on a first-come, first-served basis until the year's allocated funds run out. It's not a substitute for checking the pipe before you buy, since it only applies after you're already the owner and already on the hook for any repair, but it's a meaningful offset if a scope during your contingency period does turn up a problem you decide to live with and fix after closing, or if a clean scope later needs minor maintenance down the road.

None of this is meant to make a healthy, ordinary transaction feel riskier than it is. Most Woodland Hills sewer laterals are fine, and plenty of local sales close without anyone ever thinking about the pipe underground. The point is narrower: don't assume your general inspector, or the city's own required report, already checked it, because in Los Angeles, neither one does. A scope during your contingency window costs a few hundred dollars and either confirms you're fine or gives you real leverage to negotiate a repair or credit before you own the problem outright.

Locally, Movoto's Woodland Hills page (last confirmed update Oct 30, 2026) shows a $1,299,000 September median sale price, 379 active listings, and 50 days on market, up from 44 a year ago, a market with enough breathing room that asking a seller to address a sewer lateral issue, rather than walking away or absorbing it yourself, is a realistic ask. Frequently Asked Questions

Does a standard home inspection in California include the sewer lateral?

No. Most general home inspection contracts specifically exclude the underground sewer lateral. It requires a separate, specialized camera (CCTV) scope that most general inspectors don't perform as part of a standard inspection.

Does Los Angeles require a sewer lateral certification before a home can be sold?

No. At least 30 California cities, including Oakland, require a point-of-sale lateral inspection or compliance certificate. The City of Los Angeles does not have a dedicated ordinance requiring this.

Doesn't the city's required property report cover this?

No. The Report of Residential Property Records and Pending Special Assessment Liens (LAMC 96.300, $70.85, required before every LA residential closing) covers zoning, permits, and pending liens. It does not address plumbing or sewer lateral condition.

Who is responsible for the sewer lateral after I buy the home?

You are, as the property owner, for the full run from your home's foundation to its connection with the city's main under the street, including the portion beneath the public right-of-way.

How much does a sewer scope cost, and what if it finds a problem?

A pre-purchase CCTV scope typically runs $300 to $500 in the LA market. If a line needs full replacement, that repair averages around $20,000 in LA County, which is why checking during your inspection period, while you still have negotiating leverage, is worth the modest upfront cost.

Is there any help available if I find a problem after I've already bought?

Yes. LA Sanitation's Sewer Lateral Repair Rebate program offers City of LA ratepayers in good standing up to $300 back for televising the lateral and up to $2,500 back toward a repair, available on a first-come, first-served basis.
