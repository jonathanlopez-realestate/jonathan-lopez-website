Can I Still Get the Mortgage Credit Certificate Tax Credit to Buy My First Home in Woodland Hills?

Jonathan Lopez, Woodland Hills Realtor · Jim Sandoval Group · CA DRE #02282844 · Updated November 2026

If you are a first-time buyer house-hunting in Woodland Hills, you have probably come across the phrase "mortgage credit certificate" somewhere in a lender's pitch or a down-payment-assistance roundup, usually described vaguely as a tax break for first-time buyers. It is a real federal program, administered locally by the City of Los Angeles Housing Department (LAHD), and it is worth understanding precisely, both what it actually does and where it runs into real limits in this specific market.

Here is the mechanism. An MCC is not a loan, a grant, or a down payment source. It converts a fixed percentage, 20% in LAHD's program, of the mortgage interest you pay each year into a dollar-for-dollar federal income tax credit. A credit reduces what you owe the IRS directly, not just your taxable income the way a deduction does, and it keeps paying out every year you hold the loan and claim it, not just at closing. Lenders can also factor the expected credit into how they calculate your qualifying income, which can help some buyers qualify for a slightly larger loan than the raw numbers alone would support.

To qualify, LAHD's program carries several conditions. You generally cannot have held an ownership interest in a home in the past three years, except that LAHD waives the first-time buyer requirement entirely for purchases inside its own designated target-area census tracts. Household income has to fall under LAHD's limits: $125,280 for a one or two person household, $146,160 for a household of three or more, the same ceiling whether the property sits in a target or non-target area. There is a $150 application fee, and you apply through one of LAHD's participating lenders rather than directly with the city. LAHD's own MCC page does not list a separate homebuyer-education-course requirement, which is worth noting since some other MCC programs do require one. And the home itself has to be priced at or under LAHD's purchase price limit, $653,883 in a non-target area or $799,191 in a target area, for either existing housing or new construction.

That last number is where Woodland Hills buyers run into the real-world catch. Woodland Hills' current median sale price sits at $1,300,000 (Movoto, 368 active listings, 54-day average days on market), somewhere between roughly $500,000 and $646,000 above the MCC purchase price cap depending on whether a given property sits in a target or non-target census tract. That gap means the program, even when fully funded, is not built for the typical Woodland Hills home. It reaches the lower tier of local inventory instead: smaller condos, townhomes, and the relative handful of homes that still trade under roughly $650,000 to $800,000, not the median detached house most buyers picture when they think "first home in Woodland Hills."

There is a second, more immediate catch. LAHD's own MCC page states its status plainly: "This Program is Currently out of Funding." MCC funding allocations get replenished periodically, so this is not necessarily a permanent shutdown, but it means a buyer cannot currently walk into escrow assuming this credit will be available to claim. Anyone seriously counting on it needs to check directly with LAHD or a participating lender for the current funding status before it factors into a purchase decision, not after.

It is also worth knowing this is a City of Los Angeles program specifically, which matters because Woodland Hills sits inside LA city limits. Los Angeles County runs a separate MCC program through the Los Angeles County Development Authority (LACDA) for other incorporated cities within the county, with its own income limits ($135,120 to $157,640) and purchase price caps ($679,847 to $830,924). The two programs are not interchangeable, and a buyer researching this online can easily land on LACDA's figures while shopping for a home inside the city, which is the wrong program for a Woodland Hills address.

None of this means the credit is worthless for Woodland Hills buyers, it means it is a real but narrow tool: useful mainly for a first-time buyer targeting a condo or a smaller home priced under roughly $650,000 to $800,000, contingent on funding actually being open when you need it, and worth confirming early rather than building a financing plan around an assumption.

This is general program information, not tax advice for your specific household, and not a guarantee of current funding or eligibility. Frequently Asked Questions

What does the Mortgage Credit Certificate actually do?

It converts 20% of the annual mortgage interest you pay into a dollar-for-dollar federal income tax credit, claimed every year you hold the loan, not a one-time payment or a down payment source.

Is a tax credit the same as a tax deduction?

No. A deduction lowers your taxable income; a credit lowers your actual tax bill dollar for dollar, which generally makes it more valuable per dollar than an equivalent deduction.

Can I use the MCC on a median-priced Woodland Hills home?

Not currently. LAHD's purchase price limits are $653,883 (non-target area) and $799,191 (target area), and Woodland Hills' median sale price is $1,300,000 (Movoto), roughly $500,000 to $646,000 above those caps. It would apply to a lower-priced condo or smaller home, not the typical median-priced house.

Is the program actually available right now?

No. LAHD's own MCC page states directly: "This Program is Currently out of Funding." Confirm current status directly with LAHD or a participating lender before relying on it.

Is there an exception to the first-time buyer rule?

Yes. LAHD waives the 3-year no-prior-ownership requirement entirely for purchases inside its designated target-area census tracts, though the purchase price cap in those tracts ($799,191) is still well under Woodland Hills' median.

Do I have to meet an income limit, and is there a fee?

Yes to both. Household income must stay under $125,280 for one to two people or $146,160 for three or more, and there is a $150 application fee paid through an LAHD participating lender.

Is the City of Los Angeles program the same as the Los Angeles County program?

No. LA County's MCC, run by LACDA, serves other cities in the county, not the City of Los Angeles, and uses different income and purchase price limits ($135,120 to $157,640 income, $679,847 to $830,924 purchase price). Woodland Hills buyers fall under the city program, not the county one.
