Do I Still Need My Own Agent If I'm Buying New Construction in Woodland Hills?

Jonathan Lopez, Woodland Hills Realtor | Jim Sandoval Group | CA DRE #02282844 · Updated September 2026

New construction feels different from a resale purchase. There's a model home, a friendly on-site sales agent, and a straightforward-looking contract. That can make it easy to assume you don't need your own representation the way you would buying a resale home. The reality is closer to the opposite: it's one of the situations where having your own agent matters most, and where timing matters in a way most buyers don't find out until it's too late.

The Builder's Agent Works for the Builder

The person staffing the model home, however friendly and helpful, is a licensed real estate professional whose fiduciary duty runs to the developer who employs them. Their job is to get the builder the highest price and best terms on that transaction, the same as a listing agent's job in any resale sale. California does permit dual agency, one agent or brokerage representing both sides, but only with written disclosure of the arrangement and consent from both parties. Even with proper disclosure, an agent who's paid by and answers to the builder has a structural incentive that doesn't point toward getting you, the buyer, the best deal.

The Part That Trips Buyers Up: Timing

Most builders require a buyer's agent to either accompany the buyer on their first visit to the sales office or model home, or formally register the buyer within a short window afterward, commonly 24 to 48 hours from first contact, though this is entirely at the builder's discretion and some are less flexible than others. If a buyer tours the model home alone, calls the sales office directly, or fills out a contact form on the builder's website before identifying a buyer's agent, the builder typically records that buyer's file as unrepresented. Once that happens, many builders won't add an agent to the file later for that community, even if the buyer decides afterward that they want representation. This is the single most common way buyers end up without an agent on a new construction purchase, not because they decided against it, but because they didn't know the clock was already running on their first visit.

Why This Usually Costs Nothing Extra

Builders build a sales commission into their overall marketing and sales budget regardless of whether a buyer brings outside representation. That budget typically covers both the builder's own sales staff and a cooperating buyer's agent commission, if one is properly registered. In practice, this means a buyer who brings their own agent from the start is generally not paying anything the builder wasn't already prepared to pay out as part of that same transaction, the money simply goes to an agent working for the buyer instead of staying entirely with the builder's side. This is the same underlying commission structure that changed shape after the 2024 NAR settlement on the resale side, worth knowing about either way you're buying: what buyer's agent compensation actually looks like now.

What a Buyer's Agent Actually Does Differently Here

Beyond the fiduciary duty question, a buyer's agent reviews the builder's purchase agreement (which is written by and for the builder, not a standard state association form the way most resale contracts are), flags upgrade and lot-premium pricing against comparable resale value, and can push back on construction timelines, deposit terms, and included-versus-upgrade specifications in ways a builder's own sales staff has no incentive to raise.

Where This Applies Right Now

Woodland Hills' current for-sale inventory is almost entirely resale. The one large proposed new-construction project locally, the Woodland Hills Country Club redevelopment, is currently blocked pending litigation and not available to buyers. This guidance is for buyers looking at new construction anywhere in the broader San Fernando Valley, not a claim that a specific Woodland Hills new-build community is active today.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the builder's sales agent represent me as the buyer?

No. The agent staffing a builder's model home or sales office has a fiduciary duty to the developer who employs them, not to the buyer, even though their job is to be helpful and answer buyer questions during the visit.

Is it legal for one agent to represent both the builder and the buyer in California?

Yes, California permits dual agency, but only with written disclosure of the dual role and consent from both the buyer and seller. Even when disclosed, a dual agent can't advocate as strongly for either side as a dedicated buyer's agent would.

How soon do I need to bring my own agent to a new construction sales office?

Most builders require a buyer's agent to accompany the buyer on the first visit or formally register the buyer within a short window afterward, commonly 24 to 48 hours from first contact. Policies vary by builder and aren't guaranteed.

What happens if I tour a model home alone before bringing an agent?

Many builders will record the buyer's file as unrepresented once that first unaccompanied contact happens, whether through an in-person visit, a phone call, or an online contact form, and won't add a buyer's agent to that file later for that community.

Does using my own agent cost more when buying new construction?

Usually not. Builders typically budget a sales commission into the transaction regardless of outside representation, and that budget generally covers a buyer's agent commission if one is properly registered in time, rather than adding a separate cost on top.

What does a buyer's agent actually do differently on a new construction purchase?

Reviews the builder's own purchase agreement (not a standard resale contract form), evaluates whether upgrade and lot-premium pricing is in line with comparable resale value, and can push back on deposit terms, timelines, and included-versus-upgrade specifications.
