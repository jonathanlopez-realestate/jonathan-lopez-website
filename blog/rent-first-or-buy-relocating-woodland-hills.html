Should I Rent First or Buy When Relocating to Woodland Hills?

Jonathan Lopez, Jim Sandoval Group | Woodland Hills Realtor | CA DRE #02282844 · Updated September 2026

Relocating to Woodland Hills raises a practical question right away: should you rent first and learn the area, or buy as soon as you find the right home? The local numbers show how big the monthly cost of waiting can be, and what the buy side looks like today.

What Renting a House in Woodland Hills Costs

Apartments.com's Woodland Hills house rental page (updated August 1, 2026) lists 214 single-family homes with an average rent of $8,044 per month, about $96,500 a year. Listings ranged from $1,800 to $18,000 a month. An average across that range is a rough guide only. Your rent depends on the size, condition, and location of the home, and a listing average can be pulled upward by higher-end properties.

What the Buy Side Looks Like

Movoto's data, updated September 30, 2026, shows a median sale price of $1,348,500 for August 2026, 421 active listings, 71 of them price-reduced, and an average of 48 days on market versus 45 a year earlier. Redfin's July 2026 data put the median sale price at $1,287,061, with homes selling at 99.5% of list price on average. The sources use different methods and time frames, so the figures are not identical, but both show an active market with room to shop.

Why Renting First Can Make Sense

A short rental lets you test commute routes, neighborhoods, and school and daily-life fit before you commit to a purchase. That can matter a lot if you are moving from out of state and have only seen the area in photos. It can also make sense if your job or timeline is not settled. If you do plan to buy remotely later, see how buying a Woodland Hills home remotely works.

What Renting First Costs You

Rent is a recurring cost that does not build ownership, and the search market keeps moving while you rent. If you expect to stay several years, the length of your stay and your down payment often matter more than a single month's rent figure. If you are moving from another California home, whether you can bring your property tax base may also change the math.

Bottom Line

There is no universal answer. Run both numbers for your specific situation: how long you will stay, how much you can put down, and how sure you are of the job and the neighborhood. A rent versus buy comparison with real comparable sales is the way to make that call.

This is general information, not financial advice. Confirm loan terms with your lender and consult a tax professional about tax questions.
