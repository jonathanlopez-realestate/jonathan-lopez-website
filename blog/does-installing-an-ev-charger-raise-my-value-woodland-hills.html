Does Installing an EV Charger Actually Raise My Woodland Hills Home's Value?

Jonathan Lopez, Woodland Hills Realtor · Jim Sandoval Group · CA DRE #02282844 · Updated November 2026

Woodland Hills has plenty of EV ownership among its garage-and-driveway housing stock, and sellers in that position often ask the same question before listing: does putting in a home charger actually raise what the house is worth? It's a reasonable comparison to make against a new roof or a pool, upgrades that show up as real line items in an appraisal or a buyer's offer. The honest answer, based on the best data available, is that an EV charger helps in a different way than those upgrades do, and it's worth understanding the distinction before spending money expecting a price bump that the data doesn't actually support.

The most useful research on this question comes from Zillow's own research team, which in 2022 published an analysis of 3.1 million home sales from 2020 and 2021, examining which features mentioned in listing descriptions correlated with faster sales and higher prices. EV charging stations were one of the features studied, and the result was specific: homes whose listings mentioned an EV charging station sold about 9.5 days faster than comparable homes without one, tied with drought-resistant landscaping for the single fastest-selling feature in the entire study. Critically, the same analysis found no measurable price premium attached to the charger. That's an unusual and honest result worth sitting with: a real, data-backed selling advantage that shows up in speed, not in dollars.

It's worth being clear about what that study can and can't prove. It measured a correlation between a feature mentioned in a listing description and how quickly that listing sold, across a very large national sample, not a controlled experiment proving the charger itself caused the faster sale. A more recent, larger-scale repeat of the same study was not found during this research, so the 2020-2021 sales window is the most current large-sample data available on this specific question, and that's disclosed here rather than assumed to still hold exactly in today's market.

Speed still has real value in Woodland Hills' current conditions. Movoto's citywide data (search-snapshot accessed November 12, 2026) shows homes averaging 54 days on market against a $1,300,000 median sale price, with 368 active listings competing for buyer attention. Shaving more than a week off that average time on market is a genuine outcome for a seller, even without a corresponding bump in sale price, since it reduces carrying costs (mortgage, insurance, utilities) and the market perception risk that comes with a listing sitting visibly longer than its competition.

Cost is the other side of the decision. Installing a Level 2 home charger, the kind that charges meaningfully faster than a standard wall outlet, typically runs $750 to $2,500 installed as of 2026, according to multiple independent installer and industry cost sources. The charger unit itself is usually $350 to $700, with the rest going to a licensed electrician and permits. The biggest driver of where you land in that range is your home's electrical panel capacity and how far the charger needs to run from it: a simple plug-in installation at an existing 240-volt outlet can come in well under $1,000, while a job requiring a full panel upgrade can run several thousand dollars more. Before spending on this ahead of a listing, it's worth getting an actual electrician's quote for your specific panel rather than assuming the low end of any published range.

Putting the two pieces together: an EV charger is a reasonable upgrade to make if you already need one for your own use, or if your home's electrical setup makes the install cheap and straightforward, since the days-on-market benefit is real and well-documented. It's a weaker case purely as a pre-listing investment made solely to try to raise the sale price, since the data doesn't support that outcome happening. That's a different calculus than a new roof or an ADU, where the evidence points more directly to added dollar value, and sellers deciding where to spend a limited pre-listing budget should weigh that distinction honestly rather than treating every upgrade as the same kind of investment.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does an EV charger actually raise my home's sale price?

No measurable price premium was found in Zillow's 2022 analysis of 3.1 million home sales. The documented benefit is speed, not price.

How much faster do homes with an EV charger sell?

About 9.5 days faster than comparable homes, according to Zillow's research, tied with drought-resistant landscaping for the fastest-selling feature studied.

Is that Zillow data still current?

It's the largest and most recent large-sample study available on this specific question (published April 18, 2022, covering 2020-2021 sales). A newer repeat study of the same scale was not found, so the result should be read as the best available evidence, not a guarantee for today's exact market.

How much does it cost to install a home EV charger?

A Level 2 home charger typically installs for $750 to $2,500, according to multiple independent 2026 installer cost sources, with your electrical panel's capacity as the biggest cost driver.

Should I install a charger just to help my home sell?

It's a stronger case if you already use one or your panel makes installation cheap. As a pure pre-listing investment made only to raise price, the data doesn't support a return the way a new roof or ADU does.

Does Woodland Hills' current market make speed-on-market matter more right now?

Yes. Homes here are averaging 54 days on market against a $1,300,000 median sale price (Movoto, citywide, accessed November 12, 2026), so shaving over a week off that timeline is a meaningful outcome even without a price increase.
