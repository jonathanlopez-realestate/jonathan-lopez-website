Does the Small Estate Affidavit Actually Help You Skip Probate on a Woodland Hills House?

Jonathan Lopez, Woodland Hills Realtor | Jim Sandoval Group | CA DRE #02282844 · Updated September 2026

If you've searched anything about California probate shortcuts recently, you may have come across a claim that the "small estate" threshold jumped to $239,700 for 2026. It's worth stopping on that number for a second, because it's wrong, and it's a good example of why checking a legal figure against an actual court document matters more than trusting whatever a search result summarizes.

The Real Number, Verified Against an Actual Court Form

California's small estate affidavit process, under Probate Code Section 13100, lets an heir collect a deceased person's personal property, bank accounts, vehicles, final paychecks, that kind of thing, directly from the institution holding it, without opening a full probate case. The dollar limit for using it has moved over time: $166,250 for deaths before April 1, 2022, then $184,500 through March 31, 2025, then $208,850 for deaths on or after April 1, 2025. That $208,850 figure is confirmed directly on the official small estate affidavit form used by California courts, and it stays in effect through 2026 and 2027. The law only allows the Judicial Council to adjust these amounts every three years, so the next change isn't scheduled until April 1, 2028. At least one California estate planning firm that had originally published the incorrect $239,700 figure has since issued a public correction confirming there's no such 2026 increase.

Why This Matters More Than It Sounds Like

Here's the part that actually affects most people dealing with an inherited Woodland Hills home: the $208,850 small estate affidavit only covers personal property. It does not cover real estate. If a house is part of the estate, a completely different rule applies.

The Rule That Actually Touches Real Estate

A newer California law, Assembly Bill 2016, effective April 1, 2025, created a separate streamlined court process specifically for a decedent's primary residence, formally called a Petition to Determine Succession to Real Property. It works similarly to the small estate affidavit in some ways, there's still a required 40-day waiting period after the date of death, and it still avoids the cost and time of full probate administration. But it has its own, separate dollar limit: the primary residence has to be worth $750,000 or less to qualify.

Why $750,000 Doesn't Cover Most Woodland Hills Homes

This is where local reality matters more than the general legal explainer you'll find on most websites. Woodland Hills home values have been running well above that $750,000 line. Local market data from Movoto puts the 91367 median sale price at $1,348,500 as of August 2026. That means a typical inherited house in this area, even a modest one by local standards, is likely to exceed the threshold for this streamlined real property petition. In practice, most families inheriting a Woodland Hills home will still need to go through full probate to transfer the real estate itself, even in cases where a separate bank account might qualify for the simpler small estate affidavit. This is a similar theme to the general probate timeline and Prop 19 considerations covered here and how intestate succession is decided when there's no will, dollar thresholds and legal categories matter, but local home values decide which ones actually apply.

What This Actually Means If You've Inherited a House Here

None of this is meant to be discouraging, it's meant to save you from assuming a shortcut applies before checking. The two processes described here, the personal property affidavit and the real property petition, are genuinely useful tools, they just have hard dollar cutoffs, not gradual phase-outs, and local home values in this area put most houses on the wrong side of the real estate cutoff specifically. If you're not sure whether your situation would need full probate, a streamlined petition, or something in between, that's a question worth answering early, not after months have already gone by assuming one path or another.

The other piece worth getting early, separate from the legal process itself, is an honest, current opinion of what the house is actually worth. That number affects which process applies, what a probate attorney will tell you your options are, and eventually what selling the property, if that's the plan, will actually look like financially. I can put together that kind of value opinion for an inherited Woodland Hills property at any point in the process, whether or not a decision has been made yet about whether or when to sell.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the small estate affidavit threshold really $208,850, not $239,700?

Yes. $208,850 is the confirmed figure on California's official small estate affidavit court form for deaths on or after April 1, 2025, and it remains in effect through March 31, 2028. The $239,700 figure that's circulated online has been publicly corrected by at least one law firm that had originally published it in error.

Does the small estate affidavit cover a house?

No. It only covers personal property, things like bank accounts, vehicles, and similar assets. Real estate has to go through either full probate or a separate, dedicated process.

What's the separate process for a house?

It's called a Petition to Determine Succession to Real Property (sometimes called the primary residence petition), created under a 2025 law. It applies only to a decedent's primary residence valued at $750,000 or less, and it still requires a 40-day waiting period after death.

Does the $750,000 threshold cover most Woodland Hills homes?

Usually not. With the 91367 median sale price recently around $1,348,500, most inherited homes in this area exceed the $750,000 limit for the streamlined real property petition, meaning full probate is typically still required for the house itself.

If my situation doesn't qualify for either shortcut, does that mean it'll be expensive and slow?

Not necessarily, but it does mean planning around full probate's actual timeline and costs rather than assuming a shortcut will apply. A probate attorney can lay out the realistic cost and timeline for your specific estate.

Should I get the house valued before deciding on a probate strategy?

It's a reasonable early step. Knowing the actual current value helps you and your attorney figure out which process realistically applies, and it's useful information regardless of whether or when you decide to sell.
