Will My Student Loans Still Count Against Me When I Apply for a Mortgage in Woodland Hills?

Jonathan Lopez, Woodland Hills Realtor · Jim Sandoval Group · CA DRE #02282844 · Updated November 2026

A lot of first-time buyers in Woodland Hills carry student loan debt, and a lot of them assume that if their loan is deferred, in forbearance, or on an income-driven repayment plan showing a $0 bill, it simply won't factor into their mortgage math. For most buyers, that assumption is wrong, and which agency ends up underwriting the loan can change the answer by hundreds of dollars a month in qualifying debt.

The baseline rule. Every mortgage program has to account for existing student loan debt somehow, even when the actual bill is $0 right now. Lenders don't get to simply ignore a deferred balance, because once repayment resumes, it becomes a real monthly obligation competing with the new mortgage payment.

How Fannie Mae conventional loans handle it. Under Fannie Mae's Selling Guide, Section B3-6-05 (current version dated August 5, 2026), a student loan that's deferred, in forbearance, or shows no documented payment amount gets counted at 1% of the outstanding balance per month, unless the lender can document the actual amortizing payment instead. On a $40,000 balance, that 1% rule adds roughly $400 a month to the buyer's debt-to-income ratio. There's one genuine exception: if the borrower is on an income-driven repayment (IDR) plan, like IBR, PAYE, or SAVE, and can document an actual required payment of $0, Fannie Mae will let the lender qualify the loan using that $0 figure.

How Freddie Mac conventional loans differ. Freddie Mac does not extend that same $0 exception. When a borrower's reported student loan payment is $0, whether from deferment, forbearance, or a $0 IDR payment, Freddie Mac's Single-Family Seller/Servicer Guide requires the lender to substitute 0.5% of the outstanding balance instead. On that same $40,000 balance, that works out to roughly $200 a month, lower than Fannie Mae's 1% fallback, but still a real number added to the ratio even when the borrower's actual bill is zero.

How FHA loans handle it. FHA replaced an older flat 1% rule with a 0.5%-of-balance substitute under HUD Handbook 4000.1. FHA uses a borrower's actual reported payment when one exists above $0. When the loan is deferred, in forbearance, or reports $0, FHA counts 0.5% of the balance, the same approach Freddie Mac uses, and more favorable than Fannie Mae's 1% fallback for a buyer whose loan isn't on a qualifying IDR plan.

Why this matters at Woodland Hills' price point. At the area's current $1,300,000 median sale price (Movoto, updated November 5, 2026), a buyer's debt-to-income ratio is already working with a sizable loan amount. A few hundred dollars a month in additional counted debt, the gap between a $0, a 0.5%, and a 1% calculation on the same student loan balance, can be the difference between qualifying at a target price and needing to look at a lower one, or between a conventional loan and an FHA loan producing a meaningfully different approval outcome on the exact same income and the exact same student loan balance.

The recertification trap. Income-driven repayment plans require periodic recertification, and if a borrower's recertification comes due on or before their first scheduled mortgage payment and the renewed payment amount hasn't been approved yet, Freddie Mac's guide requires the lender to count at least 0.5% of the balance regardless of what the expected renewed payment will be. Recertifying an IDR plan well ahead of a mortgage application, rather than letting it lapse into a pending renewal, removes this avoidable risk.

The real takeaway. Which agency, Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, or FHA, ends up underwriting a buyer's loan isn't usually the buyer's first consideration, but for someone carrying meaningful student loan debt, it can be the single biggest lever in whether they qualify at their target price. This is exactly the kind of detail a real lender conversation surfaces early, before a buyer falls in love with a home their file might not actually support yet.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a deferred student loan actually count against me if I'm not paying anything right now?

Usually yes. Fannie Mae counts 1% of the balance per month on a deferred or undocumented loan, while Freddie Mac and FHA count 0.5%, unless the lender can document an actual, lower amortizing payment instead.

Is there any way to get a $0 student loan payment counted as $0 on my mortgage application?

Only on a Fannie Mae conventional loan, and only with a documented income-driven repayment plan showing an actual required payment of $0. Freddie Mac and FHA still substitute 0.5% of the balance even in that situation.

Does it matter whether I'm on IBR, PAYE, or SAVE?

What matters to the lender is the actual documented monthly payment your plan currently requires, not which specific income-driven program you're enrolled in. A $0 documented payment under any qualifying plan can work for the Fannie Mae exception; a payment above $0 gets counted at that actual amount across all three agencies.

How much difference can this actually make on a Woodland Hills-sized loan?

On a $40,000 student loan balance alone, the gap between a documented $0 payment and a 1% fallback calculation is about $400 a month in counted debt. Applied against a loan at Woodland Hills' $1,300,000 median price point, that can shift how much home a buyer qualifies for by a meaningful amount.

Should I pay off my student loans before buying a home here?

Not necessarily, and that's a lender conversation, not a real estate one. The right move depends on the loan balance, the interest rate, the buyer's other debts and reserves, and which loan program fits best. A payoff that drains a buyer's down payment or reserves isn't automatically the better move.

What should I do before I start touring homes if I have student loan debt?

Get a real pre-approval from a lender who asks for your student loan documentation upfront and runs the calculation correctly for the loan program being considered, rather than estimating. If you're on an IDR plan, make sure your recertification is current before you apply.
