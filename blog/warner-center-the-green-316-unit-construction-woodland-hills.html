Construction Just Went Vertical on a 316-Unit Apartment Tower in Warner Center. What It Means for Woodland Hills Owners and Buyers

Jonathan Lopez, Woodland Hills Realtor · Jim Sandoval Group · CA DRE #02282844 · Updated November 2026

It's hard to miss if you drive through Warner Center this month: a tower crane now stands at the corner of Califa Street and Variel Avenue, the clearest sign yet that one of the area's bigger housing projects has moved from paperwork into actual construction. The project is The Green, a seven-story apartment building from Los Angeles-based developer Elysian Housing and New York-based Capstone Equities, and as of early November 2026 it has officially "gone vertical," meaning the structure is now rising above ground level rather than sitting at the foundation stage.

What's actually being built. The Green sits on a 2.7-acre site at 21155 W. Califa Street and, once complete, will hold 316 apartments, a mix of one-, two-, and three-bedroom units, plus two manager's units, above parking for 300 vehicles. The design comes from architecture firm Koning Eizenberg. Every unit aside from the two manager's units is reserved for low- and moderate-income renters, financed through a mix of affordable-housing programs described in earlier reporting on the project. The City approved the development in December 2025, and construction has been actively underway through 2026.

Why it moved so fast. One detail is worth understanding on its own: this project cleared City approval relatively quickly because it qualified for Los Angeles's Executive Directive 1, a streamlined, largely ministerial review process the City built specifically to fast-track 100%-affordable housing developments, cutting out most of the discretionary public hearings a typical project has to go through. That stands in sharp contrast to a very different residential project a few miles away in the same general corridor: the 398-home Woodland Hills Country Club redevelopment, which the City Council voted to block in August 2026 over fire-hazard-zone and wildlife-habitat concerns, and which is now the subject of an active lawsuit filed by housing advocates in November 2026, with the state's own housing agency separately pressing the City for a written explanation of the rejection. Same general housing-shortage pressure, same part of the Valley, two sharply different outcomes, driven largely by project type and which approval pathway applied.

Not an isolated project. The Green isn't a one-off. Trade coverage describes it as one of roughly half a dozen similar affordable or multifamily developments currently under construction across Warner Center, consistent with the City's Warner Center 2035 Plan, a specific plan adopted years ago that deliberately encourages higher-density residential development inside that commercial corridor, as distinct from the surrounding hillside single-family streets that make up most of Jonathan's west Woodland Hills farm area.

What this actually means for a home buyer or seller. For anyone outside the immediate Warner Center footprint, this project doesn't create new competing inventory for a single-family home sale. It's rental housing, income-restricted, built on a commercial-zoned parcel, serving a different pool of renters than someone shopping for a detached house. What it does signal clearly is where the City's near-term density growth is actually landing: concentrated inside the Warner Center core, not spreading into the surrounding residential hillside streets. For anyone whose home, or whose home search, sits close to that corridor, that means ongoing construction traffic, cranes, and delivery trucks for a stretch of months as several of these projects continue rising at once, worth factoring into showing schedules or a buyer's day-to-day commute expectations if a search is centered near Warner Center specifically.

Where the market sits right now. Woodland Hills' 91367 zip code currently shows a $1,199,000 median sale price, roughly 212 active listings, and a 41-day average time on market (Movoto). None of that number is being driven by The Green's construction; it's a separate rental product and a separate buyer pool entirely. But for a buyer specifically weighing a home close to Warner Center for its walkability to retail, dining, and transit, understanding that density, not new single-family supply, is the direction that specific pocket of the market is moving in matters for setting realistic expectations.

The honest limits here. This post does not claim The Green, or any of the other projects near it, directly raises or lowers nearby single-family home values; no study specific to this project's effect on surrounding home prices was found, and none is asserted. It also does not have a confirmed completion date to share; none was found in sourcing.

Frequently Asked Questions

What exactly is being built at 21155 Califa Street in Warner Center?

The Green, a seven-story, 316-unit apartment building from Elysian Housing and Capstone Equities, with parking for 300 vehicles. Every unit except two manager's units is reserved for low- and moderate-income renters. It was approved in December 2025 and went vertical (visible crane, structure rising above ground) in November 2026.

Will this new building compete with my Woodland Hills home if I'm selling?

No, not directly. It's income-restricted rental housing on a commercial-zoned parcel in Warner Center, a different product serving a different pool of renters than someone buying a detached single-family home.

Why did this project move forward quickly while the Woodland Hills Country Club project is still blocked and in litigation?

The Green qualified for Los Angeles's Executive Directive 1, a fast-track, largely ministerial approval process built specifically for 100%-affordable housing projects. The Country Club project is a market-rate, for-sale single-family development, a different project type that went through the City's normal discretionary review and was voted down over fire-hazard-zone and habitat concerns, now contested in court.

What is the Warner Center 2035 Plan, and does it affect my neighborhood?

It's a City-adopted specific plan that deliberately steers higher-density housing growth into the Warner Center commercial corridor rather than the surrounding hillside residential streets. If your home sits in those hillside streets rather than inside the Warner Center core itself, this plan isn't rezoning your block, but it does explain why multifamily construction keeps concentrating in that one part of west Woodland Hills.

Should I expect construction disruption if I live or I'm searching near Warner Center?

Likely yes for a while. Coverage describes roughly half a dozen similar projects currently under construction in the area, so expect ongoing crane activity, construction traffic, and delivery trucks in that specific corridor for a period of months, separate from the normal pace of the broader Woodland Hills market.

Does this change Woodland Hills' overall home prices?

Not directly, and this post doesn't claim that it does. The 91367 zip code's current median sale price ($1,199,000), active listings (about 212), and average time on market (41 days, Movoto) reflect the broader single-family resale market, a separate dynamic from this one rental project's construction.
