What Happens to My Woodland Hills Listing If My Agent Leaves the Brokerage?

Jonathan Lopez, Woodland Hills Realtor · Jim Sandoval Group · CA DRE #02282844 · Updated November 2026

It's one of the more unsettling moments in a Woodland Hills listing: midway through marketing your home, your agent calls to say they're leaving the brokerage, retiring, or joining a different company across town. Who actually represents you now? The honest answer catches a lot of sellers off guard, and it comes straight from how California structures real estate licensing.

The legal structure most sellers don't realize is there. A listing agreement, the standard C.A.R. Residential Listing Agreement most Woodland Hills sellers sign, is a contract between the seller and the broker, not between the seller and the individual salesperson who shows up to your open houses. California law builds in that separation deliberately. Business and Professions Code Section 10130 makes it unlawful for anyone to act as a real estate broker or salesperson without the required license. Section 10131 then defines a real estate salesperson specifically as a person who is licensed and retained by a broker to perform real estate acts on that broker's behalf. In plain terms, your agent works for, and under, the brokerage. The brokerage, or the supervising broker, is the party actually bound to your listing contract, not the individual agent personally.

What that means the moment your agent leaves. When an agent departs a brokerage, whether to retire, move to a competing company, or start their own, standard practice is consistent: the brokerage retains the listing, not the departing agent. In practice, that usually means the broker reassigns your file to another agent inside the same office, who may introduce themselves for the first time well after your home is already on the market. Your marketing, your MLS listing, and your showing schedule don't automatically stop, but the specific person managing them day to day can change with very little warning to you.

You have real options, not just the reassignment. Sellers are not locked into accepting whoever gets assigned. California agency law gives sellers a unilateral right to cancel a listing agreement, a right the broker does not have a mirror version of. If you're unhappy with a reassignment, or you'd simply prefer to work with someone else, most brokers will sign a mutual, written Cancellation of Listing form, particularly if there's no offer pending yet. Ask for it directly and in writing rather than assuming you're stuck for the remainder of the listing term.

One thing you can't do: follow your old agent automatically. If you'd genuinely rather leave with your departing agent to their new brokerage, that requires a mutual cancellation of your current listing first. Your previous agent cannot keep representing you under a brokerage they no longer work for; their license now operates under their new broker, and a fresh listing agreement would need to be signed with that new brokerage if you want to go that route.

What to actually do if this happens to you. If your agent tells you they're leaving, the first call should be to the broker or office manager, not a side conversation with your departing agent about following them. Ask three things directly: who specifically will be handling your file going forward, whether your current pricing and marketing strategy carries over unchanged, and whether you have the option to request a different agent inside the same office if the assigned replacement isn't the right fit for you.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is my Woodland Hills listing agreement with my agent personally, or with the brokerage?

With the brokerage. Under California Business and Professions Code Section 10131, your agent is licensed and retained by a broker to act on that broker's behalf. The broker (or the brokerage entity) is the party actually bound to your signed listing contract, not the salesperson personally.

What happens if my agent retires or switches companies while my home is listed?

Standard practice is that the brokerage keeps the listing. The broker typically reassigns your file to another agent in the same office. Your listing itself, the MLS entry, the marketing, generally continues without a gap, but the specific person handling it day to day can change.

Can I request a different agent within the same brokerage if I don't like who's reassigned to me?

You can ask. Brokers often have some flexibility here, especially early in a listing with no offer pending. There's no guaranteed right to a specific replacement agent, but it's a reasonable request to make directly to the broker.

Can I cancel my listing and follow my agent to their new brokerage?

Only if your current brokerage agrees to a mutual, written Cancellation of Listing first. Your former agent cannot keep representing you under a brokerage they no longer work for since their license now operates under the new broker; a new listing agreement would need to be signed with the new brokerage.

Does a change in agent affect my MLS listing or my current marketing?

Not automatically. The listing itself stays active under the brokerage. What can change is responsiveness and continuity if the handoff to a new agent isn't managed well, which is exactly why confirming the plan directly with the broker matters.

What should I ask for in writing if my agent tells me they're leaving?

Ask the broker to confirm, in writing, who is taking over your file, whether your pricing and marketing plan carries over unchanged, and what your options are if you'd prefer a different agent or want to explore canceling the listing.
