Should I Sell My Woodland Hills Home As-Is or Fix It Up First?

Jonathan Lopez, Jim Sandoval Group | Woodland Hills Realtor | CA DRE #02282844 · Updated October 2026

If you are weighing whether to sell your Woodland Hills home as-is or spend money on repairs first, the honest answer starts with the size of the gap between the two. That gap is what you are really deciding on.

What the Discount Can Look Like

HomeLight's California as-is guide (published March 19, 2026) quotes expert analysis that homes sold as-is in the area typically sell for 20% to 30% less than regular listings. The same article says that with minimal improvements like paint, carpet, and landscaping, sellers could increase value by about 5%, and describes a potential return of $1.25 or more for every $1 invested. Those are general figures, not Woodland Hills data, and condition, location, and buyer pool all move them.

For scale, Movoto reports a Woodland Hills median sale price of $1,348,500 for August 2026 (data updated September 30, 2026). A 20% to 30% discount on that number would be roughly $269,700 to $404,550. That arithmetic is only an illustration of how large the stakes can be, not a quote for any specific house.

As-Is Still Comes With Disclosure

Selling as-is does not remove a California seller's disclosure duties. Houzeo's California as-is guide (published and updated September 30, 2026) notes that sellers must complete the California Seller Disclosure Form (the Transfer Disclosure Statement), and says that hiding a defect can let the buyer withdraw and can lead to a lawsuit. HomeLight makes the same basic point: the TDS covers material facts including structural issues, hazardous materials, flooding, and fire damage. For more detail, see what you are legally required to disclose when selling a Woodland Hills home.

Buyers Can Still Inspect

As-is generally means the seller will not make repairs, not that the buyer gives up inspection rights. Houzeo states that a buyer can back out of an as-is contract during the inspection period without penalty or losing the deposit, and can negotiate repairs or credits in later amendments. HomeLight adds that buyers who agree to waive the inspection contingency cannot back out over what they find. So an as-is listing in a market like this one still gets inspected, and the findings still shape the final price. If you want to see that problem before buyers do, a pre-listing inspection can show what a buyer's inspector will likely find.

Where Woodland Hills Buyers Stand Right Now

Movoto's September 30, 2026 update shows 421 active Woodland Hills listings, 71 of them price-reduced, and 48 days on market versus 45 a year earlier. With that much inventory, buyers can compare condition across many homes, which is one reason the condition gap can show up in the price. A home that needs work competes against homes that do not.

How to Decide

The useful comparison is not as-is versus fully remodeled. It is as-is versus the specific fixes that buyers in your price range notice most and that cost you less than they return. A CMA that prices your home in its current condition and again with a short list of repairs gives you both numbers side by side. Then you can see whether the repair money pays you back or just delays the listing. For a larger project, compare it to whether a kitchen remodel is worth it before listing.

This is general information, not legal or tax advice. Disclosure duties depend on your specific property and facts. Confirm requirements with a licensed attorney or your listing agent.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does selling as-is mean I do not have to disclose problems in California?

No. California sellers still complete the Transfer Disclosure Statement and must disclose material facts. Houzeo warns that hiding a defect can let the buyer withdraw and may lead to a lawsuit.

How much less does an as-is home sell for?

HomeLight (March 2026) quotes an expert saying as-is homes in the area typically sell for 20% to 30% less than regular listings. That is a general range, not a Woodland Hills specific figure.

Can a buyer still inspect an as-is home?

Yes. Houzeo says a buyer can back out of an as-is contract during the inspection period without penalty or losing the deposit, and can negotiate repairs or credits afterward.

Do small repairs like paint and flooring pay off?

HomeLight says light improvements like paint, carpet, and landscaping could add about 5% in value and cites a possible return of $1.25 or more per $1 spent. Results vary by house.

What is the median price in Woodland Hills?

Movoto reports a median sale price of $1,348,500 for August 2026, with 48 days on market as of its September 30, 2026 update.

How do I know whether to fix or sell as-is?
