Should I Market My Woodland Hills Home For A Cash Buyer?

Jonathan Lopez, Woodland Hills Realtor | Jim Sandoval Group | CA DRE #02282844 · Updated September 2026

If your Woodland Hills home is priced anywhere near or above the area's current median of $1,348,500, you've probably heard some version of this advice: "at this price point, expect an all-cash buyer." It's repeated often enough in real estate circles that it's worth checking against the actual data, because the real numbers tell a more useful, more nuanced story than the blanket assumption.

Cash Purchases Really Are More Common At Higher Price Points, Nationally

According to a Realtor.com report released in August 2026 covering the first four months of the year, more than 40% of homes sold for $1 million or more were purchased without financing, and a majority of homes sold for $2 million or more were bought entirely in cash. Realtor.com's own explanation for this pattern is straightforward: affluent households simply have the resources to purchase outright, and at the very high end, real estate increasingly functions as a hard asset that wealthy buyers want independent of mortgage rates and market swings.

The Luxury Segment Is Leaning Further Into Cash, Not Away From It

The Coldwell Banker Global Luxury program's 2026 Mid-Year Report, released July 14, 2026 and based on a survey of more than 200 Coldwell Banker Luxury Property Specialists across 120 U.S. markets, found that 63% of those specialists reported an increase in all-cash transactions among their clients this year, up from 51% who said the same a year earlier. One Coldwell Banker luxury agent quoted in the report described high-net-worth buyers as treating real estate as a "cornerstone strategy" for holding hard assets, not just a lifestyle purchase, a mindset that shows up as cash offers with no financing contingency. This is a different lever than whether a 3D virtual tour helps sell a home above the median or whether staging and photography raise price above $1 million: those are about how a home is shown, this is about who's actually buying it and how they're paying.

But Nationally, Cash Buying Overall Is Actually Cooling

That same Realtor.com report found cash purchases accounted for 31.4% of all U.S. home sales in early 2026, down from 32.3% a year earlier, as improving inventory and moderating prices let more financed buyers back into the market. Cash sales fell faster than the market as a whole: total home sales dropped 8.5% year over year, while cash sales fell 11.2%. The pattern that remains is U-shaped: cash stays common at the very bottom of the market (homes under $100,000) and the very top (homes over $1 million), while the broad middle is increasingly financed.

Locally, The Los Angeles Metro Sits Well Below The National Cash-Buying Average

The same Realtor.com data puts the Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim metro's cash share at 23.8% of sales, down 2.4 percentage points year over year, and below the 31.4% national figure. That's a meaningful data point for Woodland Hills sellers: most Southern California transactions, even in an expensive metro, are still happening under $1 million and getting financed. Woodland Hills' own median sale price of $1,348,500 (Movoto, updated September 9, 2026) sits just above that $1 million line where the national cash-buyer share starts climbing toward 40%-plus, but it's nowhere near the $2 million-plus range where cash becomes the majority outcome.

What This Actually Means For How You Market And Price

A genuine cash offer is a real advantage for a seller: no appraisal contingency, no lender underwriting timeline, and a real chance at a faster, cleaner close. If your home's condition, price point, or unique features make it a strong fit for a buyer who wants to move fast and skip financing entirely (a rare original property, a fully updated turnkey home, or a listing priced to move quickly), it's worth highlighting those features specifically. But the data doesn't support assuming a cash buyer will materialize just because your price crossed $1 million. Most buyers at Woodland Hills' current price levels are still qualifying for a mortgage, which means your marketing, staging, and pricing strategy still need to work for a financed buyer's timeline, appraisal requirements, and lending contingencies, not just the smaller slice of the buyer pool paying outright.

The bottom line: cash buyers are a real and growing part of the luxury segment nationally, but they're still the exception, not the rule, even for homes priced like Woodland Hills' current median. A smart marketing strategy accounts for both kinds of buyers rather than betting the listing on one.

Frequently Asked Questions

What percentage of $1 million-plus home sales are actually all-cash?

More than 40% nationally, and a majority of sales at $2 million or more, according to Realtor.com's report on the first four months of 2026. That's higher than the 31.4% national average across all price points, but it's still not the majority outcome until you get well above $2 million.

Is the Los Angeles area more or less cash-heavy than the rest of the country?

Less. The Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim metro's cash share was 23.8% of sales, below the 31.4% national average, and down 2.4 percentage points year over year, according to the same Realtor.com data.

Are luxury buyers using cash more or less than they used to?

More. Coldwell Banker's Global Luxury 2026 Mid-Year Report found 63% of surveyed luxury property specialists saw an increase in all-cash purchases this year, up from 51% a year earlier, a real year-over-year jump in a segment that was already cash-heavy.

Should I price my Woodland Hills home assuming a cash buyer will make an offer?

No. At Woodland Hills' current median sale price ($1,348,500), you're in the range where cash offers are more common than at lower price points, but financed buyers are still the majority. Pricing or marketing strategy built entirely around attracting a cash buyer risks alienating the larger, financed buyer pool.

What's actually different about a cash offer for a seller?

No appraisal contingency and no lender underwriting timeline, which typically means a faster, more predictable close with fewer financing-related risks. It doesn't automatically mean a higher price; cash buyers often expect a discount in exchange for speed and certainty.

How should I market my home if I don't know whether my eventual buyer will pay cash or finance?

Build a marketing plan that works for both: strong photography, accurate listing details, and pricing grounded in real comparable sales, plus messaging that highlights turnkey condition or fast-close potential if those genuinely apply to your property.
