Can I Sell My Woodland Hills Home While My Divorce Is Still in Progress?

Jonathan Lopez, Woodland Hills Realtor · Jim Sandoval Group · CA DRE #02282844 · Updated November 2026

If you're going through a divorce in Woodland Hills and also need to sell the family home, it helps to understand what actually has to happen before a sale can close, because the process is genuinely different from a normal listing.

The moment either spouse files a divorce petition in California, an Automatic Temporary Restraining Order, often called an ATRO, takes effect under Family Code Section 2040. It applies to the filing spouse immediately and to the other spouse once they're served. Among other things, it blocks either spouse from selling, transferring, or borrowing against community or quasi-community property, real or personal, without the other spouse's written consent or a court order. The family home almost always falls squarely under this rule if it was purchased during the marriage. Trying to list or sell without addressing the ATRO first is one of the more common mistakes in a divorce-related sale.

California is a community property state, which means a home bought with marital funds during the marriage is generally treated as owned equally by both spouses, regardless of whose name is on the title or the mortgage. Family Code Section 2550 requires the court to divide the community estate equally in value, though that doesn't necessarily mean splitting every single asset down the middle. A judge can award the home to one spouse in exchange for an equal value of other assets, such as retirement funds, instead. Section 721 adds a fiduciary duty between spouses on property transactions, so a spouse who tries to sell or transfer the home to intentionally shortchange the other before or during a divorce can face sanctions and be ordered to reimburse the other spouse.

With all of that in place, there are really three paths for the home once a divorce is filed. The first is a sale by mutual agreement: both spouses sign off in writing, the ATRO restriction is satisfied, and the sale proceeds like any other listing, with net proceeds split according to the divorce settlement or court order. The second is a court-ordered sale, which a judge can impose if the spouses can't agree and a sale is the only practical way to divide the estate equally, especially when neither spouse can afford to buy out the other's share. The third is a deferred sale order, sometimes called a Duke order under Family Code Section 3802, where the court lets a custodial parent and the children stay in the home, usually until the youngest child turns 18, graduates, or another set event occurs, while the non-custodial spouse's equity interest stays attached to the home and gets paid out whenever it eventually sells.

None of these paths skip the ATRO. Even with a full agreement in hand, both spouses typically still need to sign off clearly in writing, or get court authorization, before escrow can close. A title or escrow company handling a divorce-related sale will usually ask for documentation showing the restraining order has been satisfied, not just that both parties verbally agree.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I sell my Woodland Hills home before my divorce is final?

Yes, as long as the Automatic Temporary Restraining Order (Family Code Section 2040) is satisfied, meaning both spouses consent in writing or a judge issues an order authorizing the sale.

Does it matter whose name is on the title or mortgage?

Not usually. California treats a home bought with marital funds during the marriage as community property under Family Code Section 2550, generally owned equally by both spouses regardless of title.

What if my spouse won't agree to sell?

A judge can order a sale over one spouse's objection when it's the only practical way to divide the community estate equally and neither spouse can afford to buy out the other's share.

What is a deferred sale order?

Also called a Duke order under Family Code Section 3802, it lets a custodial parent and children stay in the home, often until the youngest child turns 18 or another set event occurs, while the other spouse's equity interest stays attached and gets paid out at the eventual sale.

Can one spouse just sell the house without telling the other during a divorce?

No. Family Code Section 721's fiduciary duty, combined with the ATRO, can expose a spouse who tries that to sanctions and a reimbursement order.

Do I need a real estate agent and a family law attorney, or just one?

Both, working together. The attorney handles the ATRO, settlement terms, and court requirements. The agent handles pricing, marketing, and closing the actual sale once the legal path is clear.
