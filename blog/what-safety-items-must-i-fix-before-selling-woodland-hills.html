What Safety Items Must I Fix Before Selling My Woodland Hills Home?

Jonathan Lopez, Realtor serving Woodland Hills with the Jim Sandoval Group · CA DRE #02282844 · Updated October 2026

A lot of Woodland Hills sellers ask me some version of this before we list: is there anything the state actually requires me to fix? Most repairs are negotiable. Three safety items are not, because California law ties them directly to the sale of a single-family home.

1. A working smoke alarm

California Health and Safety Code 13113.8 says every single-family dwelling that is sold must have an operable smoke alarm, approved and listed by the State Fire Marshal. The seller must also deliver a written statement to the buyer confirming compliance, either in the purchase agreement, an addendum, or a separate document, as soon as practicable before title transfers. The law treats one and two unit dwellings as single-family for this purpose, and it lets local ordinances add to the minimum.

2. Carbon monoxide devices

Health and Safety Code 17926 requires owners of existing single-family homes to install a State Fire Marshal approved carbon monoxide device if the home has a fossil fuel burning heater or appliance, a fireplace, or an attached garage. That describes a large share of Woodland Hills houses, many of which have gas furnaces, gas water heaters, and attached garages. Placement follows the building standards for new construction or the manufacturer's instructions where feasible.

3. A strapped water heater

Health and Safety Code 19211 requires every residential water heater, new or existing, to be braced, anchored, or strapped to resist falling or shifting in an earthquake, at minimum per the California Plumbing Code. The seller must certify this to the buyer in writing. That certification can go in documents you are already signing, including the Transfer Disclosure Statement or the Homeowner's Guide to Earthquake Safety. In a seismically active area like the West Valley, this is one of the first things a buyer's inspector looks at in the garage.

Who is exempt, and what if it gets missed?

The smoke alarm statement does not apply to several kinds of transfers listed in Section 13113.8, including court ordered and probate sales, transfers by a fiduciary administering an estate, trust, guardianship, or conservatorship, foreclosures, and transfers between co-owners or to a spouse or direct descendant. That matters for families selling an inherited Woodland Hills home through a trust. Missing a certification does not invalidate a sale; for smoke alarms and carbon monoxide devices, the statutes cap the buyer's remedy at actual damages of no more than $100. The practical risk is different: an inspector will flag these items, and a small fix becomes a repair request in the middle of escrow.

Why it matters in today's market

Movoto's Woodland Hills page, checked October 28, 2026, shows 379 homes for sale and an average of 50 days on market. With buyers taking their time and comparing options, a clean inspection report helps your home stand out. These three items are usually inexpensive and quick to handle before photos, not after an offer.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a working smoke alarm to sell my Woodland Hills home?

Yes. California Health and Safety Code 13113.8 says every single-family dwelling sold must have an operable smoke alarm, and the seller must give the buyer a written statement of compliance.

Is a carbon monoxide detector required?

Under Health and Safety Code 17926, owners of single-family homes with a fossil fuel burning heater or appliance, a fireplace, or an attached garage must have a State Fire Marshal approved carbon monoxide device installed.

Does my water heater have to be strapped?

Yes. Health and Safety Code 19211 requires all residential water heaters to be braced, anchored, or strapped against earthquake motion, and the seller must certify this to the buyer in writing.

Where do I certify these items?

The statutes allow the certifications in existing transaction documents, such as the purchase agreement, an addendum, or for the water heater, the Transfer Disclosure Statement or the Homeowner's Guide to Earthquake Safety.

Are any sellers exempt from the smoke alarm statement?

Yes. Section 13113.8 exempts several transfers, including court ordered and probate transfers, transfers by a fiduciary administering an estate or trust, foreclosures, and transfers between co-owners or to a spouse or direct descendant.

What happens if a seller skips this?

The sale is not invalidated. For smoke alarms and carbon monoxide devices, the statutes cap the buyer's remedy at actual damages up to $100, but a buyer's inspector will usually flag missing items, so fixing them first is simpler.
