My Employer's Relocation Package Includes a Guaranteed Buyout. Should I Take It, or Sell My Woodland Hills Home Myself?

Jonathan Lopez · Woodland Hills Realtor, Jim Sandoval Group · CA DRE #02282844 · Updated November 2026

The Buyout Offer That Shows Up With the Moving Boxes

If your employer is relocating you out of Woodland Hills, the relocation package you get handed probably isn't just a lump-sum check. A growing share of corporate relocation plans include a direct home-sale benefit, and the one that surprises people most is the Guaranteed Buyout Offer, usually called a GBO. Under a GBO, the relocation management company contracted by your employer actually purchases your home from you, in cash, at a set price, so you can move on your employer's timeline without waiting for a retail buyer to show up.

That certainty is real, and for someone on a hard relocation deadline, it can be worth a lot. But "guaranteed" refers to the company's commitment to buy, not to the price being generous.

How the GBO Price Actually Gets Set

A GBO price is typically calculated as the average of two independent fair-market-value appraisals, ordered by the relocation company, that value the home as-is and assume a conservative marketing period, often capped around 120 days from the appraisal date. That structure is deliberately risk-averse from the relocation company's side: they're about to own your house and resell it, so the number tends to land closer to a cautious floor than to what an actual competitive listing might bring in a market with real buyer demand.

The Option Most Employees Don't Know to Ask About

Here's the detail that gets missed most often: many relocation programs pair the GBO with what's called an Amended Value Option, sometimes shortened to AVO. Under an AVO, you accept the GBO as a floor, but you (or your agent) keep marketing the home for a limited window. If a genuine, good-faith outside buyer offers more than the GBO price before the relocation company's closing, the deal amends upward to that higher number instead. You still get the certainty of the guaranteed floor, with upside if the open market does better.

A related but distinct structure, the Buyer Value Option (BVO), skips the direct buyout altogether: the relocation company facilitates your sale to an outside buyer and handles much of the paperwork and tax-protection benefits, but never takes title itself. According to figures attributed to relocation management company Graebel's 2026 domestic home-sale-program benchmark, 88% of corporate programs now offer a BVO structure, compared with 44% offering a GBO and 84% offering straight cash reimbursement, meaning most employers already lean toward letting the employee's home go through an actual sale rather than a direct corporate buyout.

Why Local Market Conditions Matter Here More Than People Assume

The whole calculation changes depending on how your specific home would actually perform if listed normally, not how a conservative national appraisal model scores it. Right now in the 91367 ZIP code, the median sale price sits around $1,199,000, with homes spending an average of about 41 days on market (Movoto). That's a market pace where a correctly priced, well-marketed listing has a realistic shot at closing inside most relocation deadlines, and potentially at a number above a cautious buyout appraisal, particularly for a home with any competitive advantage (location, condition, upgrades) that a standardized appraisal model doesn't fully capture.

What To Actually Do Before You Sign Anything

Ask your HR or relocation counselor directly whether your plan includes an Amended Value Option or a BVO path, not just the baseline GBO. Get an independent comparative market analysis from an agent who actually works this specific area, and compare that honest number, net of real selling costs, against the guaranteed buyout number before you decide. If the gap is small, the certainty of a GBO may genuinely be worth it on a tight timeline. If the gap is significant, a short, aggressively marketed listing under an AVO or BVO window can put real money back in your pocket without blowing your move-out date.

Bottom Line

A corporate relocation buyout is a real benefit, not a trap, but it's built around certainty for the relocation company, not around maximizing what you walk away with. Knowing which option your specific plan actually offers, and getting an honest local comparison before you sign, is the difference between leaving money on the table and using the benefit the way it's meant to work.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a Guaranteed Buyout Offer (GBO) in a corporate relocation package?

It's a benefit where the relocation company your employer hires actually purchases your home directly, in cash, at a set price, so you don't have to wait for an outside buyer before relocating on your employer's timeline.

How is the GBO price determined?

Typically as the average of two independent fair-market-value appraisals ordered by the relocation company, valuing the home as-is and assuming a conservative marketing period, often around 120 days.

What's the difference between a GBO, a BVO, and an Amended Value Option?

A GBO means the relocation company buys your home outright. A BVO (Buyer Value Option) means the relocation company facilitates a sale to an outside buyer without ever taking title itself. An Amended Value Option lets you keep marketing the home after accepting a GBO; if an outside buyer offers more before closing, the price amends upward to that offer.

Can I still sell on the open market if my plan includes a GBO?

Often yes, if your plan includes an Amended Value Option or a parallel marketing window. Ask your HR relocation counselor specifically whether that option exists on your plan before assuming the buyout number is final.

Is the GBO price usually lower than what I'd get selling the home myself?

It's designed to be a conservative, risk-averse number for the relocation company, not a competitive market price, so in a steady or improving local market it often undershoots what a well-marketed listing could achieve, though every situation and property is different.

What should I do before accepting a buyout offer on my Woodland Hills home?

Ask about an Amended Value Option or BVO path, get an independent comparative market analysis from a local agent, and compare the real net numbers, not just the headline offer, against your actual relocation deadline.
