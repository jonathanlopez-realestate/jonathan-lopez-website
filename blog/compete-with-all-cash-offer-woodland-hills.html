Can I Compete With an All-Cash Offer When I Buy in Woodland Hills?

Jonathan Lopez, Jim Sandoval Group | Woodland Hills Realtor | CA DRE #02282844 · Updated September 2026

Losing a Woodland Hills house to an all-cash offer is a common worry for financed buyers. The data suggests the fear is bigger than the reality in Los Angeles right now, though cash still matters on individual houses.

How Common Is Cash Here?

Redfin's analysis of March 2026 purchases found that 28.8% of U.S. homebuyers paid cash, the lowest March share since 2020. In the Los Angeles metro, the all-cash share was 20.5%, down 3.9 percentage points from a year earlier. Redfin attributed the national decline to mortgage rates easing to about 6.18% from 7%+ highs, more sellers than buyers in many markets, and economic uncertainty that makes affluent buyers reluctant to draw down cash reserves. Redfin did not break out Woodland Hills or any luxury segment, so the metro figure is the smallest real geography available.

What the Local Market Looks Like

As of September 24, 2026, Movoto shows 408 active listings in Woodland Hills, 77 of them already price-reduced. The median sale price for August 2026 was $1,348,500, the average time on market was 48 days (up from 45 a year earlier), and 367 homes sold in August, up from 331 a year earlier. More inventory and longer marketing times generally give financed buyers more room to negotiate than they had in a bidding-war market. See also what happens if the appraisal comes in low, since an appraisal gap plan is part of any competitive financed offer.

Why Cash Still Wins Some Houses

A seller reading two offers is mostly comparing certainty: will this close, on time, at this price? An all-cash offer removes the loan approval, the lender's appraisal, and the financing contingency. A financed buyer's job is to close that certainty gap as far as honestly possible.

Ways a Financed Buyer Narrows the Gap

A full lender pre-approval, with documents already reviewed by an underwriter, reads very differently from a pre-qualification letter. A plan for what happens if the appraisal comes in low, decided in advance, keeps the offer from falling apart on the day it happens. Contingency periods and closing timelines that you can truly support, and not ones that look good on paper but put your deposit at risk, matter as well. Which of these you can tighten is personal to your finances and the specific home, and waiving protections without understanding what you are giving up is not something I would recommend. This is a similar theme to whether to waive the inspection to compete.

Bottom Line

Cash is a factor, but about 4 in 5 LA-metro purchases in March 2026 were not all cash, and Woodland Hills currently has room for well-prepared financed buyers. The best next step is a review of your offer strategy against real comparable sales for the home you want.

Redfin reported 20.5% of LA metro purchases were all cash in March 2026, down 3.9 points year over year. The national figure was 28.8%.

Is Woodland Hills a hot market right now?

Movoto's data (updated September 24, 2026) shows 408 active listings, 77 price-reduced, 48 average days on market versus 45 a year ago, and a $1,348,500 median sale price for August. That reads as an active market with choices for buyers, not a frenzy.

What makes a financed offer more competitive?

A full lender pre-approval, a plan for any appraisal gap, and contingency terms and timelines you can honestly support. What is safe to tighten depends on your finances and the house.

Should I waive contingencies to beat a cash offer?

That is a personal risk decision. Waiving protections can put your deposit at risk, so it should be discussed with your agent, lender, and where appropriate an attorney before you write the offer.
