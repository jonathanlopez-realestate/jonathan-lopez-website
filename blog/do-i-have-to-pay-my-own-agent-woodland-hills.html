Do I Have to Pay My Own Real Estate Agent Now to Buy in Woodland Hills? What Changed, and What It Actually Costs

Jonathan Lopez, Woodland Hills Realtor | Jim Sandoval Group | CA DRE #02282844 · Updated August 2026

Since the 2024 National Association of Realtors settlement, a lot of Woodland Hills buyers have picked up a half-true rumor: that hiring a buyer's agent now automatically means paying them thousands of dollars out of pocket. It's not automatic, but the process did genuinely change, and buyers deserve a straight answer before they start touring homes.

What Actually Changed

As of August 2024, the NAR settlement requires buyer's agents to have a signed agreement in place before providing services, and it ended the old practice of automatically splitting a seller-paid commission between listing and buyer's agents. California went further with its own law: AB 2992, signed September 24, 2024 and effective January 1, 2025, requires a written buyer-broker representation agreement to be signed as soon as practicable, and no later than the buyer's offer to purchase. In practice, that means signing it before an agent shows homes, not after.

What That Agreement Actually Does

It's not a bill. It's a disclosure. The buyer-broker agreement spells out what the agent will be paid and who is expected to pay it, whether that's the seller through a negotiated concession or the buyer directly. Before this law, that conversation often didn't happen explicitly until much later in the process, if at all.

What Buyers Are Actually Paying in Practice

Statewide, RealEstateWitch's July 2026 data shows the average buyer's agent concession at 2.7% of the sale price. Los Angeles runs slightly higher: Clever Real Estate's February 2026 survey of 27 local agents found an average buyer's agent commission of 2.8%. On Woodland Hills' current median sale price of $1,372,000 (Movoto, updated July 15, 2026), that's roughly $37,044 to $38,416, and in the large majority of deals, that amount is still paid by the seller at closing out of their sale proceeds, not by the buyer upfront.

The Scenario Buyers Should Actually Plan For

If a seller declines to offer that concession, most commonly in a competitive seller's market where they don't need to sweeten the deal, the buyer becomes responsible for that 2.7% to 2.8% directly, on top of the down payment and closing costs they're already budgeting for. That's the real risk the new buyer-broker agreement is designed to surface in writing, before an offer goes in, not after.

At a median price above $1.37M, a swing of 2.7% to 2.8% isn't a rounding error. Reading and understanding the buyer-broker agreement before touring starts is the only way to know which situation applies before making an offer. See how Jonathan walks buyers through this before touring even begins.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I have to pay for my own real estate agent when buying in Woodland Hills?

Usually not directly. Most sellers still offer a concession that covers the buyer's agent commission, averaging 2.7% statewide and 2.8% in Los Angeles as of early-to-mid 2026. But it's no longer automatic, and the buyer-broker agreement you sign will spell out exactly who's paying before you make an offer.

What is a buyer-broker agreement, and why do I have to sign one now?

It's a written contract required under California's AB 2992 (effective January 1, 2025) that must be signed as soon as practicable, and no later than your offer to purchase. It states what your agent is paid and who pays it. Most agents have buyers sign it before showing homes.

What happens if the seller doesn't offer to cover my agent's commission?

You become responsible for it directly, typically 2.7% to 2.8% of the purchase price on Woodland Hills' current price range, on top of your down payment and closing costs. This is uncommon but does happen, most often in competitive seller's markets.

Does this mean buyer's agents cost more now than before the settlement?

Not necessarily more, just more explicit. Before 2024, the buyer's agent commission was typically baked into the seller's listing agreement and split automatically. Now it has to be separately negotiated and disclosed in writing.
