Can I Value My Woodland Hills Home by Price Per Square Foot?

Jonathan Lopez, Jim Sandoval Group | Woodland Hills Realtor | CA DRE #02282844 · Updated October 2026

If you want a quick number for your Woodland Hills home, price per square foot looks like an easy answer. It is a useful reference point, but a poor way to set a price on its own.

What the Local Number Says

Movoto's Woodland Hills market page (updated October 5, 2026) shows a price per square foot of $597, a median sale price of $1,299,000, 428 active listings, 76 price-reduced, and 50 days on market compared with 44 a year earlier. It also reports 389 homes sold in September 2026, up from 327 a year earlier. The $597 figure is an average across many different homes, so it describes the neighborhood, not your house.

Why the Shortcut Misleads

Amerisave's price per square foot guide lists several reasons the number can mislead. Lot size and land value inflate the price without changing the square footage. A fully renovated 1,800 square foot home can legitimately sell for more per square foot than a dated 2,200 square foot home, because condition and updates can matter more than raw size. Smaller homes tend to cost more per square foot because fixed costs like HVAC, a kitchen, and bathrooms spread across less space. Floor plan efficiency, a garage, and location amenities also move the figure. Amerisave says the number should be used only for similar homes close by and sold recently, and treated as one check rather than the main method.

What Appraisers Do Differently

JVM Lending (published August 29, 2023) explains that appraisers compare gross living area only, and handle the lot, pool, garage, and other improvements separately to avoid double counting. Its example: a 200 square foot size difference might call for an adjustment of $20,000 or less, where simple math at $400 per square foot would suggest $80,000. It also notes that larger and larger homes often earn smaller and smaller size adjustments in neighborhoods of similar lots.

As an illustration only, 200 extra square feet at Woodland Hills' $597 average would look like about $119,400 on paper. That is arithmetic, not an appraisal, and real adjustments depend on the comparable sales used. This is a general example, not a rule for any specific house. For a related look at how automated estimates handle this, see whether your Zestimate is accurate.

What to Use Instead

A comparative market analysis starts with recent nearby sales that match your home on age, size, lot, and condition, then adjusts for the differences one at a time. Features like a pool, an ADU, or an unpermitted addition each get their own treatment, which is why price per square foot can hide big swings. See does a pool actually add value and does an unpermitted addition count toward value for how those features are treated.

This is general information, not an appraisal or financial advice. For a formal value, such as for a loan, a licensed appraiser is required.
