Does a New Roof Actually Raise My Woodland Hills Home's Value?

Jonathan Lopez · Woodland Hills Realtor, Jim Sandoval Group · CA DRE #02282844 · Updated October 2026

Woodland Hills sellers who notice their roof is aging often ask the same question before they list: will replacing it actually raise what the house sells for, or is it just a cost of doing business. The honest answer is that the data most people reach for to answer that question measures something a little different than a resale dollar figure.

The National Association of Realtors publishes a Remodeling Impact Report, produced jointly with the National Association of the Remodeling Industry, based on a survey NAR sent to a random sample of 100,000 of its members in the summer of 2024, with 4,005 responses. For each project, Realtors were asked to estimate the likely dollar value it would add at resale and to rate a "Joy Score," how much satisfaction homeowners reported after completing it. New roofing scored a perfect Joy Score of 10 out of 10 in the 2025 report, tied with only two other projects out of the full list: an added primary bedroom suite and a kitchen upgrade. That is a real, survey-based data point about homeowner and agent sentiment, not a marketing claim.

The same report also asked Realtors what they recommend sellers do before listing, and what they have seen rising buyer demand for. New roofing placed among the top recommended pre-listing projects, with 37% of Realtors naming it, behind only painting the entire home and painting a single room. On demand, 43% of Realtors reported seeing increased buyer interest in new roofing over the past two years, the second-highest increase in the survey behind kitchen upgrades.

Here is where the data runs out, and it is worth being direct about it rather than filling the gap with a guess. NAR's report separately calculates a "Recovered Project Cost" percentage for some projects, comparing the dollar value Realtors estimated a project adds against NARI's median cost figure for that project. The report publishes this as a "Top Cost Recovery" ranking, but it only lists ten projects, starting at a new steel front door (100% recovered) and ending at a bathroom renovation (50% recovered). New roofing does not appear on that published list. That does not necessarily mean roofing recovers nothing, it means NAR's own report does not disclose an exact percentage for it, so there is no honest way to hand you a specific resale-return number for a roof the way there is for, say, a new front door.

On the cost side, Angi's roofing cost guide, updated October 22, 2026, puts the national average cost of a professional roof replacement at $9,610, with most homeowners spending between $5,903 and $13,378 depending on roof size, pitch, material, and local labor rates. That is a national figure pulled from Angi's own project data, not a Woodland Hills-specific estimate, actual cost here will depend on your roof's size and the material you choose.

None of this means a new roof is a bad idea before selling. A roof in visibly poor condition can slow a sale, complicate a buyer's inspection or financing, or become a negotiating point that costs you more in a late-stage credit than the replacement would have cost upfront. But if you are deciding purely on a dollar-for-dollar resale math basis, the honest position is that the most commonly cited industry survey does not give you a clean recovery percentage to plug in, only strong sentiment data (the Joy Score and demand figures) and a national cost range.

Locally, Movoto's Woodland Hills page, updated October 28, 2026, shows a median sale price of $1,299,000 for September, 379 active listings, and an average 50 days on market, up from 44 a year ago. If you are weighing whether a roof replacement makes sense before you list, I can look at your specific home, its current condition, and recent comparable sales rather than relying on a national survey average.

The Joy Score, 37%, and 43% figures in this post come from NAR's 2025 Remodeling Impact Report and reflect national survey responses from Realtor members, not Woodland Hills or 91367-specific data. NAR's report does not publish a cost-recovery percentage for roofing. Angi's cost figures are national averages, not local estimates. This is general market information, not an appraisal or a guarantee of resale value for any specific home.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a new roof raise my home's resale value in Woodland Hills?

There is no published, Woodland Hills-specific dollar or percentage figure for this. NAR's 2025 Remodeling Impact Report measures Realtor sentiment (a perfect Joy Score of 10) and demand trends for new roofing, but its published cost-recovery ranking does not include roofing.

What is a "Joy Score"?

A rating NAR calculates from its Realtor survey measuring homeowner satisfaction after completing a remodeling project, on a scale of 1 to 10. New roofing scored a perfect 10 in the 2025 report.

Do Realtors recommend a new roof before selling?

Yes, among the more commonly recommended projects. 37% of Realtors surveyed named new roofing as a top project to recommend before listing, and 43% reported rising buyer demand for it over the past two years.

How much does a new roof cost?

Angi's data, updated October 22, 2026, puts the national average at $9,610, typically $5,903 to $13,378, depending on size, pitch, and material. This is a national figure, not specific to Woodland Hills.

Why doesn't NAR give an exact percentage for roofing like it does for other projects?

NAR's "Top Cost Recovery" list in the 2025 report only publishes figures for ten projects, from a new steel front door down to a bathroom renovation. Roofing is not among the ten projects NAR chose to publish a cost-recovery percentage for in that report.

Should I replace my roof before listing even without a clear resale number?

That depends on your roof's actual condition and how it's likely to show up on a buyer's inspection, not just the resale math. I can look at your specific situation and recent local comps to help you decide.
