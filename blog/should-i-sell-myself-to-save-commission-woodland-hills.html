Should I Sell My Woodland Hills Home Myself to Save on Commission?

Jonathan Lopez, Woodland Hills Realtor | Jim Sandoval Group | CA DRE #02282844 · Updated September 2026

Every seller thinks about it at some point: skip the agent, list it on Zillow, save the commission. It's a reasonable question to ask. What's less commonly seen is what actually happens to the sellers who go through with it, and the national data on that is more one-sided than most people expect.

FSBO Is at an All-Time Low, for a Reason

According to NAR's 2025 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers, For Sale By Owner transactions made up just 5% of home sales nationally over the past year, the lowest share on record, edging out the previous low of 7% the year before. A record 91% of sellers sold with an agent's help. FSBO's share has been shrinking for four decades: it sat at 21% of sales back in 1985 and has stayed in the single digits since 2010. That's not a fluke of one bad year, it's a long, consistent trend.

The Price Gap Is Real, But It Needs Context

NAR's data puts the median FSBO sale price at $360,000 nationally versus $425,000 for agent-assisted sales, an 18% gap. It would be misleading to present that as a clean before-and-after comparison, though. NAR itself notes that FSBO homes skew toward lower-cost properties, including more mobile homes and rural sales, which pulls the median down independent of who sold it. The honest takeaway isn't "you'll lose exactly 18% by going FSBO," it's that the sellers who do go it alone are, on average, working with a tougher set of circumstances and less pricing leverage than the typical agent-assisted seller.

What FSBO Sellers Actually Struggle With

A separate survey by Clever Real Estate digs into the FSBO experience directly, and the numbers are blunt. Sixty-four percent of FSBO sellers say they did not achieve their desired sale price. Forty-three percent admitted to making a legal mistake somewhere in the process. Nearly 30% struggled with pricing their home, and many of those relied on an online home-value estimator instead of a real comparative market analysis, the same pattern covered in more detail in this site's earlier look at Zestimate accuracy. More than half describe the process as stressful, and 47% say it brought them to tears. About 1 in 5 FSBO sellers eventually give up and hire an agent partway through anyway.

The Commission "Savings" Often Isn't What It Looks Like

Here's the detail that surprises a lot of would-be FSBO sellers: most of them still end up paying a buyer's agent commission, typically as a seller concession, since the vast majority of buyers are represented and that concession is often what keeps a listing competitive. The commission a seller actually avoids by going FSBO is usually just the listing side, not the whole transaction. Meanwhile, NAR's own survey of sellers who did use an agent found 86% said their agent provided a broad range of services and managed most aspects of the sale, and 87% said they'd recommend their agent again.

What This Looks Like on a Real Woodland Hills Number

Woodland Hills' current median sale price is $1,348,500 (Movoto, updated September 14, 2026). At a typical California total commission rate of 5.47% (Listwithclever's 2026 survey, the same figure used in the original net-proceeds breakdown on this site), that works out to roughly $73,762 total, split between the listing and buyer's agents. That's the number to actually weigh, not against zero, since most FSBO sellers still pay a buyer's agent concession anyway, but against the real, documented risks: a 64% chance of missing your target price, a meaningful chance of a legal misstep, and the very real possibility of needing to hire an agent mid-process regardless.

The 40% Who Never Even Tried

One more data point worth sitting with: NAR's research found 40% of FSBO sellers didn't actively market their homes at all. In a market like Woodland Hills, where buyers are searching specific portals, price ranges, and neighborhoods (a dynamic covered in more depth in this site's post on portal search behavior), a listing that never gets real marketing exposure is competing at a real disadvantage before a single showing happens.

Where This Leaves a Woodland Hills Seller

None of this means FSBO never makes sense, a sale to a known buyer, a friend, or a relative, is a different situation than an open-market listing, and those pre-arranged sales make up a meaningful share of the FSBO total. But for a typical open-market sale, the national data is consistent and has been for decades: sellers who go it alone are more likely to struggle with pricing, more likely to make a legal misstep, and more likely to end up hiring an agent anyway after losing time on the market. A real conversation about what a licensed CMA, professional marketing, and negotiation support actually get you, priced against Woodland Hills' real commission numbers, is worth having before deciding either way.

Frequently Asked Questions

What percentage of home sales are FSBO now?

Just 5% nationally over the past year, the lowest share on record, according to NAR's 2025 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers. A record 91% of sellers used an agent.

Do FSBO homes really sell for less?

NAR's data shows a median FSBO sale price of $360,000 nationally versus $425,000 for agent-assisted sales. Part of that gap reflects FSBO homes skewing toward lower-cost and rural properties, so it isn't a clean apples-to-apples comparison, but the direction of the gap has held for years.

Do FSBO sellers actually avoid paying any commission?

Usually not entirely. Most FSBO sellers still end up paying a buyer's agent commission as a seller concession, since the large majority of buyers are represented. The commission actually avoided is typically just the listing side.

What do FSBO sellers say went wrong?

In a Clever Real Estate survey, 64% didn't hit their desired sale price, 43% admitted a legal mistake, and nearly 30% struggled with pricing, often relying on an online estimator instead of a real comparative market analysis.

How many FSBO sellers end up hiring an agent anyway?

Roughly 1 in 5, according to Clever Real Estate's survey, typically after struggling to sell on their own for a period of time.

Does this mean FSBO never makes sense?

Not always. Sales to a known buyer, a friend, or a relative are a different situation than an open-market listing, and those pre-arranged sales make up a real share of the FSBO total. The data above applies most directly to a typical open-market sale.
